> **[CAM 04: OBSERVATION GALLERY | 20:41:17]**

The picture fades in mid-sentence.

Scientist 2: "—will the door hold?"

No one answers.

The gallery is a narrow steel box, three strides from the door to the window. The window runs the length of the far wall, thick as a forearm and tinted faintly green. It looks down on the containment chamber from high above: a bare concrete room about the size of a classroom, with a drain set into the tiled floor.

At its center, a steel table lies on its side, one end torn from the bolts that held it to the tile. A restraint strap dangles from its raised edge, the buckle swaying like a pendulum. The second strap is still fastened. The cuff is empty.

Scientist 1 stands with one palm pressed flat against the glass. Scientist 2 hunches over a bank of monitors. One screen shows only static.

Scientist 1: "Where is the subject?"

Scientist 2: "Behind the table. I can't see past it."

Scientist 1: "Change the angle."

Scientist 2: "The chamber camera isn't responding."

An impact thuds through the microphone, deep enough to rattle the speaker. The window shivers in its frame.

Scientist 1 pulls their hand away.

Scientist 1: "Sedation?"

Scientist 2: "Administered."

Scientist 1: "Administer it again."

Scientist 2: "We already did."

The picture shudders and splits into pale, jittering squares, each holding a fragment of light.

> **[CAM 09: WING B CORRIDOR | TIMESTAMP UNAVAILABLE]**

The corridor holds on the screen for several long seconds, empty beneath its humming lights. Numbered doors line both walls, ending at a heavy security door at the far end.

The hum slips half a note lower, and the air in the frame seems to tighten around it.

At the far end of the hall, by the security door, something small steps into the light and becomes, slowly, a child.

> **[EARLIER FOOTAGE]**
>
> **[CAM 11: CELL BLOCK C | TIMESTAMP UNAVAILABLE]**

They sit in the corner of a cell barely longer than they are tall, knees drawn tight against their chest, so still the footage seems to have frozen.

A thin strip of light slides in beneath the barred door. Now and then a shadow crosses it, and the child's eyes follow each one, wary and unmoving. Their breath fogs in the cold. It is the only proof they are alive.

Footsteps stop outside. A figure in white stands just beyond the bars, head bowed to peer at the child.

"Still awake?"

The figure's face stays out of the light.

The child does not answer.

"You want to leave, right?"

The child stays silent, but their eyes lift an inch toward the tall shape above them.

The figure crouches beside the bars. Their eyes are lost in shadow.

"I asked you a question."

The child stares through the bars.

"…what?"

Their voice is weak, almost indistinguishable from the silence around them.

"You don't like it here. The tests. All that pain. You're tired, aren't you?"

The child's fingers tighten around their knees.

"Do you want me to let you out?"

The child glances at the empty corridor behind the figure.

Then back at the door.

"Tell me. This is your only chance."

Slowly, they unfold themselves and crawl forward, a thin, scaly tail curled around their waist like a belt. Confusion crosses their face, then pain, then a glimmer of hope.

One small hand reaches through the bars. The other grips the cold metal.

"Yes."

The figure leans closer.

"I can't hear you. Speak up."

"Please," the child says. Their voice gains strength, but it is as shattered as glass. "Please! PLEASE!"

Teeth catch the light. A smile.

The child snatches their hand back.

"Oh."

The figure watches the small fingers vanish into the dark. The smile begins to unfurl, slow and malicious.

"That's all it took?"

The child stays on their knees, rigid. Their breathing quickens.

"You actually believed me."

A short laugh scrapes from the figure's throat. It builds into something louder, a hollow, hysterical sound that rattles the microphone.

"Listen carefully, reptile. You still don't seem to understand. The only reason any of you dragons are still alive is that you can still be useful."

The child backs away, their body moving on instinct alone.

"Be grateful we haven't slaughtered you. You will serve the purpose we have blessed you with. That alone is your reason to live."

The figure stands.

"Freedom."

Another laugh, slower this time. Almost humored, with a thick undertone of disgust.

"All of you scaled bastards should have died five hundred years ago."

The footsteps recede.

The child waits until the sound is gone.

They crawl back to the corner, pressing against the wall until they nearly seem a part of it. The tail tightens around them. Their shoulders begin to shake.

The recording keeps running.

No sound reaches the microphone at first.

Minutes pass. The child does not look up.

Then, faintly, a hitching breath.

> **[CAM 02: CONTAINMENT CHAMBER | PARTIAL RECOVERY]**

Their eyes drift toward the voice.

“That’s better.”

The image skips.

A needle. A gloved thumb presses beside the wound.

The child’s back lifts from the table. The straps catch them before they can rise any farther. A sound escapes their open mouth, thin and cut short by a breath that will not come.

Skip.

A hand pins their wrist to the steel. Their fingers spread, trembling. Beneath the restraint, a narrow smear of blood marks where they have pulled against it.

“Hold still.”

Skip.

The cloth is darker now. One corner hangs over the table’s edge.

A drop falls.

Then another.

A white sleeve crosses the lens. Behind it, the child’s heels scrape against the steel. The motion stops when the sleeve moves away.

Skip.

The child is staring past the camera now. Their hands lie loose against the table. A tear follows the curve of their cheek and disappears beneath their head; they make no movement to wipe it away.

A scientist asks them a question.

Then asks it again.

Their chest rises in small, uneven movements.

Their eyes stay open.
