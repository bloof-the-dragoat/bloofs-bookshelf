
# Chapter 11 - Again

> [!warning] Content Note  
> Graphic violence, injury, and killing.

They all moved at once.

I'd been watching the knife-man. That was the mistake. I'd put my whole attention on the thin man with the narrow blade, and in the half second I took to look, the room closed.

The chain was the first thing.

It had been hanging off the crossbar of Doyle's frame, the one I'd noticed and filed away. The fourth man, the one on my left, had taken hold of it without a sound. He gave it a snap. Steel links whipped out of the dark and wrapped my right forearm to the elbow, and the shock ran up my arm into my shoulder.

_The knife._

I couldn't swing. I couldn't pull. The chain bit through the sleeve into the skin, and I felt something in my wrist grind. I tried to burst away, to take the first step and snap it free, and the burst came, a spasm of heat in my legs, and it took me _into_ the chain instead of out of it.

_Too far._

I'd overshot. The speed had carried me exactly where the man wanted me, and the slack went out of the chain with a hard, flat crack. I stumbled. My feet went from under me.

The knife-man was already there.

I never saw him cross the floor. He was suddenly in front of me, close enough to smell, and his hand came up from his hip in one smooth, unhurried arc. I saw the blade. I saw the tip of it, narrow and bright, aimed at a place I couldn't have named.

It went in under my breastbone.

It didn't hurt at first. That was the horror of it. It felt like a hard shove, a cold, flat pressure, as though someone had pressed a thumb into my stomach. Then he twisted, and the cold turned to something enormous.

_Shit._

It went in a hand's depth. It went up. I felt it find a thing inside me that wasn't supposed to be found, and I felt that thing _give,_ a wet, soft, terrible tearing. A sound came out of my mouth. It wasn't a scream. It was smaller and worse than a scream.

"There," the knife-man said, almost tenderly. He pulled the blade out.

I looked down.

I saw it. I'll never forget it. A dark flower opened across my uniform, spreading through the fabric, and under it something hot began to pour down my legs. My knees buckled. The chain went slack. I had my own knife in my left hand, and I couldn't lift it. I couldn't remember how.

"_Spade!_"

It was Voeren's voice, from very far away, and it was the worst sound I'd ever heard.

The big one hit me then.

I hadn't seen him either. He'd come from behind, the brute, a shape like a wall, and his fist came around in a short, flat, ugly hook. It caught me in the left side, over the ribs.

I heard it. A crack, then a series of cracks, dry and quick, like a bundle of kindling stepping out of a fire. The force lifted me clean off the floor. My feet left the ground, and for one stretched, silent moment I was in the air, and I thought, quite clearly, _that's what a punch from an enhanced person feels like,_ and then I hit the concrete.

I skidded. I slid through blood that was mine, five meters, six, until my shoulder struck the base of a frame and stopped me. My lungs wouldn't work. My ribs had folded in on one side like a crushed can, and every breath I tried to take ended in a wet, tearing grind. I lay on my back with my mouth open, and the work light blazed above me, and the room tilted slowly and sank away.

_Get up._

I couldn't.

_Get up. **Get up**._

I knew it was no use. I knew it the way I'd known it on the table in the Doctor's lab. My body had been taken apart, and nobody was going to stop it.

The sound began somewhere to my right.

It wasn't a word. It was a raw, ragged, rising noise, a roar that rose to a scream and tore in the middle. It was my name. It was hers, and it went on and on, and under it, there was the groan of steel being hauled against its bolts. I turned my head. It took everything.

Voeren was straining against the frame.

Every cord in her neck stood out. The blood had gone from her face, and the bruises stood out like paint. Her shoulders had come forward in spite of the bar, and the cuffs bit into her wrists, and a red line of blood ran down each forearm and dripped from her fingertips. The frame shuddered. Its bolts rang in the concrete. It didn't give, because it had been built not to.

"_SPADE!_"

Her good eye found me across the room. It was wide open, and it was the color of something I'd never seen. It wasn't fear. I had seen her afraid, and this was past that. It was something stripped and animal, without a single wall left in it.

I tried to say her name.

Nothing came out but air.

"Well," the big man said, in the dry, pleasant voice. He sounded faintly disappointed. "That was quick."

_Don't breathe._

The thought was the only clear one I had. It came out of some old, cold place under the pain, a place that remembered a floor in a bar and a man's weight across my chest.

_Don't move. Don't let them see._

I lay on my side with my face turned toward the wall, my right arm flung out and my left folded under me. Blood lay warm and slick under my hip and spread slowly outward. I could feel it running toward the slope of the floor, toward the drain. I didn't have to see the wound. I knew what it looked like. A hole the size of a fist, and a ruin behind it.

My knife was under my left hand. I'd fallen on it.

_Hold still._

The hot, crawling ache began.

It was the same as before, the same slow, relentless, furious pull, but it was _faster._ I could feel it in the first two seconds. It wasn't a trickle now. It was a tide, coming in against the damage in a long, grinding sweep. Something in my belly shifted and tightened, a wet, awful sliding like a rope being hauled through water. My ribs began to move under my skin.

_Count._

_One._

I would not make a sound. I bit down on the inside of my cheek until I tasted copper.

_Two. Three._

"Check it," the big man said.

Footsteps crossed the floor. They were slow and heavy, and they came close, and a boot nudged my shoulder.

I let it. I let my body roll an inch and fall back. I let my head loll. My eyes were shut, and I kept them shut, and I made my chest as still as I could and let the air in and out so slowly that nothing in it moved.

_Four. Five. Six._

"Look at that," a voice said above me, rough with amusement. It was the fourth man, the one with the chain. "Opened him up like a letter."

"Pulse?"

"Don't need one." The boot pushed at my hip. "Look at his belly, boss. You can see the floor."

I didn't know what the wound looked like. I could feel it. At that moment, it was open, a raw, bleeding mouth, and every breath I didn't take was a small lie told to a man who wanted to believe it.

_Eight. Nine._

"Dead?" said the big man.

"Dead."

"Good." A pause. "Leave him. He'll bleed out into the drain."

The boots moved away. I didn't open my eyes. I listened to them go, the heavy tread of the fourth man, then a sound I couldn't place, the soft slap of a hand on a face.

"Staff Sergeant."

It was the big man, and his voice was close to Voeren's frame.

"I'm sorry about your friend. I really am. I'd hoped for a little more of a performance." A rustle. "He had a good first ten seconds. I'll give him that."

Voeren didn't answer.

_Eleven. Twelve._

I'd begun to feel the ribs knit. It was an obscene sensation, a dozen small, wet clicks like a hand running over a row of switches. Each one sent a bolt of light up my side. My left lung, which had been a sodden, folded thing, shuddered and began to unfold.

I held my breath. I didn't dare take one. I felt the whole room go dim around the edges.

_Fifteen._

"You know," said the big man, almost gently, "I'd have liked to keep him. Someone like that. Fast, clever. You could do a lot with that. But no matter." He sighed. "He wasn't going to live anyway, was he? Nobody told him it was a trap. Nobody told him you were the bait."

"Say that again."

Her voice was very low.

I'd never heard it that way. It had no volume at all. It was a flat, quiet, level thing, like a blade laid on a table.

"I said, you were the bait. You and your friend. We needed someone to come." A small, satisfied laugh. "You've been very useful."

"Come here."

"I'm sorry?"

"Come _here._" The frame rattled. "I want to say something to your face."

_Eighteen. Nineteen._

A boot scraped. The big man moved closer. I could hear the soft, careful tread of a man who finds a thing funny and is going to enjoy it. His voice dropped.

"Go on, then."

There was a long, wet, rattling breath.

"When I get out of here," Voeren said, "**I'm going to make you choke on your own intestines**."

The laugh that followed was a real one.

"Oh, that's _good._" He clapped his hands once. "Oh, that's lovely. You hear that, boys? A woman, her hands cuffed, and she's got all of that—"

"I'm going to take it out of you a foot at a time."

"Of course you are."

"**And when I'm done, I'm going to find every one of your friends and do it again**."

His laughter stopped. I couldn't see him, but I felt the silence change, the way a room changes when somebody tests a thing and finds it isn't a bluff.

"Brave," he said at last. His voice had gone a little colder. "Very brave."

"Come closer."

"I don't think so."

"_Coward._"

I understood.

It took me a second. The count had reached twenty-six, and the ribs on my right side had begun to settle, and the first deep, aching breath shuddered through me in a narrow, shallow thread. I felt it in my whole chest. I felt, under the pain, something I hadn't felt in a long time: _space._

_She's not talking to him._

She was talking to give me time.

I opened my left eye a fraction. It was only a slit. The concrete lay a hand's breadth from my face, red and brown and slick with my own blood, and beyond it, at the edge of my vision, was the foot of her frame. I couldn't see her face. I could see her boots, bare and bloodied, braced against the steel. I could see the line of her calf, taut with the strain.

And I saw, very slightly, her toes move.

They flexed. Twice. A small, quick, deliberate motion, a signal.

_I see you._

I let out a breath I hadn't known I'd been holding. It came out as a thin, wet whisper, and I made it into a nothing.

"You know what your problem is?" the big man said, somewhere above her. "You think strength is the same thing as power. It's not. Power is deciding who comes in and who doesn't, and who leaves." A pause. "And you don't."

"Keep talking."

"Excuse me?"

"Keep talking. You're the most interesting man in the room." Her voice was rough as a rasp. "Tell me about it."

He laughed again, delighted, and began to talk.

_Thirty. Thirty-one._

I lay in the dark and listened to the cruelest man I'd ever met describe his philosophy to a woman who was bleeding on the floor, and I counted. I counted because it was the only thing that held. The stab wound was closing. I could feel it in my gut, a long, slow, red-hot suturing, as though someone had taken a needle of fire to my insides and was sewing me shut. My eyes watered. My teeth clamped on the inside of my cheek until it tore.

_Don't. Don't. Don't make a sound._

_Thirty-eight. Forty._

The others had drifted. I could hear them: the soft scrape of the knife-man cleaning his blade on his sleeve, a low grunt as the brute shook out his hand, the quiet tread of the fourth man circling the frames. They'd gathered, loosely, around her. Drawn to the show. Not one of them was looking at the floor.

_Forty-six._

My own knife was still under my hand. I closed my fingers around the hilt. It was warm, and wet, and it felt like the only real thing in the world.

_Fifty._

The last rib clicked. My stomach gave a long, slow, shuddering heave, and something deep inside me knit shut with a pull I felt all the way to my spine. A wave of heat washed through my body. My vision cleared.

I took a breath.

It was a full one, deep and slow, the first in nearly a minute. It filled the whole of my chest. It hurt like fire, and I could have wept.

_Fifty-eight._

I opened my eyes.

I didn't move at first.

I lay there for two full breaths and let the room come back. It was a slow, careful thing, like surfacing from deep water. The concrete was cold under my cheek. The drain's metal smell hung in the air. Somewhere to my right, the big man's voice rolled on, pleased with itself, and beyond it I could hear the soft, steady grind of Voeren's breath.

_Positions._

I knew them without looking. I'd mapped them while I lay dead, one sound at a time. The leader stood in front of her frame with his back to the room. The brute stood at his shoulder, shaking out his fist like a man who'd hit a bag too hard. The knife-man had leaned against the near side of Doyle's frame to wipe his blade, a lazy scrape of steel on cloth. And the fourth man, the one with the chain, was still pacing the circle, the links trailing behind him with a soft, clinking sound.

_Four._

I counted what I had. A knife, still under my left hand. A body that had been put back together a minute ago and still felt like it was held with tape. A reserve so low I could feel the bottom of it, a hollow scrape where the heat should have been. There wasn't enough left to fight four enhanced men.

There was enough for four _steps._

_The first step. Only the first. Never the second._

I drew my knees under me a quarter-inch at a time, and I passed the knife from my left hand to my right without letting the blade touch the floor. I turned my face against the concrete so the light wouldn't catch my eyes. My blood had dried tacky on the floor, and it pulled at my sleeve as I rose, and I held my breath until it stopped.

The fourth man came around the frame.

He was ten feet from me, walking away, his head tilted and his shoulders loose. He'd looped the end of the chain around his fist and was swinging it in a slow, idle arc, like a man with nothing to do. I watched his boots. I watched his weight settle onto his left foot and lift off his right.

_Now._

The first step took me across the gap. It was a clean, small burst, two meters in a single beat, and I stopped exactly where I'd meant to, a hand's breadth behind his shoulder. I put the point of the knife into the hollow at the base of his skull.

I'd learned from a man in a bar that nobody is armored everywhere.

It went in with almost no resistance. I felt it find the join between the spine and the bone, and I angled it up and pushed. His whole body locked. The chain slid out of his fist and I caught it in my left hand before it hit the floor, a long, cold coil of steel that didn't make a sound. I eased him down onto his side, and his boots scraped once and went still.

I didn't look at him.

The knife-man was ten meters away, leaning on the frame with his back to me, scraping his blade along his sleeve. I could see the polished steel catching the work light, a long, narrow, bright strip. As I crossed the floor I saw something else in it too, a small, dark, blurred shape behind him, rising out of the floor.

_Me._

His head came up.

It was a small movement, a flicker. He'd seen the reflection. He turned, and his face was already changing, the lazy smile folding into something sharp and puzzled.

I took the first step. I took it clean. Four meters in the blink of an eye, and then I was inside his reach, with my left hand clamped on his wrist and his blade turned aside. His fingers opened. The narrow knife dropped to the floor with a small, bright ring.

He stared at me.

"You," he said.

His eyes went to my chest, to the torn, stiff, bloody hole in my shirt and the clean, whole skin that showed beneath it.

"That's—"

I put my knife into his armpit.

It went up under the plates where the arm meets the body, and I felt it find the big artery there and tear it open. I pulled it out and put it into his throat, and what he'd been about to say turned into a long, wet, bubbling sound. I held him against the frame until the strength went out of his knees, and then I let him slide down and sit.

His eyes stayed open. I didn't close them.

The brute had noticed.

It was the big one with the flat, heavy face, the one who'd broken my ribs. He'd turned at the sound, and I watched the thought cross his face like a cloud over a field. His mouth opened.

"Boss," he said. "Boss, he's—"

He came at me like a falling wall.

I didn't meet him. I let him come. I dropped under the first swing, and his fist went over my head with a sound like a slammed door. I went past his knee, and in the same motion I cut the back of it. It was a short, hard slash through the cord behind the joint, and it opened like a seam.

His leg folded. He went down on that side with a roar, and I was already behind him, and he turned his head, a slow, enormous, bewildered motion. He was looking for me. I let him find me.

I put the knife through his eye.

I felt it stop against the back of the socket, and I pushed. He shuddered. His big hands closed on empty air. Then every ounce of the weight went out of him at once, and he lay on the concrete with his cheek against the floor and his one good eye pointing at nothing.

"_Spade!_"

Voeren's voice cracked across the hall.

I turned.

The big man had turned too.

He stood where he'd been, three meters from her frame. He was looking at me. For the first time all morning, he wasn't smiling.

I stood in the white light with blood to my elbows, and I watched a thought cross his face and fail to settle.

"That's not possible," he said.

"Told you," said Voeren.

She said it softly. Her voice was a ruin, split and cracked and full of blood, and underneath it was something fierce, glad, and savage. "I told you to keep talking."

He looked at her. Then he looked at me.

"You were dead."

"**I got better.**"

It wasn't a clever line. It was just the truest thing I had. I watched it land.

"*Then I'll just have to kill you again*."

He came for me.

He was enormous, and he was faster than the others, a long, flat lunge with both arms wide, the kind of rush that ends a fight in a second. I took the first step, and it was exactly the right one. I stopped a hand's breadth outside his reach. His hands closed on air.

I felt it then. I'd felt it for hours, a low, grinding thing under the pain and the hunger, and I'd kept it shut in a box in the back of my head. The box came open.

I wasn't thinking of anything. I was thinking of everything.

I was thinking of a corridor with a mustache drawn on a poster. I was thinking of every _sit down, Sergeant,_ every _yes, sir,_ every time I'd said _fine_ and put a task in my hands and walked out of a room with my jaw shut. I was thinking of a card with a colored tab. I was thinking of a boy named Haskins who'd made a bad joke, and a man named Doyle hanging by his wrists, and a woman in a steel frame with her face beaten shut, who had gone on holding her head up.

I was thinking of the sound of a lens clouding red.

He swung. I let it pass.

*Again.*

He'd said it so easily, as if dying were something a person could be asked to do twice. I'd been choked on a gantry. I'd been choked in a bar. I'd been opened on a floor a few minutes ago, and I'd lain in my own blood and counted. Everyone I had ever known had assumed I would take it again.

I stepped inside, and I put the knife into him.

It went in under his ribs. He gasped. I pulled it out and put it in again, higher, and he grabbed at my sleeve with a hand that had no strength in it. I drove him back against the frame, and I stabbed him again.

Again.

I stabbed.

**Again.**

I know that the knife kept going in and coming out. I know that he went down, and I went down with him, and I was on my knees on the concrete with my hands slick to the wrists. I know there was a sound in the room, a low, harsh, ragged sound, and I know it was coming from me. Hot blood sprayed across my face, my throat, my chest. It ran into my mouth. I tasted copper and salt and something I couldn't name. I kept stabbing. I kept stabbing a man who'd stopped moving a long time ago.

_Stop,_ said a very small voice somewhere inside me, a long way off. _Stop. This isn't—_

**Again.**

_It's not enough. It's not enough. It's not—_

**AGAIN.**

"_Spade._"

It came from far away. It was low, and rough, and utterly steady.

"Spade."

I stopped.

My arm was raised. The knife was in my fist, dripping. I could feel it trembling, a fine, hard shiver that ran from my shoulder to the blade.

"Spade," Voeren said again, very quietly. "That's enough. He's gone."

I looked down.

I couldn't have said what I was looking at. It was a shape, and it was dark, and it was wet, and it had stopped being a man. Blood had run out across the concrete in a wide, glistening sheet. It covered my hands and my forearms. It had soaked through my shirt to the skin and clung to my face in a warm, tacky mask. It dripped from my chin.

_I did that._

The thought arrived, flat and clear. It had no horror in it yet. The horror would come later, in the dark, in a bed I didn't want to be in.

I looked up.

She was standing in the middle of the room, in her frame, with her wrists raw and her eye swollen shut, and she was looking at me. Her face held no fear. It didn't hold judgment, either. It held something more careful than either, a kind of quiet, terrible attention, as though she were learning a language.

"Come here," she said.

I couldn't.

"Spade," she said. "Come here. Slow."

I put the knife down. I did it carefully, as though it might break. I stood up, and my knees nearly went, and the room turned slowly around me.

I didn't count the bodies. I didn't want the number.

---

I don't remember crossing the floor.

I remember the rack. It rose out of the dark in front of me like a thing in a dream, a long steel bank of levers under a hooded lamp, and I put my hand on the first one and it slipped.

I looked at my hand.

It was red to the wrist. The blood had begun to dry in the creases of my knuckles and was still wet over the palm, and the lever slid out of my grip like a wet bar of soap.

_Wipe it._

I wiped my hand on my trousers. It didn't help. I wiped it again, and I took a breath, and I tried to remember what she'd said.

_Top left is Doyle. It's hydraulic. You've got to hold it down._

"Spade."

Her voice was close behind me. It was rough, and cracked, and very quiet. 

"I—," I said. "I know. I know."

I took the lever in both hands and pulled.

It resisted. A heavy, grudging pressure fought me for the first inch, and then something in the wall gave a long, hydraulic hiss. Across the room, Doyle's frame shuddered. The cross-bar at his chest swung away from him with a clunk. The cuffs at his wrists and ankles snapped open one after another, a ragged, clanking chorus, and he slumped forward and hung by his arms from nothing.

"Hold it," Voeren said. "Hold it down."

I held it. My arms shook. The lever tried to rise under my slick hands, and I leaned on it with all my weight, and my vision went gray at the edges.

_Don't._

"Now the second," she said. "Next one over. Don't let go of the first. _Spade._"

I couldn't hold both. I knew it. I looked at the second lever, and I looked at the first, and I did the only thing I could think of. Somewhere on the way across the floor, I'd picked up the knife-man's narrow blade. I didn't remember doing it. I wedged it under the first handle and jammed it against the frame. The lever held.

I pulled the second.

It came easier. The frame at the center of the hall gave a deep, metallic groan. I heard the clamp at her chest release. I heard the cuffs on her wrists spring open with a sound like a dropped chain, and one by one, the bolts at her ankles and her knees.

She dropped.

I'd expected her to fall. She didn't. She caught herself on the floor with both hands and stood up in a single motion, and the air around her changed. I felt it, the old, hard pressure, like walking into a wall of heat. Her shoulders rolled. The cords stood out on her forearms. In the white light, with her face ruined and her blood on the concrete, she looked, for one instant, like the thing the Combine had been afraid of.

She crossed the floor in three strides.

I didn't see her cross it. I had my back against the rack, and the room had begun to tilt. The pain I'd been holding off for the last ten minutes was coming in at last, in one long, immense, crushing wave. It rolled up from my belly into my chest, and my ribs began to burn, and every muscle in my body seized at once.

_Not now._

"Spade." She was in front of me. Her hands were on my shoulders. They were enormous, and warm, and shaking. She didn't flinch from the blood. "Hey. Look at me."

"The strike," I said.

I hadn't known I was going to say it. It came out in a thin, dry thread.

"What?" 

"Nine-thirty." My tongue felt thick. It took a long time to find the words. "There's a strike. Stroud— he called it. On the annex."

Her face changed.

"Nine-thirty," she said. "What time is it?"

I tried to look at my watch. The numbers swam. "I don't— it's—"

"Spade."

"Minutes." I wasn't sure. I couldn't hold the thought. "Not many. I don't— _Doyle._ He needs—"

"I've got him."

"Pouch." I tried to lift my hand. It wouldn't go. "On my vest. Pressure. Hold—"

"Spade. I've got him. I've got _both_ of you."

I looked up at her.

She was crying. She didn't seem to know it. Tears were running down her ruined face, through the dried blood, and she looked at me the way she'd looked at me in the dim light of a hospital room, and on the pier at dawn, and in the doorway of my apartment, when she'd said _I can do that._ It was no use. I couldn't look away.

"Not—my blood," I said. It was almost a whisper. I'd meant to say something else. "Most of it."

A sound came out of her. It was a laugh, or the ghost of one. It broke in the middle.

"I know."

"I'm sorry."

"For what?" Her voice cracked. "For _what,_ Spade?"

I didn't have an answer. I'd meant the rest of it. I'd meant the sound I'd made. I'd meant the man on the floor, and the knife, and the part of me that had gone somewhere dark and hadn't wanted to come back.

The wave hit me.

It was the whole day, every minute of it, arriving at once. The knife and the punch. The heat and the hunger. The hundred small wounds I'd taken and closed, and every second of the pain I'd put off, coming due in a single instant. My legs went. The floor swung up. My vision narrowed to a tiny, bright circle with her face in the middle of it.

_I'm going to fall._

She caught me.

I felt her arms close around me, and I felt the whole weight of me go out of my body and into hers. She took it without a sound. One of her hands cupped the back of my head, and the other held my ribs, and she was warm and solid and smelled of blood and sweat and something under both that I'd have known anywhere. She didn't care what I was covered in. She held on anyway.

"I've got you," Voeren said.

Her voice was low, and rough, and utterly certain.

"I've got you, Spade."

It was the second time she had said it. The first time, I'd done much less to earn it.

*Again,* I thought, and I let go.
