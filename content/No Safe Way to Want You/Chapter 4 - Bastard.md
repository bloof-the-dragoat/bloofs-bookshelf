## Chapter 4: Bastard

> [!warning] Content note
> Graphic violence, eye injury, strangulation, sexist and gender-based harassment, sexual threats, traumatic recollections, references to prior assault, alcohol impairment, and medication misuse.

> [!important] Driving note
> Never drink and drive. In this fictional setting, the vehicle assumes full autonomous control on a supported route before moving; neither intoxicated occupant drives or is required to supervise it.

His fist came over the end of the table. I ducked toward the seat back. His knuckles caught my ear instead of my face, a sharp sting through the piercings, and I drove the glass up into him.

It broke against his cheek.

The jolt went straight through my tender palm. Alcohol splashed across his face, and he stumbled back with a hand clamped over one eye. What remained of the glass cut into my fingers. I dropped it.

Voeren’s hand left my shoulder. “Spade—”

A man reached past the leader for my shirt. Voeren caught his reaching arm, stepped back enough to make room, and drove a boot into his chest.

He hit the table across the aisle. One side collapsed beneath him. A pitcher burst against the floor, and a seated man went backward with his chair, both hands flying toward his face.

For a moment everyone looked. Then the fallen man’s friend grabbed the stranger by the shirt.

“What the fuck is wrong with you?”

The stranger swung at him.

A second contractor shoved in to help his friend. Someone behind him tried to pull him away and got an elbow in the mouth for it. A woman backed out from the next table, shouting for everyone to stop, and collided with a man carrying three drinks.

Most people moved toward the walls or the exits. A few moved toward their friends. The aisle disappeared.

I slid out of the booth while there was still a gap. My knee resisted the turn, stiff from sitting, and I caught the table edge to keep my feet underneath me. Pain flashed across my palm.

*Excellent start.*

Voeren shoved someone away from the booth. The second large man stepped into her path, with the muscular woman moving around his other side. Then two of their friends came for me.

The first threw a wide right. I stepped inside its arc and caught his arm against my forearm, turning him across the narrow space between the booth and a chair. The second man was already swinging. I ducked. His fist struck his friend above the ear.

“Watch it!”

I came up close enough to smell beer on the second man’s breath and drove my elbow into his face. He reeled into the booth divider. I moved before he could recover, but my boot dragged against a chair leg and stole half a step.

The first man caught my sleeve. I turned with the pull instead of fighting it, took his wrist with both hands, and stepped across his path. My back protested when I bent. I kept the movement small.

His balance went before his grip did. He hit the floor shoulder-first, dragging me down farther than I wanted. I released him, planted a boot, and straightened with a hot pull through my lower back.

He rolled onto an elbow. I kicked his arm out from under him. His chin struck the floor, and he stayed there, swearing through clenched teeth.

The other man came off the divider with blood running from his nose. I put the chair between us and shoved it into his knees. He stumbled over it, reaching for the seat, and I moved past him.

I was breathing too hard already.

Across the aisle, the muscular woman caught Voeren’s upper arm. Voeren turned into her, broke the grip, and drove her backward into the larger man. Both stayed on their feet. He grabbed a table and shoved it between them, knocking a chair sideways.

Voeren looked down at the table, then back at him. “Hah! You paid for all that muscle and this is what you do with it?”

He lunged over it. I lost the rest behind a man trying to get his friend off the floor.

A bottle cracked beside me.

The other woman from the group stood at the end of the booth with a broken bottle in her hand.

*Seriously? You too?*

She thrust it toward my stomach. I stepped back and turned. The jagged edge caught the loose front of my overshirt, pulling it taut before tearing free. There wasn’t room for another retreat.

I knocked her wrist outward with my forearm and caught her arm above the elbow. She tried to wrench away. I followed, keeping the bottle pointed past me, and drove her wrist against the booth divider.

Glass struck wood. The bottle fell.

She swung her free hand at my face. I tucked my chin, took the blow along the side of my head, and hooked a boot behind her ankle. My knee gave a sharp warning when I shifted my weight. I abandoned the wider sweep and shoved her over the trapped foot instead.

She landed on her hip and shoulder, hard enough to stop her next breath.

I let go. “Don’t.”

I wasn’t sure whether I meant the bottle or getting up.

A movement at the edge of my vision made me turn. The leader was already coming. I couldn’t clear his path. I jumped back anyway.

His shoulder struck my ribs. The floor left my feet.

I hit the edge of another table and heard its legs scrape before I went down beside it. My teeth caught my lower lip. Something warm ran over my chin.

I tried to breathe.

Failed.

My mouth opened again. My chest jerked around an empty, useless effort.

*Shit.*

He came around the table with one hand still pressed to his face. His left eye was swollen shut. Blood ran between his fingers.

No ordinary man should have been able to throw me like that.

*Enhanced.*

Aftermarket, probably. Not that knowing where he’d bought it would help.

I got a foot under me and pushed up against the table. My back seized. I stood anyway.

He reached. I knocked his hand aside and moved past his shoulder, striking him in the ribs as I went. He grunted. I hit him again, higher, and turned an elbow into his jaw.

His head moved. The rest of him didn’t.

He caught the back of my overshirt. I twisted toward his hand, fabric tightening across my shoulders. A button snapped free. Before I could slip out of the shirt, he hauled me back into him.

My knee folded awkwardly. Then I was on the floor, his weight following.

I got my right forearm between us before his elbow came across my throat. My left arm was trapped beneath his knee, wrist bent against the floor. I tried to turn onto my side. He flattened me, pressing pain through the fading bruises on my neck.

“What’s wrong, bitch?” His breath struck my face. “Where’s your big friend now?”

I pushed against his elbow and got enough space for a thin breath.

“Busy,” I rasped. “Your friends needed help standing.”

His mouth tightened. The elbow came down harder. I clawed for room beneath it, my fingertips slipping against his sleeve.

“Funny little fucker.”

A table crashed somewhere beyond him. He glanced toward the sound, and the pressure eased just enough for me to breathe again.

“Giving that good shit to women now.” He looked back down at me. “Should’ve given it to me.”

I tried to pull my left arm free. His knee ground harder against it.

“Look at you. Don’t even know what the fuck you are.” His gaze moved over my face. “Sure as shit ain’t a man.”

I didn’t have the air to correct him. Or the slightest inclination.

I planted my boots and tried to shift my hips. He followed easily, keeping me pinned. My ribs hurt every time I managed a breath.

His attention dropped to my shirt, pulled open where he’d grabbed it. Then lower.

My stomach turned.

“Not bad, though,” he said. “Without that mouth on you.”

I shoved at his elbow again. He smiled at the effort.

“Tell that bitch to fuck off. You can come with me instead. I’ll even forgive the glass—”

Voeren’s voice cut through the room. “Spade!”

His head started to turn. I drove my free hand into his face. My thumb found his open eye.

He screamed.

The elbow lifted off my throat, and I sucked in air so hard it hurt.

“You bastard!”

He jerked back. For one instant his weight shifted off my trapped arm. I pulled it free.

Then both his hands closed around my neck.

My head struck the floor. I hit his wrist. His arm. His face. My knuckles glanced off his cheek, and he squeezed harder.

The room narrowed. His mouth was moving. I couldn’t hear the words.

*Too fucking familiar.*

A knee on my chest. Grit against my cheek. Someone dragging a sheet across concrete.

*No.*

I could see the booth above his shoulder. The torn seam in the upholstery. A pale scrape where something had struck the wood. This wasn’t the yard.

Still no air.

My hands stopped trying to pull his apart.

*Do something else.*

His weight was forward now, both arms committed to my throat. I turned beneath him as far as I could, rolling onto my left side. Pain tore through my ribs. My right hip lifted clear of the floor, and I reached behind it.

Overshirt. Belt. The sheath.

My fingers slipped once on the handle.

*Come on.*

I caught it.

He couldn’t see what I was doing. His eyes were squeezed shut, his face wet, his hands still tightening. I pulled the knife free and brought it up between us. There was no room for a proper swing.

I struck at the side of his head, close enough that my knuckles scraped his ear. The blade met resistance.

I pushed.

His whole body jerked. For a moment his grip stayed exactly as it was.

Then one hand slipped.

I dragged in a broken breath. His weight came down before I could move, pinning my right elbow against my side. His face pressed against my shoulder. Something warm spread through the fabric there.

I waited for him to lift his head.

He didn’t.

I worked my forearm out from between us far enough to pull the knife free. Then I tried to shove him away. Nothing moved.

“Get—”

The word scraped apart in my throat. I tried again. My ribs flared. His body rose with the effort, barely, and settled back over me.

Boots struck the floor beside the table.

I flinched. The large man from the group stumbled backward into view, his heels skidding through spilled beer. Beyond him, the front door stood open and people were crowding out onto the pavement.

Voeren caught him before he found his balance. She drove him down the clear stretch of aisle and threw him through the doorway. He struck the frame with one shoulder and disappeared outside.

“YOU BASTARDS GET THE FUCK AWAY FROM THEM!”

She turned back toward me. The muscular woman was on her hands and knees beside the overturned table, trying to rise. Voeren stepped past her, one hand held out toward the remaining crowd.

Nobody came closer.

She stopped beside my head. “Spade.”

I looked up. Her mouth was drawn back from her teeth. Blood marked one corner of it, and her chest was rising fast. I had seen her excited in a fight. I had heard her laugh while people tried to kill her.

She hadn’t sounded like that. Not a damn time.

Her gaze dropped to the man across me. Then a metallic clack came from the counter.

“Enough.”

The bartender stood behind it with a shotgun. A towel lay crumpled beside her hand. I hadn’t seen her get the weapon. I hadn’t seen where she’d kept it.

Voeren went still. So did the woman beside the overturned table.

“Hands where I can see them,” the bartender said.

A man near the pool table slowly set down a cue. The bartender watched him do it, then came around the end of the counter, keeping the muzzle down the aisle and her finger clear of the trigger. Nobody stepped into her path.

A cigarette hung from the corner of her mouth. Up close, she looked younger than the voice had made her sound. Thirties, maybe. There was a fresh red mark across one forearm.

Her gaze passed over Voeren, then down to me. To the body.

“You military?”

“Yeah,” Voeren said.

“Thought so.”

“Where’d you get that sick gun?”

The bartender looked at the weapon as if she’d forgotten she was carrying it.

“Same place I’ll take that guy.” She nodded toward the corpse on top of me. “Nowhere.”

One corner of Voeren’s mouth moved. Then she crouched.

“I’m getting him off you.”

I tried to nod. She took hold of his shirt and lifted. His weight left my chest in one awful pull, and I curled onto my side, coughing.

It hurt everywhere.

Voeren lowered him beside the table. He landed loosely, one arm turning palm-up.

I looked away.

“Any of y’all got a problem with that?” the bartender asked the room.

Nobody answered. One contractor had a wad of napkins pressed to his friend’s forehead. Another sat on the edge of the broken table, staring at the blood on his hands. The man I had put down near the booth was moving now, slowly, with his cheek swollen and one arm held against his chest.

No one looked eager to continue.

“Good,” the bartender said. “The rest of you can clear out. If your friend can’t walk, carry them.”

The muscular woman sat back against the overturned table. She looked at the body once, then toward the door.

Voeren knelt beside me. “Hey.”

I tried to answer and coughed instead. Her hand stopped just short of my shoulder.

“Can you sit?”

“Yeah.”

It barely sounded like a word. I pushed myself upright with my left forearm. My right hand still held the bloody knife.

My knife.

I stared at it.

Voeren followed my gaze. “Take your time.”

The blade shook when I moved it toward the sheath. I used the back of my left hand to lift the overshirt clear, then guided it in carefully. It caught at the mouth.

I stopped. Tried again.

This time it slid home.

Only then did I notice how badly my palms were throbbing.

Voeren waited until my hand had left the knife. “Ready?”

I nodded. She offered her forearm. I took it, keeping pressure off the worst of my palm, and she helped me to my feet. My knee straightened reluctantly. The room tilted.

Her other hand settled against my back, above the place that hurt most.

“Easy.”

I stood still until the floor settled. Behind her, the bartender went back to the counter. She put the shotgun down within reach and took a bottle from a shelf.

“Take your buddy and go,” she told Voeren. “And take this.”

Voeren glanced at the label. “Seriously?”

“Before I change my mind.”

The bartender pushed it into her hand, then looked past us at the body.

“Always hated that fuckin dickwipe.” She spat beside his face. “Sexist prick.”

Someone near the pool table asked for their jacket.

The bartender pointed without looking. “Chair by the wall.”

That was all.

No one knelt beside him. No one asked whether he might still be breathing. A man edged around his boots to collect a dropped phone.

I looked down at my shirt. Rust-brown over cream. There was too much red on both.

Voeren’s hand shifted at my back. “Stay here a second.”

She moved to the booth, close enough that I could still reach her, and collected her jacket. The borrowed pen lay beside the plate. She set it on the table edge, clear of the spill.

The drawing was still there. A dark wet patch had spread across one corner of the napkin, stopping short of the harbor walls.

Voeren touched the dry edge. “You want this?”

I looked at her. Then at the city.

“Yeah.”

She folded it once, carefully, and tucked it into her jacket pocket before draping the jacket over the arm holding the bottle. I reached for her free arm without being asked. Her fingers closed lightly around my forearm.

We made it down the aisle slowly. The woman with the bottle had moved to a chair. She kept her eyes on the floor as we passed. Beyond her, the contractors were helping their injured friend stand.

I watched each place I put my feet. Broken glass. A chair leg. Someone’s spilled drink.

At the door, Voeren paused to look outside. The man she’d thrown was sitting against the wall several feet from the entrance, one hand pressed to his shoulder. He saw her and turned his face away.

She guided me past him.

Cold air touched my wet lip. I stepped onto the pavement, and the noise of the bar fell behind us.

The car was where we had left it. Same charcoal paint under the streetlight, same shallow dent in the curb beside the passenger door. I looked at the window and remembered sitting on the other side of it, trying to explain why I wanted to spend the evening with her.

That part, at least, had survived.

Voeren slowed when I did. “Nearly there.”

“I can see it.”

“Good. Was worried you were just following me because I had the tequila.”

“I was following you before you had the tequila.”

She looked down at me. Her smile arrived slowly enough that I had time to regret nothing.

“Yeah,” she said. “You were.”

I tried to laugh, caught at my throat, and stopped. Her hand steadied against my back without pushing me along.

At the car, she opened the passenger door and put the bottle on the floor behind the seat, then laid her jacket across the back. I lowered myself carefully. Pain caught beneath my ribs halfway down, and I stopped with a hand against the door frame.

Voeren waited. “Take a second.”

“I’m taking several.”

“We’ve got time.”

I looked up at her. One corner of her mouth lifted.

I got into the seat and shifted the sheath behind my right hip clear of the backrest. My back objected to that, then to the way I tried to correct it. By the time I settled, my eyes were watering.

Voeren crouched beside the open door. “Want a hand with the belt?”

“I can manage.”

“I know. Wasn’t the question.”

I reached over my shoulder, missed, and looked at her. She kept a remarkably straight face.

“Yes,” I said.

She drew the belt across me slowly, keeping it clear of the sorest part until she had to lower it. Her arm passed close enough that I caught the smell of her jacket on her skin.

“This all right?”

“Yeah.”

The buckle clicked. She looked at my face before drawing back.

“Thank you,” I added.

“You’re welcome.”

She shut the door, and for a few seconds I was alone with the quiet. My right hand lay on my thigh. Blood had dried along the side of my thumb. I rubbed at it with a fingertip, felt the skin underneath sting, and stopped.

The driver’s door opened. Voeren settled into the seat and caught me watching the hand she briefly rested against her side.

“Don’t.”

“I was going to ask nicely.”

“That’s how you get me.”

“I’ll remember.”

She smiled as she fastened her belt and woke the console.

“Aren’t you too drunk to drive?”

“Nahhh.”

I turned my head toward her. She looked back, entirely pleased with herself.

“This is so dumb.”

“Dumber than starting a bar fight against enhanced while drunk?” She tapped the screen. “And either way, I’m not gonna be the one driving.”

The car remained parked while she selected the route.

“Autonomous. Garrison.”

A pale line crossed the map.

“Supported route confirmed,” the system said. “Autonomous control active. No occupant supervision required.”

Voeren took both hands away from the controls. “Better?”

“I had a whole argument ready.”

“Save it. You’ll find something.”

The car signaled and pulled away from the curb. I watched the bar shrink in the side mirror until a parked van hid the doorway.

Beside me, Voeren let out a breath and tipped her head back against the seat.

“Fuck.”

I looked over. She turned toward me, and the smile starting at the corner of her mouth didn’t quite match the rest of her face.

“What?”

“The two by the booth.”

I glanced down at my hands.

“Before,” she added quickly. “The first two.”

“Oh.”

“You put one straight into the other.”

“He was going that way.”

“He punched his own fucking friend.”

Her laugh escaped before she finished the sentence. I looked at her, then at the dashboard, and could feel myself starting to smile.

“And you came up with the elbow before either of them worked out what happened.” She shifted toward me. “Spade. That was fucking good.”

“You sound surprised.”

“I’m delighted. There’s a difference.”

My face heated. “You had your own thing going on.”

“Still caught that part.” Her grin widened. “You’ve been keeping things from me.”

“I mentioned the guitar. Didn’t realize you wanted a demonstration of everything.”

“I’m starting a list.”

“That sounds threatening.”

“Depends what else you’ve got.”

I looked at her mouth and immediately lost whatever sensible reply I might have had. She noticed. Of course she noticed. Her smile changed, and I turned toward the window.

“Ten seconds,” she said.

“What?”

“Let me enjoy finding out you can do that for ten seconds before you explain why I shouldn’t be impressed.”

I rubbed at the seam of my trousers. “I don’t usually start fights.”

“I know.”

“You don’t know.”

“No,” she conceded. “But you looked more nervous asking me for a drink than you did telling that guy to fuck off.”

“That was worse.”

Her eyebrows rose.

I shut my eyes. “Not what I meant.”

“Too late. Keeping it.”

I laughed, carefully, and felt it pull at my ribs. Her grin faltered when my hand went to my side.

The quiet left room for the part I hadn’t answered. I had known we should leave. I’d counted them twice. Her hand had been on my shoulder, and all I’d had to do was stand.

Instead I had looked at the drawing and thought about the way she’d been laughing.

*So you decided to get hit by a fucking truck. Brilliant.*

“I wanted us to stay,” I said.

Voeren looked at me.

“At the table. Before.” I watched my fingers curl against my thigh. “We were having a good time.”

“We were.”

“I didn’t want him to get to decide that was over.”

She was quiet for a moment, then reached across and touched my forearm.

“I didn’t either.” Her thumb rested against my sleeve. “But I would’ve had a good time somewhere else. With you.”

I looked up. Her face was open in a way that made the answer matter.

“I’m getting there,” I said.

“Good.”

---

Her gaze dropped to where I was holding my side. Before either of us said anything else, she shifted closer and caught the hem of my tee beneath the torn overshirt.

Cool air touched my stomach. Voeren lifted the fabric, looked down, and stopped.

I looked at her. She looked at her hand.

“Oh. Fuck.” Her eyes came back to mine. “Sorry. I should’ve asked.”

Color rose across her cheeks. She started to lower the shirt and withdraw, and I caught her wrist lightly.

“No, it’s okay.”

She paused. “Can I?”

“Yeah. Inspect as you wish.” I looked away, which would have worked better if I hadn’t still been holding her wrist.

A small laugh left her. “As I wish?”

“The injuries.”

“Right.” I glanced back in time to catch her smile. “Those.”

“You’re enjoying this.”

“Not the injuries.”

That was unfairly clear.

I let go of her wrist before I started answering questions she hadn’t asked. She worked the hem clear of the belt and lifted it, careful not to drag the fabric across my side. She lifted the shirt again, slowly this time. Her expression sobered as she studied the swelling beneath my ribs, and two fingertips settled beside it.

I watched her hand. The rings were cool against my skin; her fingers weren’t. She followed the edge of the bruise with a light touch, then rested her palm against an unmarked patch below it.

“You all right?”

“Yeah.”

She glanced up at me. Her eyes stayed there for a beat before returning to my side.

“I didn’t know,” she said.

“What?”

Her gaze moved briefly across my stomach. Then she looked back at the bruise.

“Nothing.”

I smiled despite the heat spreading into my ears. “Very convincing.”

“Let me recover.”

“You’ve been making me do that all evening.”

“And you’ve been doing so well.”

I was still trying to decide whether to be pleased when her fingers moved lower and pressed beside a tender spot.

The sound that escaped me was small and entirely unplanned.

Her hand stopped. “Too much?”

I opened my mouth and found nothing useful. It *had* hurt. There was no confusion about that. My stomach had tightened around the pressure, and then something warm had gone through me so abruptly that I’d almost leaned toward her hand.

*What the fuck?*

“Spade?”

“A bit.”

She lifted her fingers immediately. I missed the contact before I’d finished being relieved.

“Want me to stop?”

“No.” That came out quickly enough to make me look away. “Just… lighter.”

“All right.”

She rested her hand beside the bruise without pressing. I let out a slow breath and concentrated on the passing lights outside.

Pain had never done that before. Or her hand hadn’t been there before. I wasn’t equipped to sort out the difference, especially while she was watching my face as if every little thing it did was worth noticing.

“You’re staring,” she said.

I turned back. “So are you.”

“I’m checking a bruise.”

“Your hand’s missed it.”

Her eyes dropped to where her palm rested against uninjured skin. For once she didn’t have an immediate answer.

I smiled. “There. That’s fair.”

Her laugh was quiet and a little breathless. “You getting brave with me?”

“Apparently.”

She looked at me for a moment longer, then lowered the shirt, careful of the sorest part.

“All right,” she said. “Keeping that too.”

“What’s the verdict?”

“That I like you being brave with me.”

I stared at her.

She smiled, then shook her head. “And that someone who knows what they’re doing needs to look at those ribs.”

“Oh.”

“Both can be true.”

I tried to settle deeper into the seat and regretted it. “I don’t want to go back.”

“The hospital?”

I nodded. She glanced toward the route on the screen.

“They’ll ask,” I said.

Her eyes returned to the blood on my shirt. “Yeah.”

About the drinking. About my neck. About why I’d managed to turn light duty into this before the end of the day.

About the knife.

Voeren rubbed at her jaw. “I’ve got a field injector.”

“Reknit?”

“Yeah. Emergency kit.”

She opened the compartment beside her seat and took out a compact pouch. Dressings, a sealed wipe, the injector. I recognized the markings.

“Weaker than what they gave you,” she said.

“Marlowe said no more.”

Her hand stopped. “How long?”

“Until review. Specifically mentioned field injectors.”

She looked at me, then down at the pouch. “Of course they did.”

“Apparently they had a very accurate picture of my evening.”

That got a brief smile, but she started putting the injector back.

“Wait.”

Her gaze returned to mine. I swallowed and felt the shape of his hands again, the memory laid over the soreness.

“I know what they said.”

“Then why are we doing this?”

I looked at the pouch. “Because I want something to help.”

The answer sat between us without getting any better.

Voeren’s fingers tightened around the injector. “I don’t know what another dose will do after the hospital stuff.”

“Neither do I.”

“And this doesn’t tell us there isn’t something worse going on.”

“I know.”

She waited.

“We still get it checked,” I said.

“Not just if it gets inconvenient.”

I looked up at her. “Yeah. We get it checked.”

I held out my arm. She studied my face for another moment, then opened the wipe.

“You’re sure?”

“No,” I said. “But yes.”

She didn’t laugh.

She cleaned the spot and waited for my nod before using the injector. A brief sting, then nothing dramatic at all. My ribs still hurt. My throat still hurt. When I moved my head too quickly, the car took a moment to catch up.

Voeren capped the injector and put it into a separate pocket of the pouch. I watched her stow it.

“You’ve got blood on your mouth.”

She touched the corner. “Occupational hazard.”

“Your side?”

“A little sore. It’s healed.”

I looked at her.

She smiled. “You want to inspect my work too?”

“Apparently I’ve been checking your side all evening.”

“I noticed.”

“Could’ve saved me some worrying.”

“Would you have believed me?”

I considered that. “Probably needed a second opinion.”

“From?”

“Me. Looking again.”

Her laugh came out loud enough to fill the car. She tipped her head back against the seat, and I smiled despite the ache in my lip.

“There it is,” I said.

She looked at me, still grinning. “What?”

“Much better.”

Her smile softened. “You’ve got blood all over your shirt and you’re trying to make me feel better?”

“I’m versatile.”

“Apparently.”

For a while we watched the city. A takeaway sign passed across the window, bright enough to turn the blood on my sleeve nearly black. Someone stood beneath it with two paper bags and a phone balanced against their shoulder.

I wondered what they were taking home.

---

Voeren retrieved the tequila from behind her seat and looked at the label. I held out my hand.

She looked from it to my face. “You still want a drink after all that?”

“Absolutely.”

“Spade.”

“You’ve still got that mission in three days.”

Her eyebrows rose. Then she laughed. “Fuck. You’re more stressed about it than I am.”

“Someone has to contribute.”

“Almost looks like you’re worried about me.”

“I am!”

The answer came out before I could make it sound less like an accusation. Her smile faltered, and she looked down at the bottle.

“Yeah?”

“I mean… of course I am.” I shifted toward her and felt my ribs object. “They’re sending you back there with three people, after everything that happened. And he stood there blaming you like you decided to get half the team killed.”

The words scraped against my throat. I swallowed and kept going.

“It’s fucked up.”

Voeren held the bottle still between her hands.

“And I’ll be at headquarters,” I said. “Listening.”

“Keeping the net up.”

“I know.”

“Do you?”

“I know the job matters.” I looked down. “It just… doesn’t feel like enough when it’s you out there.”

She didn’t answer immediately. When I looked up, she was watching me without any of the earlier amusement. She shifted the bottle into one hand and lifted the other toward my face.

“Can I?”

I nodded. Her fingers settled beneath my chin and tipped it up gently.

“Spade. I don’t think there’s anyone else I’d trust to have my back right now.”

Every sentence I’d been assembling disappeared.

“You stayed with me,” she said. “You kept finding things we could use. You kept us moving.”

“You were doing most of the—”

“Don’t.” Her thumb shifted lightly against my jaw. “Let me tell you what you did.”

I shut my mouth.

“I want you on that net,” she said. “Not because it gets you out of the way.”

My face was hot. I looked at the bottle because it was easier than looking at her.

“Wow. Haven’t even had any of that yet.”

Her mouth curved. “Still going to blame the tequila?”

“It looks very strong.”

“Mm.”

She didn’t withdraw her hand immediately, and I didn’t move away from it. After a moment, her smile faded.

“What?” I asked.

“Are you okay?”

“These?” I glanced toward my side. “They’ll heal. Eventually. Probably going to complain about it the entire time.”

“I’ll survive.”

“You say that now.”

“Spade.”

I looked back at her. Her gaze moved to my neck.

“I mean him,” she said. “He was about to—”

“Oh. Yeah. He was fucking gross.” I shrugged and regretted that too. “Wouldn’t be the first time.”

Her hand went still.

I heard the words properly once I’d finished saying them. For a second I thought I could keep talking and leave them behind.

“Anyway—”

“Spade.”

I looked at her face. The anger there made my stomach sink before I understood it wasn’t aimed at me. Her jaw had tightened, and her other hand was closed around the bottle hard enough to whiten the skin beside her rings.

“Sorry,” I said. “I didn’t mean— It was a long time ago. It’s not a whole thing.”

She set the bottle upright in the console holder between the seats.

“You don’t have to apologize.”

“I just made this weird.”

“No.”

The word was immediate.

I looked down at my boots. There was a pale scrape across one toe that hadn’t been there earlier.

“Shit happens,” I said.

She was quiet long enough that I wished I could reach into the last minute and take it back. Then her hand left my chin.

For a moment I thought that was it.

Instead, she offered both hands where I could see them and waited until I looked up. I nodded.

She cupped my face gently, keeping clear of the bruises on my neck. The care of it did something worse to me than a question would have. I could answer questions. I could decide how much to say.

I didn’t know what to do with her looking at me like that, *holding* me like that.

“You don’t have to tell me anything else,” she said.

“I wasn’t going to.”

“All right.”

No pause. No attempt to bargain for more.

My eyes started to sting.

*Absolutely fucking not.*

Voeren’s thumbs rested against my cheeks.

“I will never let that happen to you again.”

She said it quietly, with so much certainty that I wanted to climb inside the sentence and stay there.

She couldn’t know that. I knew she couldn’t. Tonight she had been only a few tables away.

But her hands were warm, and she was looking straight at me, and I was tired of wanting things carefully.

A startled laugh escaped before I could stop it.

“Hah. Well. I guess I’m in your hands, then.”

Her expression softened. I leaned into one palm, a small movement I couldn’t pretend was accidental.

She smiled. “Yeah.”

I let myself stay there.

Then the console interrupted.

“Garrison arrival in—”

Voeren looked toward it. “Wait. Why the fuck did I set this to the garrison?”

I laughed, carefully. “Muscle memory?”

“Terrible habit.” She lowered her hands reluctantly enough that I noticed. “Pull over somewhere safe.”

The car checked the lane, signaled, and guided itself into a marked stopping bay. Only after it confirmed the stop did she turn back to the map.

“It’s Friday,” she said. “We don’t have to finish the evening there.”

“No argument from me.”

“How far’s yours?”

“Eight miles from here, apparently.” I leaned toward the display, then thought better of it. “We could go to mine. Order food.”

“We should definitely order food.”

“You look very committed.”

“I’ve earned dinner.”

“You have.”

She tapped the map, then hesitated. “Mine’s only four.”

I turned toward her. Her hand rested on the edge of the console.

“We could go there,” she said. “If you want. I’ve got food. Well. Places that deliver food. Somewhere comfortable to sit.”

“You’re selling this very well.”

“I was doing better before I started talking.”

I smiled. She looked at me then, and the uncertainty in her face made my chest ache somewhere that hadn’t been hit.

“I want to,” I said.

Her mouth softened. “Yeah?”

“Yeah. I’d like that.”

She selected her address, still smiling.

“Resume autonomous route.”

“Supported route confirmed. Autonomous control active.”

The car pulled out, and she settled back beside me.

“Quieter at mine.”

“Low bar.”

“No broken bottles. No one giving us shit.”

“Sounds suspiciously pleasant.”

“I do pleasant.”

“I noticed.”

She glanced over, visibly pleased.

“And nobody choking you.”

“Well, I wouldn’t have minded so much if it was—”

I stopped.

Voeren turned her head slowly. “If it was?”

“Different.”

“Different how?”

“The circumstances.”

Her eyebrows lifted. I looked at the windshield.

*Oh my God.*

“Spade.”

“You know what I need?”

Her mouth twitched.

“Another shot,” I said, reaching toward the bottle between us.

She caught it first. “Me first.”

She winked.

I stared at her while she unscrewed the cap.

*She knows. She absolutely— No. Maybe she doesn’t. Maybe she’s just like this.*

She took a drink, lowered the bottle, and watched me over it.

“We can leave that sentence unfinished tonight,” she said.

“That would be very kind.”

“Another time, if you want.”

I looked at her. She was still smiling, but she waited for the answer.

“Yeah,” I said, quieter. “Another time.”

“All right.”

She offered the bottle. I reached for it, and she drew it back just far enough to make me look up.

“Oh, come on.”

“Want me to?”

I looked from the bottle to her face.

Her grin spread. “Open up. Gotta make sure you don’t get too drunk.”

“That ship’s gone.”

“Could be coming back. Heard things about the tides.”

I laughed before I could help it. “You listened.”

“Been doing it all evening.”

That stopped me more effectively than the teasing.

Then I leaned closer. “Fine. But I can take more than you think.”

Her hand paused for the smallest moment.

I noticed.

*Oh.*

Her eyes returned to mine, and now both of us were trying not to smile too much.

“A little?” she asked.

“Maybe more than that.”

I opened my mouth. She lifted the bottle slowly, watching the angle. Her other hand hovered near my chin without touching it. A small measure reached my tongue, and she tipped the bottle back before I needed to hurry.

The tequila burned. So did my throat.

I swallowed, shut my eyes, and let out a breath through my nose.

“Fuck. Good tequila. Terrible timing.”

“Your throat?”

“Yeah.”

She lowered the bottle. “I should’ve thought of that.”

“So should I.”

“That doesn’t make either of us clever.”

“No.”

She capped it. I wiped a drop from the uninjured corner of my mouth, then caught her watching.

“What?”

She looked up at my eyes a fraction too late. “Nothing.”

I smiled. “Actual nothing?”

Her laugh was soft. She put the bottle in the rear door pocket.

“Water when we get in.”

“And food.”

“And food.”

“And maybe a long period where I don’t have to move.”

“Can arrange that.”

“Excellent hospitality.”

“You haven’t seen the place.”

“I’ve seen the host.”

She looked toward the windshield, smiling in a way she seemed unable to do anything about. For a moment I just watched her.

Then she glanced back. “You’re getting very good at that.”

“What?”

“Making it difficult for me to think.”

My face heated. “Oh.”

“Yeah. Oh.”

We looked at each other and started laughing. It hurt. I pressed a hand carefully against my ribs, and she waited until I could breathe comfortably again before shaking her head.

“Going to have to stop being funny.”

“Can’t ask that of me. It’s all I’ve got.”

“Not even close.”

I looked down at my hands. She didn’t make me answer.

---

We spent the next part of the drive arguing about food. I said I didn’t mind. She asked whether that meant I genuinely didn’t mind or was trying to be easy company.

I objected to the accuracy of the question.

“You’re allowed to want dinner,” she said.

“I do want dinner.”

“Specific dinner.”

“Demanding.”

“I’ve been warned.”

I gave her an answer. She grinned, then ordered enough for both of us and added something she wanted without pretending it was for my benefit.

“Anything else?”

“Water.”

“Got that covered.”

“And I want to hear you play.”

Her hand stopped over the console. I looked at her.

“Not tonight,” I added. “I don’t think I’m surviving a drum kit tonight.”

She smiled. “Still interested?”

“Yes.”

The answer was easy now. I let her hear that.

“And I still want you to hear me,” I said. “When my hands stop being dramatic.”

Her eyes dropped to them. “I can wait.”

“You’d better. Apparently I’ve got a list of demonstrations to get through.”

Her grin returned. “Looking forward to it.”

The car turned onto a quieter street. I settled back carefully, moving the sheath clear of the seat again. My knee had stiffened. My back ached in layers, old pain beneath new. The injector hadn’t erased any of it, and the tequila hadn’t made me less aware of the blood drying on my clothes.

Beside me, Voeren stretched her fingers once and rested her hand between us. I turned mine palm-up. She looked down, then settled hers against it, keeping clear of the torn skin.

Our fingers found a comfortable place slowly.

We didn’t talk for the last part of the drive. The car slowed, signaled, and turned in.

“Destination reached,” the system said. “Vehicle secured.”

Voeren looked toward the house, then back at me.

“Here we are.”

A porch light warmed a small patch of the dark beyond her window. I had spent the first drive wondering whether she really wanted me there. Now her hand was in mine, her jacket was behind us with my ridiculous little city in its pocket, and she was waiting to take me inside.

I squeezed her fingers as gently as I could.

“Yeah,” I said. “Here we are.”
