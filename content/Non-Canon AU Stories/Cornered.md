# Cornered

>[!note] Character pronouns
>Bloof uses they/them, and Seris uses she/her

> [!note] The Crown Eyes
> The Crown Eyes are canon, but they have not yet appeared in the mainline story. They are an ancient draconic sensory trait, not prophecy or a Calamity. They let a bearer sense heat, magical energy, and the tension in a body just before it moves, so danger shows up before it takes shape.
>
> Bloof's are a second, upper pair of red eyes with white pupils. They usually stay closed and open when Bloof is in danger, shocked, or under severe stress. In this AU, they also open on their own under strong emotion.

The music was the first problem. The second problem was the number of beastfolk who wanted to shake Bloof's hand.

Bloof stood just inside the doorway of the rented hall, a lean dragon-goat in baggy techwear cargos and a black compression tank, silver claws jammed into pockets that suddenly didn't feel deep enough. The door had barely swung shut behind them, and already the bass had found their ribs. Strobes sliced the dark into pieces. Light slid across their swept-back horns and the black-and-red dreadlocks tied neatly at their nape, caught the ring in their septum, then skated down bare arms where every old scar stood out in pale relief. Their tail was clamped against the back of one leg, its tip ticking like a metronome set to panic.

_Five minutes,_ they had promised themself on the walk over. _Be seen, then leave._

That was the plan. It was a good plan. It was just the plan that had nothing to do with why their pulse had been running too fast since the elevator.

_Is she here yet?_

Bloof shoved the thought down before it could finish. _Doesn't matter._ Their eyes went to the nearest flash of white across the room anyway, a coat, a sleeve, a tail of pale fabric. A stranger. Their stomach dropped, then, to their annoyance, lurched in something that felt a lot like disappointment.

_Stop it._

"Bloovrn!" A wolfish guy seized their hand and pumped it hard enough to rattle their wrist. His breath smelled like citrus and cheap liquor, and his grin was too wide for a stranger's. "Seris talks about you all the time!"

Everything in Bloof went still.

"Does she," they said flatly. Their ears flicked back before they could stop them. The words came out a half-beat too slow, and they hated that, hated the heat that crept up the inside of their collar. "That's... concerning."

_What does that mean? Talks about me how? Why would she..._

_Nope._ A mental door slammed. _Not here. Not now._

The wolf was already talking, something about a friend of a friend, something about a rooftop party. Bloof nodded in the right places and heard none of it. Every conversation after that felt like a puzzle with the instructions missing. Someone asked what they studied, and Bloof managed a few honest sentences about magi-tech hardware before three people started talking over each other and a fourth leaned in close to shout something at their ear. Warm bodies pressed from every side. The air had thickened into a perfumed fog of fur and spilled liquor and sweat, and each breath tasted of other people's.

The bass climbed until Bloof felt it in their back teeth. Light smeared into color, voices stacked into noise, and behind their closed upper lids the Crown Eyes pushed like something leaning on a door. A thin, familiar pressure began to build behind Bloof's skull, the one that meant too much, too fast, too many.

And underneath all of it, in the small quiet place they refused to look at, one thought kept scratching.

_She's going to walk in any second._

Their hands had started to shake. They curled their claws into their palms until the points bit.

_Not a problem. This is not a problem. You've fought things with teeth the size of your torso. You can survive a party. You can survive..._

They couldn't finish that sentence either.

So they found the bar.

The first drink was to steady their hands. It burned going down, a hot line from tongue to stomach, and for a moment the noise took half a step back. The second was because it worked. By the third, the room had gone soft at the edges, the glare of the lights melting into something almost kind, and their tail had loosened enough to sway behind them. The scratching thought got quieter. Not gone, but wrapped in cotton.

The fourth? Well, the fourth was just for fun.

They ended up against a wall near the back, nursing the last of it and watching the crowd move like water. The plaster was cool against their shoulder blades, a small, solid thing to lean on. The noise had dulled to a hum. Their heartbeat, finally, had slowed to something that felt almost normal.

Almost. Their eyes still drifted to the door every few seconds, and they did not bother to pretend they weren't.

_Ugh. This is Seris's fault. I swear when I see her..._ they thought, with something that wasn't quite resentment. _"Alright. Maybe just one hour, Bloof. You'll survive."_

---

Seris was not having the same night.

She moved through the party like it had been built around her. The long white tails of her coat swept behind her, the pearls at her throat caught every flash of light, and her purple tail rode lazily at her heels, the cyan markings along it shimmering like heat on water. Every few steps someone reached for her attention, and she gave each of them exactly as much as she meant to.

Tonight the amber in her glass was already half gone, and the smile that usually took effort came to her like breathing. Even her tail, normally so disciplined, had started to sway with the music.

She found Bloof by tracking the one place in the room where the crowd thinned out. They were against the far wall, tipping back the last of a drink, ears drooping, tail swaying in a slow, unguarded arc.

Seris stopped a few steps away and let her smile widen until the fangs showed.

"Well, well," she said, low and smooth as poured silk. "The *famous* Bloof Graymark. Hiding by the wall like a cornered cat."

Bloof lowered the glass and looked up at her. The lids of their upper eyes twitched, but stayed shut. "Dragon-goat, technically," they said. "And I'm not hiding. I'm observing from a defensible position."

"Mm. Defending yourself from what? Fun?" She came closer and plucked the empty glass from their fingers to sniff it, one dark claw tapping the rim. "Three drinks. Maybe four. You're swaying, darling."

"I'm _standing,_" Bloof said, with tremendous dignity. Their tail thumped once against the wall. "You peer-pressured me into a room full of strangers. I'm allowed to be a little drunk."

"A little?" Her eyes glittered. "I've watched you take a hit from something three times your size without blinking, and a crowded party does this to you."

"Crowds are worse than hits." A small, crooked smile tugged at Bloof's mouth. "Hits, I understand."

Seris laughed, a low and genuine sound, and her tail curled in a slow, pleased loop behind her.

"Come on," she said, tipping her head toward a side door. "You've been brave enough for one night. Want a break from all the fur and noise? I have a room upstairs."

Bloof's tail went still for half a second. Then it swung out behind them, wide and unmistakably relieved.

"ABSOLUTELY," they said, much too quickly. Then, clearing their throat: "Ahem. Yes. Please."

---

The door clicked, and the bass cut off like a hand over a mouth.

The suite beyond was all dim amber lamps and floor-to-ceiling glass, the city scattered beneath it like spilled sparks. A bed wide enough for four sat against the far wall as if it had been waiting. The party shrank to a dull heartbeat through the floor, and the quiet that settled in felt like a held breath.

Bloof exhaled all at once. Their shoulders dropped, their ears came up, and for the first time in an hour they breathed all the way down. Their tail unwound from their leg and swayed behind them, loose and easy.

"There it is," Seris murmured. She poured two drinks from a bottle on the sideboard, and before she handed one over she tilted hers toward the lamp and studied it, a habit so old she did it without thinking. "You look like a different person when no one's watching."

"I'm not different. Just less overwhelmed." Bloof took the glass and sank onto the arm of the couch, close to the wall. The leather creaked under them. "Thanks. Really. Even if you did drag me here."

"I _invited_ you."

"You sent me eleven messages."

"Persuasive invitations."

"One of them was just a photo of your face," Bloof said, side-eying her. "Smirking."

"It worked, didn't it?" Seris leaned against the sideboard, and her tail gave a lazy, smug flick. "You're here."

"I'm here," Bloof agreed, letting out an audible sigh. The alcohol had warmed their voice, and they let the smile through. "I'd be mad, but you've got the better snacks and the quieter room. Suspiciously well-planned, honestly. Did you plan this? Leave me out there to melt, then rescue me with a suite?"

Her eyebrow arched. "You think I'm that devious?"

"I think you're exactly that devious." Bloof sipped, watching her over the rim. "You walked up to me like a predator that already had the prey cornered. Very dramatic. Great coat, by the way. Very 'villain in the second act.'"

For half a second Seris's composure slipped, and her tail jerked in surprise. Then the smile came back, slower and warmer.

"Careful," she said softly. "Flattery will get you everywhere."

"I said _villain._"

"I heard 'great coat.'"

Bloof laughed, a real one that shook their shoulders and bared their fangs. Seris watched with an expression she kept carefully light, and her tail slid slowly along the floor toward them.

---

Time slipped by in easy pieces. They traded dumb stories, and she teased them about their cargo pants. They fired back that her tail had been tapping her ankle the whole time she called herself composed. She went still and looked down. Bloof grinned in triumph.

"Lucky guess," she said.

"See, I notice things too," Bloof said. "You just don't think I do."

She tilted her head, and for a moment the playful look turned thoughtful. Then she set down her drink and wandered over, close enough that the tip of her tail brushed the couch.

"It's a wonder, honestly," Seris said, "that no one's snapped you up. Look at you. Smart. Dangerous. Those _arms._ And you're still single."

The words landed somewhere unexpected. Bloof's ears twitched, and their tail stiffened and curled in on itself.

"Ha. Yeah. Well." They stood up a little too quickly and ended up with their back against the wall. "Not really the priority. School, work, jobs, my mom. You know."

"I know what you _say._" She took a step. "That's not the same as a reason."

"It's four good reasons, actually." They scratched at the back of their neck, avoiding her eyes. "Can we talk about something else? Like how you were so sure you could get me to come tonight?"

"Hm." Another step. Her tail slid behind her, slow and curious. "No."

Bloof's ears pinned halfway back. "Seris."

"You went quiet. Your tail did that thing." She smiled, small and sharp. "Interesting."

"It's _not_ interesting." Bloof set their glass on a nearby table with exaggerated care, as if that would restore their dignity. "I'm going back to the party. People are probably wondering where we went."

"Of course," Seris said sweetly, though she didn't move an inch.

Bloof tried to leave. They got two steps, and then a hand slapped flat against the wall beside their head. The whole world narrowed to the whisper of her coat tails settling, the scent of something faintly floral and sharp, and the cool weight of her tail curling around Bloof's ankle.

Bloof's breathing stopped.

Seris stood close, six feet and two inches of lean, easy confidence pinning them against the plaster. She touched them with nothing but that one hand, and the grin on her face was entirely cocky.

"You really want to go back out there?" she asked, though both of them knew the answer. The question slid in like a pin through a moth, polite and precise.

Heat flooded up Bloof's throat in a rush they could feel in the tips of their ears. Their tail snapped straight as a drawn wire, scales ridged along its length, the tip trembling. The Crown Eyes strained hard against the lids, wanting to open, and Bloof clamped down on them. Their face went carefully, stubbornly blank. Seris saw all of it anyway.

"What," Bloof said evenly, "are you doing?"

"Hmm." She leaned in until her breath brushed the base of their horn. "I'm answering your question. The one you didn't ask."

"I asked a _different_ one."

"You know, you're not very good at stoicism when you're drunk." One clawed fingertip trailed down the dark line of their forearm, over the old scars, light as a feather and twice as unhurried. "Your ears give you away. And this." Her tail tugged, ever so gently, at their ankle, and Bloof's tail flailed once in pure betrayal. "Every part of you is telling me something very different from your face."

"That's... a lot of confidence for someone who just got stared down by a dragon." Bloof's eyes narrowed and locked onto hers.

"Oh, darling." She laughed under her breath, the sound dropping lower. "I've been wanting to see you try that voice on me. Say it again."

Bloof's jaw worked. "I'm not saying anything."

"Mm. That's too bad." Her thumb traced the edge of the tank's short sleeve, just below the shoulder. "I had a few things I wanted to say to you. Things I've been holding back all night, in a room full of beastfolk."

"Seris..."

"Do you want to hear them?" Her voice dipped, velvet and wicked. "Or do you want to go back downstairs and pretend you didn't just stop breathing?"

Bloof opened their mouth. Nothing came out. Their tail curled halfway round hers, the way a person might grab a rope, and neither of them said a word about it.

---

Neither of them spoke.

The party thudded faintly through the floor, and the only sound in the suite was Bloof's breathing, gone shallow and much too deliberate. Seris held her pose, one hand braced beside their horn and her tail still looped around their ankle. She watched them with the patience of someone who had already won and wanted to see how long the other side would take to notice.

Bloof decided not to notice.

They shifted their weight with a slow tilt of the shoulder and pressed their free hand flat against her collarbone. It wasn't a shove, just a firm pressure that said _back up._ Their claws rested on the white of her coat, silver against silk. If they could turn the angle, they could slip out sideways and reclaim the space.

Seris let the hand sit there for a heartbeat. Then she turned her head and brushed her lips against the inside of their wrist, a ghost of contact, fangs just barely showing.

Bloof's fingers twitched, and the pressure faltered.

_Okay,_ they thought, with the sharp clarity of four drinks. _Adjust._

They dropped their other hand to catch her hip, planning to hook a leg behind hers and pivot. It was a clean, practiced move, the kind they'd used in sparring on people twice Seris's weight. Their tail even came off the floor to counterbalance.

Seris's tail tightened around their ankle in one slow, deliberate squeeze, and Bloof's weight went nowhere. Her hand slid from the wall to catch their wrist and press it gently, firmly, back against the plaster above their head. She used no force at all. It was just _exactly enough,_ as if she had measured it.

Bloof tried again with the other hand. She caught that one and pinned it too.

_Oh._

Their ears slicked back and their breath hitched. Their tail whipped against the floor, loud in the silence, and neither of them looked down at it. The Crown Eyes strained against the lids, and for half a second a thin seam of red light showed beneath the left one before Bloof forced it shut.

Seris saw it. Her smile unfolded like a blade leaving its sheath.

_She planned this,_ Bloof realized. _The couch, the wall, the "wonder you're single." All of it._

She still hadn't said a word. She let them stand there, pinned, with their pulse hammering against her fingertips and the heat climbing past their jaw, over the star on their forehead, to the tips of their ears. There was nothing left to hide behind. The flush ran bright under the grey of their scales, vivid even in the low amber light.

Seris's gaze dropped to it and lingered, almost hungry.

Her head dipped, slowly, and she turned her face toward the side of their neck. She stopped a finger's width from the skin. The warmth of her breath rolled down the curve of it, long and deliberate.

Bloof's whole body jolted. Their tail locked rigid, then curled inward as if it wanted to hide.

She breathed out again, even slower, and the corner of her mouth curved against the edge of their jaw.

Then she opened her mouth and bit their ear.

It was gentle, just the point of a fang and the press of her lips against soft fur and the cold bar of the piercing. A low sound escaped Bloof before they could stop it, small and startled and unmistakably not a word.

Seris went still against them, and her tail gave a slow, delighted flick.

---

"Shut up," Bloof said hoarsely, though neither of them had said anything.

Seris pulled back just far enough to meet their eyes. Hers were cyan and gleaming, full of dry amusement. "I haven't said a word, darling."

"You were thinking it."

"I was thinking," she said, silky, "that you made a very interesting noise."

"I didn't." Bloof's voice cracked on the second word. They cleared their throat, and their ears flattened in sheer embarrassment. "That was a... hiccup. Alcohol-related."

"Ah. _Alcohol-related._" Her thumb stroked slowly over the inside of their wrist. "Of course."

"Don't do the voice."

"What voice?"

"The one you're doing." Bloof tried to shrug one shoulder, a casual gesture ruined entirely by the fact that they were still pinned. "The villain-monologue one. 'Oh, I've been planning this for weeks.' I'm not impressed."

"Aren't you?" Her tail slid higher in a lazy coil around their calf. "Your tail seems quite impressed."

"My tail is drunk."

"Your tail is _honest._" She tilted her head, the smile razor-thin. "That's the difference between you and it."

"I'm honest!" Bloof said, a little too loudly. "I'm extremely honest. I'm the most honest person you know."

"You told me you weren't hiding by that wall."

"I was observing."

"With your tail around your leg."

"Tactically."

Seris laughed against their cheek, low and warm, and the sound ran through Bloof like a current. They squeezed their eyes shut, which only made their trapped hands feel more trapped. Then they dragged them open again and glared at her with all the stoicism they had left.

"This is a power play," they said. "I see what you're doing. You think because I'm a little off-balance—"

"A little."

"—a little off-balance, I'll just fold. But I've been in worse spots than this. I've been shot at. I've been—"

She leaned in and licked their neck.

Bloof's sentence ended in a soft, strangled noise.

"You were saying?" Seris murmured, lips so close to their pulse that they could feel her smile.

"I was saying," Bloof managed, "that you're, um, very. Confident."

"And you're very flustered."

"I'm composed."

"You're red to the horns, darling."

"I'm _warm._ The room is warm. It's genetic."

"Oh, absolutely," she agreed gravely. "I'll have it looked at." Her thumb pressed the pulse point of their wrist, and she felt it jump. "After."

"After what?"

She tilted her head, studying them with open, wicked interest, like a predator who had finally cornered its prey.

"You tell me."

Bloof opened their mouth to snap back, but nothing sharp came to mind. Their tail betrayed them again, sliding slowly, helplessly, around hers.

"...You're insufferable," they muttered.

"You're adorable."

"I'm not adorable. I could set this room on fire."

"You could." Her grin widened, all fang. "You won't."

"I _could._"

"Then do it, Bloof." She leaned in until her nose brushed theirs, and her voice dropped to almost nothing. "Make me stop."

Neither of them moved. The room was very, very quiet.

---

"I'm... recalculating," Bloof said, a little hoarse.

"You keep not answering." Seris released their wrists, and her hands slid down their arms, her thumb tracing the edge of their sleeve, over the bare curve of their shoulder, and settling at the hinge of their jaw where the scales gave way to soft fur. "And yet you haven't left."

"The wall's in the way," Bloof said. The red in their face still gave them away, held back only by willpower and spite, and contested by embarrassment and liquor.

"The wall isn't doing anything. _I'm_ the one in the way." Her smile sharpened. "Push me, then. You could. We both know it."

Bloof's eyes dropped to her mouth and rose again, too slowly to pass for casual. Their tail tightened around hers and held on.

"...Annoying," they muttered.

"Mm?"

"You're _really_ annoying, you know that?" Their voice cracked on the last word, and they hated it. "You do this on purpose. You always do this on purpose. Gorgeous, smug, and sitting in my head rent-free for months, and you just..."

They stopped. Seris had gone completely still.

"Gorgeous," she repeated softly.

Bloof's ears flattened. "I didn't say that."

"You did."

"It was the alcohol."

"It was _absolutely_ the alcohol, and I'm going to remember it forever." But her tail gave a quick, embarrassed little flick against the floor, and Bloof caught it. The grin came back, warm and crooked.

"You're blushing," they said. "Under the purple. It's faint, but it's there."

"I do not blush."

"Your ears... well, you don't have ears. Your neck. Your neck is doing it." They lifted a hand and ran one silver claw, very gently, along the glowing cyan markings at her throat. Seris's breath caught audibly. "Oh, that's _really_ good. I'm keeping that."

"Bloof." A warning, but her voice came out too low to carry it.

"Say please."

Her eyes narrowed. Then she laughed under her breath, dangerous and delighted, and pinned their wrists to the wall again.

"Oh, you _brat,_" she murmured. "You're going to regret that."

"Doubt it."

She kissed them.

It wasn't gentle. Months of teasing and near-misses went into it, and Bloof made a startled sound against her mouth before their hands fisted in the white lapels of her coat and hauled her closer. Fangs, heat, the taste of whatever she'd been drinking, sweet and smoky. Her tail wound up their leg, past the cargo pocket and the knee, tight enough to make their breath stutter. Their own tail lashed once against the wall, then wrapped around her waist as if it had always belonged there.

When they broke apart, both of them were breathing hard.

"That," Bloof managed, "was... not nothing."

"No." Seris's voice had lost most of its polish. "It wasn't."

"Bed?" Bloof said, and then, appalled, "Gods, I said that out loud."

"You did." Her grin turned wolfish. "Good instincts."

---

They didn't make it gracefully.

Seris walked them backward with a hand at their hip, and Bloof walked her back, and somewhere along the way they swapped places twice. Her coat ended up half off one shoulder. The pearls at her throat swung against Bloof's cheek when they kissed her neck, cool and smooth against their skin, and she hissed through her fangs and arched into it before she could stop herself.

"Don't," she said.

"Don't what?" Bloof's mouth moved lower, pressing against the pulse beneath the glowing scales. "Don't do _this?_"

"Ah..." Her claws dug into their back. "Bloof..."

"That's the second time you've said my name like that." They looked up, flushed and smug and wrecked. "I'm keeping that one too."

Seris's answer was to shove them back onto the enormous bed, and she followed. The mattress swallowed them both in a rush of cool sheets. She straddled their hips and loomed over them with her hair falling loose around her horns, looking down at them with an expression halfway between hunger and fondness.

"You think you're winning," she said.

"I _know_ I'm winning." But their hands had gone to her hips, and their voice was hardly steady. "Your tail's shaking."

"That's anticipation."

"Is that what we're calling it?"

She bent and caught their bottom lip between her teeth, carefully, and Bloof's whole body shivered. Her hands slid beneath the hem of the compression tank, cool claws against hot scales, and pushed it up. Air touched their stomach, and the muscle there jumped. Her gaze tracked every old scar as if it were something worth keeping.

"Still with me?" she asked, and for a moment the tease was gone and she meant the question.

"Yes." Bloof's tail curled around her thigh, and they didn't look away. "Very. Don't stop."

Something eased in her face. Then it burned again.

They rolled. Bloof ended up on top, pinning her wrists above her head, grinning like they'd won a war. Their breath came hot against her ear.

"Not so composed now, are you?"

"Give me a minute," Seris panted, "and I'll fix that."

"Mm. I'll give you thirty seconds."

They kissed her again, and this time they made it last: a slow press, a retreat just out of her reach, the faint scrape of fang when she chased them. Their hips shifted against hers in a way that wrenched a sharp, helpless sound from her throat. Her tail slammed against the sheets. It was the first time all night she'd lost the thread of what she wanted to say, and Bloof took a ridiculous amount of glee in it, nosing at the soft place under her jaw while she swore at them in a language they didn't recognize.

"Oh. Now _that's_ new," Bloof murmured against her skin. "What does that one mean?"

"It means," she gasped, "that you are enjoying this too much."

"Oh, absolutely I am."

Bloof gathered both of her wrists in one hand, silver claws careful against her pulse, and let the other trail down. It followed the line of her throat, over the pearls, and across the glowing cyan markings that ran down her neck to the open collar of her shirt. The scales brightened wherever they touched, a pale blue flare rising under each fingertip. Bloof watched it happen with open fascination.

"These light up," they said. "When I do this."

"They do _not._"

"They do. See?" They dragged a claw slowly down the center of her chest, and the light followed it like a bead of water. Seris's back bowed off the mattress. "Oh, that's _lovely._"

"Bloof." Her voice was a warning, but it had no teeth left. "Stop narrating."

"Nope." Their smile turned devilish, reveling in the moment.

"I mean it."

"You don't." They bent and pressed their mouth to the hollow of her throat, right where the glow pooled brightest, and felt the vibration of the sound she tried to swallow. "You could've broken my grip ten seconds ago. You've got claws. You've got poison. You're a professional."

"I'm thinking."

"Mm-hm. About what?"

"About how I'm going to..." Her breath hitched as Bloof's teeth grazed the edge of her collarbone. Her tail curled helplessly, then straightened, then curled again. "About how I'm going to make you _pay_ for this."

"I'm shaking."

"You're _smiling._"

"Because you've been trying to get out of this for a minute and a half," Bloof said, delighted, "and you haven't moved."

Seris opened her mouth. Nothing useful came out of it.

Bloof's grin widened. They let go of her wrists and watched. Her hands stayed exactly where they were, above her head, curled into the sheets. A fine tremor ran through her fingers, and her glowing eyes stayed fixed on Bloof's face.

"Huh," Bloof said softly. "Look at that. You're just _letting_ me."

"I'm evaluating," she managed.

"Is that what it's called."

They worked down her slowly, learning her. Their fingers undid the buttons of her shirt one at a time, and each time her breath stuttered, they paused and made her wait. Her necklace slipped to the side. Bloof kissed the place where it had been, then the line of her ribs, then the flat of her stomach, humming low against her skin the entire time. Every few seconds they glanced up to check her face. Each time, she looked away, and each time her tail gave her away, lashing the sheets or tightening around Bloof's calf as though it had decided on its own.

"You're going very quiet," Bloof observed, breath warm across her stomach.

"I'm concentrating."

"On what?"

"On not saying anything I'll regret."

"Oh." The smile in their voice was unbearable. "Now I _want_ to hear it."

Her hips jerked when their mouth moved lower. She said a word that was almost their name, and a lot of other words that weren't any language Bloof knew. One hand left the sheets and tangled in the soft fur at the back of their neck, pressing at the base of a horn. Bloof made a pleased sound at that and didn't stop.

By the time Bloof drew back to look at her, Seris was flushed to the tips of her horns, hair wild, lips parted, and the cyan in her eyes had gone almost white. She stared up at the ceiling and tried to find her composure somewhere in it.

"This isn't," she said, in a voice with no composure left in it, "what I planned."

"No?"

"I was going to be in _charge._"

"You were." Bloof crawled back up her body, slow and smug, until their forehead rested against hers. Their tail swayed behind them, loose and gleeful. "For about an hour. I let you."

"You did not _let..._" Seris stopped. Her tail tightened around Bloof's ankle as if it were afraid they'd leave. She looked at them for a long moment, and something in her eyes shifted from helpless to dangerous. "...You are going to regret that."

"I really doubt it," Bloof whispered, and kissed her again.

---

She turned it around with a single move.

One second Bloof was winning. The next, Seris had lifted her hands from the sheets, flipped them, and pinned them face-down against the mattress with a knee settled between theirs and her weight behind it. A clawed hand pulled their tank top high, baring the long line of their back.

"Seris..."

"Shh." Her voice had dropped back into that smooth, low register, with a smile in it Bloof could hear. "You had your turn."

Her fingertips found the ridge of scales along their spine and traced down it, slow as a held breath. Bloof's whole body arched. A shudder rolled down to the base of their tail, which lashed wildly, once, then went rigid.

"There," Seris whispered. "That's one."

Bloof tried to answer. It escaped as a tight, breathless noise.

She leaned down and kissed the back of their neck, right at the base of one swept-back horn. Then she did something else. She raked a claw slowly up the line of their spine, rubbed her thumb along the thick ridge where the horn met skull, and pressed.

The sound that came out of Bloof was nothing like their usual voice. It started low in their chest, a deep, helpless rumble like a furnace banking, and went on and on. Their eyes fluttered shut. Their ears fell soft and flat. Their tail went loose and happy against her leg, and Bloof couldn't have told you whether they were still breathing.

Seris froze, then smiled, slow and entirely satisfied.

"Ohhhhh," she murmured. "_There_ it is."

"Don't," Bloof tried. It came out as a purr. "Don't... stop..."

"I wasn't planning to."

She took her time. Her claws traced the scales down their spine one at a time, light as dust settling, and every inch of Bloof's back arched into her touch. Where the scales ran thick along their shoulders she pressed her mouth, and the heat of their skin warmed her lips. She felt the rumble deepen beneath her, a steady vibration she could feel in her own ribs.

"Listen to you," she whispered against the nape of their neck. "All that stoic, and it takes _this_ to get an honest answer."

"Shut... up," Bloof breathed. It was the weakest threat of their life.

"Make me."

They couldn't. They tried to push up onto their elbows and gave up halfway, trembling, when her hand slid around to splay across their stomach and pull them back against her. The pressure was firm and warm. Her other hand never left the base of their horn, circling in slow, patient strokes that dragged the purr out of them again and again. Their tail wrapped around her wrist and clung there.

She kissed down the line of their spine, each kiss paired with a drag of fang, never quite a bite. The scales on their back glowed faintly ember-red wherever her mouth had been. Bloof's breath came ragged, and their fingers fisted in the sheets until the fabric creaked.

"Seris..."

"Mm?"

"I can't... think."

"Good." A kiss between their shoulder blades. "That's the idea."

Her hand slid lower, unhurried, over the curve of their hip, down the scaled outside of one thigh, and back up the inside. She drew a slow, deliberate pattern with one claw, and every time Bloof's breath caught, she stopped, held there, and watched. She was merciless about it. The third time she paused, Bloof made a sound that was half laugh, half sob.

"You're _cruel,_" they gasped.

"I'm thorough." Her lips brushed the shell of their ear, the cool metal of the piercing against her mouth. "Tell me how it feels."

"Hot." The word was barely a breath. "You're... everything's... _hot._"

"Good boy," she said, then caught herself, and her tail flicked in a quick, embarrassed twitch. "...Good _Bloof._ Whatever you are."

"Good Bloof," they repeated, dazed, and the purr rose again, so loud it shook the pillow.

Seris laughed softly into the fur at the back of their neck. It had no mockery in it at all.

She turned them over. Bloof went easily, boneless, and lay there with flushed cheeks and kiss-bruised lips, tail trailing across the sheets. Their lower eyes were hazy and half-lidded. The upper lids twitched, and for a heartbeat a thin line of crimson showed beneath them before Bloof shuddered and let them fall closed again.

Seris saw it. Something fierce and tender went through her face.

"There you are," she whispered, framing their jaw with both hands. "No more fighting me."

"Thought I was winning."

"You were." She pressed a kiss to the star on their forehead, then to the red crescent under each eye. "For a while."

She settled over them, flushed and disheveled, lips swollen, her own breath uneven. She cupped their cheek.

"Seris," they whispered at last, raw and unguarded. "Please."

Her voice was softer than it had ever been. "Tell me what you want."

They told her. It wasn't elegant. There was a lot of her name in it, and none of the banter was left.

She worked her way down them, slowly, with her mouth and claws and the heavy curve of her tail twined around their ankle. Every sound she pulled from them was one nobody else had ever heard. When Bloof's hands came up to tangle in her hair, she let them. When their hips began to move and their voice came apart into her name, she held them through it, a steady hand at their waist and her mouth never leaving them. Their purr built higher and higher, shaking through their chest, until it broke on one long, shuddering breath.

Her tail tightened. Their tail tightened. For a long moment, neither of them could have said whose was whose.

By the end, the last thing in the room that resembled composure was her tail, wrapped around their waist and holding on like it never meant to let go.

---

Later, the room was quiet. The bass downstairs had faded to a distant pulse, and the city glittered patiently beyond the glass.

Bloof lay sprawled on their stomach with their cheek in the pillow, fur mussed, one horn smudged with a faint cyan glow where her fingertips had been. Their tail was draped loosely over her ankle, and the rumble in their chest hadn't quite died, just dropped to a contented hum. The upper eyes stayed closed. For once they'd had nothing to watch for.

Seris lay propped on one elbow beside them, coat discarded, necklace askew, tracing slow patterns along the scales of their shoulder. Her tail coiled around theirs. She hadn't stopped smiling.

"Well," she said, voice lazy. "Shall we get back to the party?"

Bloof's whole body went rigid.

Their eyes snapped open. The hum cut out. Their tail slapped the mattress, hard, and they shoved themself halfway up on one elbow, staring at her with wide, panicked eyes and ears standing straight up.

"The... _what?_ Now? We're... I'm not... my _shirt..._" They looked down at themself, then around the room for pants, then at her again. "Seris, I can't go back down there looking like _this._"

Seris let it hang for exactly three seconds. Then she buried her face in the pillow and shook with laughter.

"You," she wheezed, "should see your _face._"

Bloof stared. Realization crept in. Their ears flattened, their tail whipped, and heat rose all the way to the tips of their horns.

"You absolute _snake._"

"Viper, technically." She wiped her eyes, still grinning. Then she reached over and tugged them back down against her side, tucking their head beneath her chin. Her tail wrapped around theirs again. "I'm not letting you leave for a very long time, darling. The party can survive without us."

Bloof grumbled something into her collarbone. Their tail, traitorous to the last, settled warm and boneless over hers.

The rumble started again, low and steady, and neither of them mentioned it.
