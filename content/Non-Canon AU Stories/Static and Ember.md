> [!note] The Crown Eyes
> The Crown Eyes are canon, but they have not yet appeared in the mainline story. They are an ancient draconic sensory trait, not prophecy or a Calamity. They let a bearer sense heat, magical energy, and the tension in a body just before it moves, so danger shows up before it takes shape.
>
> Bloof's are a second, upper pair of red eyes with white pupils. They usually stay closed and open when Bloof is in danger, shocked, or under severe stress. In this AU, they also open on their own under strong emotion. Bloof has long felt ashamed of them, because most people see two sets of eyes as a mutation. Bazemir recognizes them as something much older.

# Static and Ember

The door was shut, the lights were off, and Bloof had not moved in a long time.

They sat on the floor at the foot of the bed with their back against the frame, knees pulled up, tail wound around them with the tip tucked under one arm like something they were trying to keep from escaping. Their jacket was still on. The chains on the pants were still clipped where they belonged. Taking anything off would have meant admitting the mission was over.

*Monster.*

The enemy had said it with blood on their teeth. They had been strong, stronger than the report claimed, and they had not begged or bargained. They had looked at Bloof, at the red light still bleeding out of their scales, and said it as if it were a fact they'd always known.

*YOU TOOK HER FROM ME. YOU'RE A FUCKING MONSTER!*

Bloof hadn't known who "her" was, not at first. Then the memory had arrived, because it always did. It was the night they went berserk, the night Wrath had taken the wheel and Bloof had let it. There had been a house, a doorway, and someone in it.

The cultist's death had been justified, and Bloof knew it. They would have killed three more people before the night ended. Bloof had run the numbers over and over, and the numbers were fine. But the numbers couldn't take away how they felt.

*Just what they wanted,* Bloof thought. *The Program built something to hurt people and it works. Perfect product.*

Their ears had been flat against their skull for an hour. Somewhere in the walls, the sanctuary's wards hummed their low, steady note, the sound of a place that existed because someone had decided Bloof deserved one.

The knock came softly, two taps, a pause, then a third.

Bloof was on their feet before they'd decided to stand. They scrubbed at their face with a sleeve, hard enough to sting. The tail uncoiled and snapped into some kind of casual position, which fooled no one, least of all the tail.

"Yeah," Bloof said. It came out rough. "Door's open."

Bazemir stepped inside and closed it behind him. He hadn't changed out of the long blue coat, and the gold trim caught what little light came from the hall. His hair fell loose and pale down his back, and his eyes, amber and unhurried, found Bloof at once and did not leave.

He said nothing about the dark room, the untouched bed, or the black tracks running down from Bloof's eyes where their mascara had given up. He took all of it in, and Bloof watched him do it.

"Not hungry," Bloof said before he could ask, forcing a half-smile. "I'm basically self-sustaining. Monster energy and spite."

The joke came out thin, with no weight behind it.

"I'm fine," they added, smearing the black further across their fur. "Long night, that's all. Mission was clean. You don't have to check on me."

"I know I do not have to."

Bazemir crossed the room slowly, giving Bloof time to see him coming, and sat on the edge of the bed with his coat pooling over the blanket. Then he waited. The silence stretched. Bloof's tail twitched, curled in, and began wrapping around their own leg without permission.

"You are doing it again," Bazemir said quietly. "Your tail. You have kept it around yourself since the day I met you whenever you are trying not to be seen. It is a very honest tail, Bloof. It has never once lied to me."

Heat crawled up the back of Bloof's neck. They yanked the tail loose, and it immediately curled back in. "Traitor," they muttered.

"I find it endearing."

Bloof's ears jerked halfway upright in pure shock. "You—what?"

"I said what I said." Bazemir's mouth moved at one corner, dry and gentle. "I would repeat it, but I suspect you would leave through the wall."

*Why does that work?* The thought was almost resentful. *He says one stupid nice thing and my whole head just... stops.* There was a pull in their chest toward him, constant and quiet and impossible to account for. They had spent months refusing to look at it directly.

---

"Sit," Bazemir said. It was an invitation, not an order. "Please."

Bloof stood there for three more seconds out of pure stubbornness, then sat a careful distance from him, elbows on their knees, eyes on the floor. Even without looking, they could feel his gaze, so deep it made them feel as though the clothes they wore weren't even there. It made them quite... embarrassed.

"Bloovrn." His voice held something beyond pure worry, almost a yearning for Bloof to speak. He moved a little closer, which, weirdly, made Bloof's heart skip.

"Don't worry about me." It came out very low. "You've got centuries. You don't need me falling apart on top of it. I'll handle it. I always do."

"I have no doubt you will," Bazemir said. "That is the part that concerns me. You have the habit of someone who learned early that pain is a private matter, that if it is carried alone, it cannot cost anyone else anything. I know the habit. I wore it for a very long time." He paused. "Look at me, please."

Bloof did, slowly, reluctantly, and then wished they hadn't. It wasn't pity in his eyes. It was recognition, worry, and something else, the kind you only get from someone who's walked the same ground.

Something in Bloof's face gave. Only a little, a crack at the edge of the mask, but they saw him see it.

"It's nothing," Bloof said, and their voice cracked clean down the middle. "I'm just tired. I'm tired, okay?"

A single tear cut a line through the smeared black on their cheek, and then another. Their vulnerability made them feel stupid and downright pathetic, and they dragged their knees inward, retreating into themselves, hoping everything would just go away.

Bazemir didn't touch them. He didn't hurry, and he didn't fill the quiet with reassurance. He let it be.

"When I was young," he said at last, "I watched the dragons of my people hunted. Not in one night. Slowly. Whole households. Scholars I had read since boyhood. Children, in numbers I will not say aloud. There were sky-roads we had flown for a thousand years, and then they were empty, and nobody spoke of why." His tail shifted against the blanket, small and controlled. "I fought hundreds of them. I was terrifyingly good at it."

"I know," Bloof whispered. They'd seen him fight. It was one of the few times they had ever seen violence and dared to call it beautiful. The precision. The clean strokes of his sword.

"And I was caught anyway." His voice did not waver, but something beneath it did. "All of that grief turned into precision, and it bought no one anything. I lay in that cold for hundreds of years, awake for more of it than I would have chosen, and thought of every person I had not saved." He looked down at his long-fingered hands. "I have a long list of people I killed. Some of them I would do again. And on the nights after, when I could finally be still, I did not think *they were enemies.* I thought: *what has it made me?*"

Bloof's breath caught.

"So when you tell me you are fine, I believe you want to be. But I would be a poor friend, and a worse..." He stopped, something quick and quickly buried crossing his face. "A poor friend, if I let you carry it in the dark."

---

Above Bloof's brow, beneath the star-mark, the Crown Eyes slid open on their own, red and bright, as though forced open in awe. Below them, their face shone with tears.

"Bazemir." The words came out reluctantly. "Do you think I'm a monster?"

Everything broke at once: their voice, the mask, all the control they had spent years building.

"No," Bazemir said immediately. "Bloovrn, no—"

"Don't." Bloof was already scrambling backward off the bed until their shoulders hit the wall and the chains rattled. Their tail clamped tight around their legs. "You don't get to say it like it's obvious. You weren't there. I killed someone's *her*. I don't even remember her face. I remember a door, and I remember it feeling *good*, and I let it, Baz. And then some stranger looks me dead in the eyes and says what I already fucking know." A laugh tore out of them that had nothing to do with humor. "Cruel monster. Yeah. No shit. That's the whole point of me, isn't it?"

"That is not—"

"I'm so fucking weak. Years of discipline, and I still can't hold it. The second it wants out I'm not even in there. I'm just watching, like it's a movie and I'm the one holding the knife." Their back slid down the wall. "They made me this way. They looked at a kid and saw a place to put something. And I came out exactly how they wanted, so what the fuck is wrong with me that I'm *good* at it?"

"Bloof."

"I just wanted a normal life!" It came out raw enough to hurt. "A degree, a shitty apartment, mom not having to worry. Soccer on weekends like an idiot. And I don't even get to want it. Two sets of eyes. Horns. Heat coming off me like a furnace. Normal people don't have to check if they're going to kill someone at dinner. I'm a fucking mistake. A lab-grown fuck-up that got up and walked out, and I'm not even a beast, I'm just what's left over when they—"

Their breath hitched, and then it would not come back. They could only gasp, loud and ragged, shoulders locked.

Bazemir let it run longer than he wanted to. He knew, from the long years behind the ice, that some things had to be said into the open before they could be answered. But there was a limit, and he reached it. He crossed the room in three steps, went to his knees in front of Bloof, and pulled them into his arms.

Bloof went rigid. "No—don't, I'm still hot, I'll burn you, get off—"

"Then I will burn," Bazemir said into their hair. His voice was not steady anymore. "Let me."

"You don't get it—"

"I understand more than you would like." One hand cradled the back of their head, gentle around the base of their horns. "You are not weak. You are not a mistake. You are not a monster. I am not saying it to make the pain stop. I am saying it because it is true, and I will go on saying it until you are tired of hearing it."

Bloof fought for a few more seconds. Then it stopped all at once, as if a string had been cut, and their fists closed in the fabric and held on. They had never once, in all the time they'd known him, held on first. Their forehead fell against his shoulder and a sob shook out of them, then another, the way a drowning person grips whatever is solid. Slowly, their tail uncoiled from their own legs and curled around his instead.

It was Bloof who spoke first, hoarse and wrecked, into the front of his coat.

"I don't get why you bother. You're brilliant. You're, like, old-world royalty or something, and you could have anyone. And you keep picking up the broken thing the Program left on the floor. Nobody would ever choose to be around that. Who the hell would want—"

Bazemir moved before he'd decided to. One moment Bloof was talking, and the next he had cupped their face in both hands and tilted it up, and his mouth was on theirs.

It was not graceful. It was sudden, and fierce, and a little desperate, as if the words *nobody would ever want you* had cracked something old in him that had been holding the same sentence for centuries.

Bloof's eyes were wide open, both pairs, black and red and shining. Every thought they had ever had left their head at once.

---

It lasted perhaps three seconds.

Then Bazemir realized what he was doing and pulled back so fast his horns clipped the bed frame. A flush spread dark along his cheekbones, up into the fine scales at his temples, and a thread of static snapped between his fingers and the wall.

"Bloovrn. Forgive me. I did not intend— Oh gods, I am sorry. You were in pain, and I took advantage of a moment I had no right to. If you want me to leave, I will leave. We will never speak of it, and I will—"

"Baz."

"—make certain it never happens again, I give you my word, I—"

"*Baz.*"

He stopped, his ears gone flat with mortification.

Bloof's tail was slowly unwinding. Their hand came up, shaky and warm, and touched their own mouth like they were checking it was still there. "You kissed me and now you're apologizing."

"It is the correct response to—"

"It's a really bad response." A wet, startled laugh escaped them. It was small and cracked, but it was real, and it was the first real one all night. "Oh my god. You're blushing. The big scary ancient dragon is blushing."

"I am simply... recalibrating."

"You stole that from me."

Bloof looked at him for a long moment. The Crown Eyes were still open, and for once they weren't showing a threat. They were showing the thing underneath. Under his skin, the fine lightning markings along his hands and throat had lit faintly, like circuits waking after a long dark, and his pulse was racing. He was watching them with the terror of someone who has said the thing out loud and is waiting to be told it was a mistake.

*He's scared,* Bloof realized. *He's actually scared of what I'll say.*

Nobody had ever been scared of losing *them* before.

"Don't leave," they said. They pushed off the wall onto their knees, and their hands settled on the front of his coat, over the gold trim, and held on. "I don't know why I feel like this. I've never been able to explain it. Every time you're around, everything just goes quiet. I hate that I can't make sense of it, but I don't hate you. I could never hate you."

Bazemir's breath left him slowly. "Then let me ask, if I may."

"Yeah." Bloof swallowed. "You may."

This time he moved slowly, giving them every chance to say no. His thumb brushed the smeared black from beneath their eye, and Bloof leaned into the touch before he finished the motion. The kiss was gentle and unhurried, and it tasted faintly of salt. Bloof's arms went around his neck, pressing closer, as if trying to climb inside the warmth of it. Their scales ran hot, and Bazemir, who had been cold in his bones for four hundred years, leaned into it with something close to a sigh.

"You are warm," he murmured against their forehead. "I had forgotten what it was to be warm without it being a weapon."

"I'm literally a furnace," Bloof said unsteadily. "It's a design flaw."

"It is a gift." He pressed his lips between the star-mark on their brow and the lids of the Crown Eyes, light as breath. "You are stubborn and sharp and impossibly kind to people who do not deserve it, and you have spent every day since I met you apologizing for existing. I will not spend my remaining centuries listening to you do it."

Bloof's face went scarlet to the tips of their ears, which promptly folded flat. They hid in his shoulder. "I'm really bad at being told nice things. I don't know what to do with them."

"You do not have to do anything with them. You only have to let them stay."

Bloof was quiet for a while. "The guilt isn't going away," they said eventually, muffled. "She's still gone. I still did it."

"No," Bazemir agreed gently. "It will not simply go away, and I will not insult you by pretending otherwise. But you do not carry yours alone now. Let me hold the weight beside you."

Something in Bloof's chest, a knot they'd worn so long they had stopped noticing it, finally loosened.

---

The second kiss started soft and did not stay that way.

Somewhere in the middle of it, Bloof made a small, hungry sound against his mouth, and Bazemir felt it all the way down his spine. His hands, which had been so careful, tightened at their waist. He pulled them into his lap, and Bloof went willingly, knees on either side of him, claws curling into the gold-trimmed collar of his coat.

"Baz," Bloof breathed, and it came out unsteady. "I'm— my scales are going to..."

They were. Red light was creeping up along the ridges of their shoulders and spine, ember-bright, and the heat rolling off them rippled the air. Bloof tried to pull back, ears pinned with worry. Bazemir caught their wrist before they could.

"Do not hold it in," he said, low and rough. "Not with me."

"I'll hurt you."

"Then I will find out how much I can bear." Something dangerous and happy curved at the corner of his mouth. "I am rather difficult to hurt, little ember. And I have been cold for a very long time."

He kissed them again, deeper, and let go of the careful restraint he had worn for centuries.

The air answered him. A crackle ran across the blanket. The fine hair at the nape of Bloof's neck lifted, and a slow wave of static ran down Bloof's arms, every fur and scale on their body prickling at once. Bloof gasped into his mouth, and their heat flared in reply. Where fire touched lightning, the room filled with thin, bright threads of both, red and blue-white, tangling across their skin like they'd been waiting for each other.

"Oh," Bloof said faintly, staring at the sparks climbing up Bazemir's wrists. Through the Crown Eyes, they could see all of it: the lightning moving under his scales like a second pulse, the heat in their own chest answering it. "Oh, that's... that's pretty."

"So are you." He said it plainly, as if reporting a measurement, and watched the words land. "I should have said so months ago. I confess I spent a great deal of that time trying not to look at your mouth."

Bloof's ears folded flat. "You did *not.*"

"I did. Frequently. During briefings." His thumb dragged slowly across their lower lip. "It was a poor use of my attention, and I regret none of it."

"You're—" Bloof's voice cracked. "That's not fair, I can't think when you say stuff like that."

"Good," Bazemir said, so softly it was almost a purr. "Then do not think."

His coat hit the floor first, then the waistcoat, the buttons coming undone under Bloof's clumsy, eager claws. They dragged the ascot loose with a grin that was half-shy and half-wicked, and Bazemir let out a breath that was almost a laugh. When Bloof's palms slid inside his open shirt and flattened against his chest, he shuddered. Lightning flickered across his ribs in bright, branching lines, and his whole body went still under the touch.

It had been so long. Centuries of ice and cold stone, and nobody's hands but the ones that measured him. Now someone touched him, warm and unafraid, and he felt every inch of it.

Bloof noticed. They stopped. "Hey." Their voice softened, and the fire in their scales dimmed to something gentle. "You okay? You're shaking."

"I am." His voice was thick. "Forgive me. I did not expect it to feel like..." He couldn't finish. His eyes were bright and a little wet, and he looked more undone than any scholar had a right to. "It has been a very long time, Bloovrn."

Bloof's heart cracked open. They cupped his face in both hands, the way he had held theirs. "Then we go slow," they said. "You don't have to be anything but here. Okay?"

He nodded, wordless, and for a moment he only breathed against their palm. Then the corner of his mouth lifted again, and the shaking in his voice turned into something warmer.

"Though I will say, if you intend to go slowly, you are doing a remarkable job of making it difficult."

Bloof choked. "Baz!"

"What? It was an observation." His eyes glinted, wicked and amber. "You are sitting in my lap, little ember. I would be a poor scholar if I did not notice."

Bloof's face went so red it nearly matched their scales. They shoved their forehead into his shoulder with a strangled noise. "Oh my *god.* Who are you? Where did the stuffy ancient dragon go?"

"He was cold for four hundred years," Bazemir murmured against their ear. "He has decided he would prefer to be indecent."

Bloof kissed him mostly to make him stop talking. It did not work. It worked, in fact, in the opposite direction, because they pushed him back among the pillows and climbed over him with their tail curling around his thigh, and his hands settled low on their hips as if they'd always belonged there.

They kissed their way along his jaw, down the long line of his throat, and over the fine scales at the hollow of his collarbone. Each time their mouth passed over a lightning mark, it flared. By the time they reached his ribs, a low, helpless sound was spilling from Bazemir's throat, and his hands had tangled in the blanket.

"That," he managed, "is entirely unfair."

"You started it." Bloof grinned against his skin, and their claws traced a slow, deliberate path down his side. His breath caught. The static in the room spiked.

"I am going to make you regret that," Bazemir said, a little breathless.

"Promise?"

The word was out before Bloof could stop it. Bazemir went completely still. Then he smiled, slow and devastating.

"Oh, little ember," he said. "That was a mistake."

He flipped them with a fluid strength that startled a laugh out of Bloof, then caught the laugh with his mouth. His hands slid under their shirt, the scales along their sides burning warm beneath his palms. He took his time, mapping them like a text he intended to memorize. When he brushed his thumb along the base of their horn, Bloof arched, a sharp breath tearing out of them, and every ember along their spine flared at once.

"There," Bazemir murmured, entirely too pleased with himself. "I had wondered."

"You're the worst," Bloof gasped. "I take it back. You're a— *oh.*"

Lightning prickled along his fingertips, faint and controlled, a fine, delicate current that traced the lines of their ribs and made every nerve sing. Bloof's breath came out in short, ragged pulls, and their claws dragged shallow marks in the sheets. The heat rolling off them rose with each pass of his hand until the air above the bed shimmered.

"Tell me if it is too much," Bazemir said, his lips against the hollow of their throat, his voice dropped low enough to feel in their bones. "I can be gentle. But I suspect you do not want gentle right now."

"I don't—" Bloof's breath hitched as the current ran down their spine. "I don't know what I want."

"Allow me to suggest." His mouth moved lower, and his voice moved with it, unhurried and soft. "You want to be looked at. You want to be touched like you are worth the time. And you want to stop being the one who takes care of everyone else for a few hours." A slow, deliberate kiss to their sternum. "Let me do that. Let me take care of you."

Bloof made a sound they would never admit to. "That's— you can't just *say* that."

"I have said it. I intend to say a great deal more." His eyes lifted to theirs, dark with want. "You are very beautiful when you stop fighting it, Bloovrn."

Then his mouth found the pale scar near their collarbone, and Bloof stiffened.

Bazemir stilled at once, lightning dimming, hands gentle. "I will stop."

"No." Bloof's fingers tightened in his hair. "Don't. It's just— those are from..." They didn't finish.

"I know." He pressed a kiss to the scar, then another, slow and reverent, before lifting his eyes to theirs. "They are part of you, and I would not take a single one away. I only wish to be kind to them."

Bloof's eyes stung again, but it felt less like falling and more like being set down. They pulled him back to their mouth and kissed him hard, with the whole of their shaking, grateful heart.

When they broke apart, Bazemir's voice had dropped to something rougher and a good deal less formal.

"I want you," he said. "I will say it plainly, since I have spent far too long being polite about it. I want to feel you against me, every inch of you. I want to hear what you sound like when you stop holding back." His forehead rested against theirs. "But only if you want it too."

Bloof stared at him, wide-eyed, breathing hard. Every witty deflection they owned had burned away. What was left came out small and certain.

"Yes," they whispered. "Yeah. I want that. I want *you.*"

"Then say my name."

"Baz."

"Again."

"*Baz.*"

The sound he made in response was low and ragged, and sparks leapt from his horns to the headboard.

Clothes were lost somewhere between kisses. Bloof's chains slid off the edge of the bed with a quiet clatter, and the black-and-red jacket went somewhere unseen. His hands found their hips and their hands found his back, and they moved together, slow and then not slow at all. Bloof's tail wound tight around his leg, and the feathered tip of his own wrapped around theirs as if it had a mind of its own. His mouth was at their ear the whole time, and the words he spoke there were the kind that made Bloof's claws dig into his shoulders and their breath stutter out in broken pieces.

"Look at you," he murmured. "So warm. So perfect. You feel like the sun, do you know that?"

"Baz— please—"

"Please what?" he asked, wicked and tender at once. "Tell me. I want to hear you ask."

"*More,*" Bloof gasped, and they hated how wrecked it sounded and loved it all at once. "Please, just— *more.*"

"Yes," Bazemir breathed. "Yes. Anything."

Heat pooled low and bright between them, and the last of his control finally gave way. He pressed his face into Bloof's neck and let himself burn, his voice breaking over their name, a name and a prayer. "Bloof. Bloovrn. *Mine*— oh, forgive me, I did not mean—"

"Don't take it back," Bloof said, shaking, clinging to him. "Please. I've got you. I'm right here."

Sparks arced from his horns to theirs, bright blue against red. The wards in the walls flickered and hummed, caught somewhere between startled and delighted. Outside the sanctuary, far off, thunder rolled over the dark hills. Bloof's Crown Eyes blazed, and when the last of it broke over them, they saw it clearly: his lightning rising to meet their fire, the two lights flaring brighter than either could alone, and then settling, slow and gold, into something like peace.

---

Much later, the lights were still off, but the dark felt different.

Bloof lay against his chest with their head tucked under his chin, one leg tangled with his, a blanket pulled haphazardly over both of them. The last of the static drifted across the sheets in lazy blue threads, and every so often it caught against a still-glowing ember along Bloof's shoulder and flickered out. Their tail was no longer wound around themself. It was wrapped around him, loose and relaxed, the red-tipped end curled against the feathered tip of his own.

Bazemir's fingers moved slowly through their hair, brushing the base of a horn every few passes. Each time, Bloof made a low, helpless, contented sound they would deny forever.

"Your mascara is on my pillow," Bazemir observed, voice rough and soft all at once. "And on my shirt. And, somehow, on the ceiling."

"That's *definitely* your fault."

"I will not argue." He pressed a kiss to the top of their head. "I could find I do not mind."

"Baz," they mumbled, half asleep.

"Yes, little ember?"

Bloof's ears twitched. "...Did you just give me a nickname?"

"It is possible I did."

"It's dumb."

"You are smiling."

"I'm not." They absolutely were. They pressed their face into his collar. "...Okay. Maybe it's not that dumb."

His chest shook beneath them with a soft, silent laugh.

"Sleep, Bloovrn. I will be here."

"Promise?"

"On every name I have ever carried."

Bloof breathed out. The tension went out of their shoulders all at once, and their tail tightened, once, around him, a small, honest signal, the way it had always been.
