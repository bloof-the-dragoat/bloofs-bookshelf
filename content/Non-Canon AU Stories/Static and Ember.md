# Static and Ember

The door was shut, the lights were off, and Bloof had not moved in a long time.

They sat on the floor at the foot of the bed with their back against the frame and their knees pulled up to their chest. Their tail was wound around their legs, the red-tipped end tucked under one arm like something they were trying to keep from escaping. The jacket was still on. It still smelled of smoke and iron. Every chain on their pants was still clipped where it belonged, because taking anything off would have meant admitting the mission was over.

Their right hand rested on the floorboards beside them. Where their claws had been pressed, a thin curl of smoke rose from the wood, and four small black scorch marks had burned into the grain. Bloof watched them without really seeing them.

*Monster.*

The enemy had said it with blood on their teeth, in a wet, rattling voice. They had been strong, stronger than the report claimed, and they had not begged or bargained. They had looked up at Bloof, at the red light still seeping out of the scales along Bloof's arms, and the glow had been reflected in their eyes like two tiny fires. They had said it like a fact they'd always known.

*YOU TOOK HER FROM ME. YOU'RE A FUCKING MONSTER!*

Bloof hadn't known who "her" was, not at first. Then the memory had arrived, because it always did. It was the night they went berserk, the night Wrath had taken the wheel and Bloof had let it. A house. A doorway with the light on behind it. Someone standing in it.

The cultist's death had been justified, and Bloof knew it. They would have killed three more people before the night ended. Bloof had run the numbers over and over until the numbers were just a hum, and the numbers were fine. But the numbers couldn't take away the sound of that voice.

*Just what they wanted,* Bloof thought. *The Program built something to hurt people and it works. Perfect product.*

Their ears had been pinned flat against their skull for an hour, and the fur along the back of them had gone stiff. Somewhere in the walls, the sanctuary's wards hummed one low, steady note, like a cello string someone had drawn a bow across and never lifted. It was the sound of a place that existed because someone had decided Bloof deserved one.

The knock came softly, two taps, a pause, then a third.

Bloof was on their feet before they'd decided to stand. They dragged a sleeve across their face hard enough to sting and smeared black across their gray cheek-fur. Their breath shook on the way in. The tail uncoiled and snapped into something that was supposed to look casual, and fooled no one, least of all the tail.

"Yeah," Bloof said. It came out like gravel. "Door's open."

The latch clicked. Bazemir stepped in and closed it behind him, and the smell of the hallway came with him, cold stone and rain and something sharp underneath, like air just after lightning. He hadn't changed out of the long blue coat. A strip of hall light caught the gold trim along its hem and the high collar, and the hem whispered against the floor when he moved. His pale lavender hair fell loose past his shoulders. The feathered tip of his tail flicked once behind him and went still.

His eyes, amber and unhurried, found Bloof at once and did not leave.

He said nothing about the dark room. He said nothing about the untouched bed, the scorched floorboards, or the black tracks running down from Bloof's eyes where their mascara had given up. His gaze moved over each of them in turn, slowly, the way he read a page, and Bloof stood there and felt every second of it.

"Not hungry," Bloof said before he could ask. They forced a half-smile that pulled at one corner of their mouth and went nowhere. "I'm basically self-sustaining. Monster energy and spite."

The joke came out thin and landed on the floor between them with no sound at all.

"I'm fine," they added, scrubbing at their cheek again, which only smeared the black wider. "Long night, that's all. Mission was clean. You don't have to check on me."

"I know I do not have to."

Bazemir crossed the room slowly, giving Bloof time to see him coming, and sat on the edge of the bed. The mattress dipped. His coat pooled over the blanket in a long fall of blue. Then he waited, hands resting on his knees, and did not say another word.

The silence stretched. Bloof's tail twitched, curled in, and began winding itself around their own leg, one slow loop after another, without any permission at all.

"You are doing it again," Bazemir said quietly.

Bloof looked down. The tail had already made three turns around their calf. They yanked it loose. It snapped back in at once.

"Your tail," Bazemir said. "You have kept it around yourself since the day I met you whenever you are trying not to be seen. It is a very honest tail, Bloof. It has never once lied to me."

Heat crawled up the back of Bloof's neck and into their ears, which they could not stop from twitching. "Traitor," they muttered, glaring down at the tail.

"I find it endearing."

Bloof's head came up so fast the chains rattled. Their ears, flat all night, jerked halfway upright. "You—*what?*"

"I said what I said." The corner of Bazemir's mouth lifted, dry and gentle. "I would repeat it, but I suspect you would leave through the wall."

"I'd consider it." Their face was burning. They did not know what to do with their hands. They shoved them in their pockets, found nothing there, and pulled them out again.

*Why does that work?* The thought was nearly a snarl. *One stupid nice thing, and my whole head just stops.* It was like a switch had been thrown somewhere behind their ribs. There was a pull in their chest toward him, quiet and constant, like the hum of the wards. They had spent months refusing to follow it to its end.

"Sit," Bazemir said. It was an invitation, not an order. "Please."

Bloof stood there for three more seconds out of pure stubbornness. Then they sat, a careful arm's length from him, elbows on their knees and eyes fixed on the floorboards. Even without looking, they could feel his gaze resting on them, steady and warm. It was so direct that their shoulders crept up around their neck, as if they'd forgotten how to wear their own clothes.

"Bloovrn." His voice had dropped, and under the calm it was a plea, soft and low, asking them to speak. He shifted closer, and the mattress creaked. Bloof's heart skipped, then kicked hard against their ribs.

"Don't worry about me." It came out barely audible. "You've got centuries. You don't need me falling apart on top of it. I'll handle it. I always do."

"I have no doubt you will," Bazemir said. "That is the part that concerns me. You have the habit of someone who learned early that pain is a private matter, that if it is carried alone, it cannot cost anyone else anything. I know the habit. I wore it for a very long time." He paused. "Look at me, please."

Bloof looked, slowly, reluctantly, and wished they hadn't. There was no pity in his face. His brows had drawn together, and the amber in his eyes had gone soft and bright, and he was looking at Bloof the way a person looks at a road they have walked themselves.

Something in Bloof's face gave. Only a little, a tremor at the corner of their mouth, a crack at the edge of the mask. But they saw him see it.

"It's nothing," Bloof said, and their voice split clean down the middle. "I'm just tired. I'm tired, okay?"

A single tear cut a clean line through the smeared black on their cheek, and then another followed it. Bloof turned their face to the wall and pressed their lips together until they went pale. The only sound in the room was their breathing, uneven and trying not to be. They dragged their knees up and curled inward, shoulders hunched, claws digging into their own sleeves, as though they could fold small enough that none of it would show.

Bazemir didn't touch them. He didn't hurry, and he didn't fill the quiet with soothing noise. His tail shifted against the blanket with a soft rasp of scale on cloth, and he let the silence be.

"When I was young," he said at last, "I watched the dragons of my people hunted."

Bloof's ears turned toward him, one at a time, and their shoulders loosened half an inch.

"Not in one night. Slowly. Whole households. Scholars I had read since boyhood. Children, in numbers I will not say aloud." His gaze had gone somewhere past the wall. "There were sky-roads we had flown for a thousand years, and then one season they were empty, and nobody spoke of why." The feathered tip of his tail twitched against the blanket, small and tightly controlled. "I fought hundreds of them. I was terrifyingly good at it."

"I know," Bloof whispered. They had seen him fight. One blue-white streak, one clean arc of steel, and a man hitting the ground before the thunder reached them. It was one of the few times in their life they had watched violence and dared to call it beautiful.

"And I was caught anyway." His voice did not waver, but something beneath it did, like a floor over deep water. "All of that grief, honed to a point, and it bought no one anything. I lay in the cold for hundreds of years, awake for more of it than I would have chosen, and thought of every person I had not saved." He looked down at his long-fingered hands, lying very still on his knee. "I have a long list of people I killed. Some of them I would do again. And on the nights after, when I could finally be still, I did not think *they were enemies.* I thought: *what has it made me?*"

Bloof's breath stopped in their throat.

"So when you tell me you are fine, I believe you want to be. But I would be a poor friend, and a worse..." He stopped. Something flickered across his face, quick and quickly buried. "A poor friend, if I let you carry it in the dark."

Above Bloof's brow, beneath the purple star-mark, the seam of skin split. The Crown Eyes slid open on their own, red and glowing, the white pupils wide, as though something older than Bloof had decided to see him properly. Below them, their face shone with tears.

"Bazemir." The words came out in pieces. "Do you think I'm a monster?"

Everything broke at once, their voice, the mask, all the control they had built over years, like a dam with one crack running up it.

"No," Bazemir said immediately. "Bloovrn, no—"

"Don't. ***Don't***!" Bloof was already scrambling backward off the bed. The chains clattered against the frame and their shoulders hit the wall hard enough to rattle the pictures. Their tail clamped around their legs so tightly the scales creaked. "You don't get to say it like it's *obvious.* You weren't *there.* I killed someone's *her.* I don't even remember her face. I remember a door, and I remember it feeling *good,* and I let it, Baz. And then some stranger looks me dead in the eyes and says what I already **fucking know.**" A laugh tore out of them that had no humor in it at all, rough as torn cloth. "*Cruel monster.* Yeah. *No shit.* That's the whole point of me, isn't it?"

"That is not—"

"I'm so *fucking* weak." Their hands were shaking so badly their claws clicked together. "Years of discipline, and I still can't hold **Wrath** in! The second it wants out, I'm not even *in there!* I'm just *watching.* Like it's a movie and I'm the one holding the knife." Their back slid down the wall until they were crouching. "They made me this way. They looked at a *kid* and saw a place to put something. And I came out exactly how they wanted, so what the *fuck* is wrong with me that I'm *good* at it?"

"Bloof—"

Their breath hitched and would not come back. They could only drag in great, ragged gasps, with a wheeze at the bottom of each one, shoulders locked up around their ears.

One hand had found their other forearm. Bloof didn't notice it happen. Their silver claws had sunk in through the torn sleeve, and with every strangled breath they dragged a little further, four slow, parallel lines down the scales. The scales split first. Then the skin beneath them opened, and blood welled up black in the dark, ran down their wrist, and fell onto the floorboards in fat, quiet drops. Where it touched their overheated scales it hissed and spat. The sharp, coppery smell reached the Crown Eyes before it reached anything else. Bloof's gaze stayed fixed on nothing, wide and glassy and pointed straight through the wall. Their hand kept dragging.

Bazemir let it run longer than he wanted to. He knew, from the long years behind the ice, that some things had to be said into the open before they could be answered. His hands had curled into fists at his sides, and tiny blue sparks crawled along the knuckles. But he had listened to this for as long as he could stand, and he could stand a great deal.

Then the first drop of blood hit the floor, and he stopped listening.

He crossed the room in three strides, dropped to his knees, and closed one hand around Bloof's wrist, firmly, but not hard enough to hurt. The claws stopped mid-drag.

"Bloovrn." His voice was very quiet, and not steady. "Your hand."

Bloof blinked. They looked down at the line of red running over Bazemir's fingers, the torn sleeve, the claws still half-buried in their own arm, as if looking at something that belonged to someone else. "Oh," they said, small and blank. "I didn't..."

Bazemir eased the claws free, one at a time, and pulled Bloof into his arms.

Bloof went rigid as a drawn bowstring. "No—don't, I'm still hot, I'll burn you, get *off*—"

Heat rolled off them in waves. The sleeve of Bazemir's shirt began to smoke where it pressed against their shoulder. He did not let go.

"Then I will burn," he said into their hair. His voice was no longer steady. "Let me."

"You don't get it—"

"I understand more than you would like." One hand cradled the back of their skull, fingers spread gently around the base of their horns. The other pressed flat between their shoulder blades, firm and unhurried, over the pounding of their heart. "You are not weak. You are not a mistake. You are not a monster. I am not saying it to make the pain stop. I am saying it because it is true, and I will go on saying it until you are tired of hearing it."

Bloof fought him. The shove came first, both palms against his chest, harder than they meant it, and Bazemir's breath left him in a short grunt. He did not let go. Their claws snagged in the gold braid of his coat and tore a thread loose. Their tail lashed against the floor with a crack that rattled the bed frame, and the heat coming off their scales spiked until the wool of his sleeve began to smell of singed thread. A bead of blood from the torn arm smeared across the blue, dark and wet.

"Let go." Their voice was a thin, cracked thing. "Let go, let *go*, you're getting blood on your coat, you're, I'm, I'll *hurt* you—"

"You have not."

"I *will.*" They twisted in his grip, shoulders jerking, the back of their skull cracking against the wall. "That's, that's what I *do*, Baz, that's the only thing I do, you can't just—" Their voice splintered. "Don't. Don't be nice to me. I don't, I'm not, I can't *take* it, I didn't *earn* it. You don't, you shouldn't be *touching* me, I'm not someone you're, you're supposed to—"

"Supposed to what?" Bazemir's arms tightened, careful of the wounded arm, but not loosening. "Tell me what I am supposed to do, Bloovrn. Leave you on the floor? Hand you a report and a blanket and close the door?"

"*Yes!*" It tore out of them. Their fists beat against his chest, once, twice, weaker each time. "Yes, that's, that's what you do with, with *things* like me, you put them somewhere and you, you don't—you don't *hold* them, you can't, I'm not *worth*—"

"You are."

"I'm *not.*"

"You are." He said it into their hair, quiet and absolutely certain, as if reading a fact off a page. "You may fight me for as long as you like. I will not move."

Bloof fought him for another handful of seconds that felt like hours. The heat came off them in waves. Sparks leapt from Bazemir's shoulders to the floor, to the wall, to the edge of the bed, and neither of them noticed. Their shoves slowed. Their claws caught in his coat and stuck there, and the tail that had been lashing against the floorboards dragged to a stop.

Then it all stopped at once, as if a string had been cut.

Their fists closed in the front of his shirt and held on.

In all the time they had known him, they had never once held on first.

Their forehead fell against his shoulder. A sob shook out of them, deep and ugly, and then another, the way a drowning person grips whatever is solid. Bazemir's shirt grew damp and warm with tears and black smears of mascara. Slowly, the tail uncoiled from around Bloof's own legs and curled around his instead.

Bazemir held on through all of it. He felt the heat on his chest ease as they cried, scalding at first, then merely hot, then just warm.

It was Bloof who spoke first, hoarse and scraped raw, into the front of his shirt.

"I don't get why you bother."

"Bloof—"

"You're brilliant. You're, like, old-world royalty or something, and you could have anyone. And you keep picking up the broken thing the Program left on the floor." Their voice cracked. "Nobody would ever choose to be around that. Who the hell would want—"

Bazemir moved before he'd decided to.

One moment Bloof was talking, and the next he had taken their face in both hands, tilted it up, and his mouth was on theirs.

It was not graceful. It was sudden and fierce, and a little desperate. It was the kiss of someone who had heard the words *nobody would ever want you* and felt something old crack open in his chest, a sentence he had been holding in the same place for centuries.

Bloof's eyes were wide open, both pairs, black and red and shining. A spark snapped between their lips. Every thought they had ever had left their head at once.

---

It lasted perhaps three seconds.

Then Bazemir realized what he was doing and jerked back so fast his horns cracked against the bed frame. Color flooded his face, dark along the cheekbones and up into the fine scales at his temples. A thread of blue static leapt from his fingers to the wall with a sharp *snap*, and a small black star appeared in the paint.

"Bloovrn. Forgive me. I did not intend— Oh gods, I am sorry. You were in pain, and I took advantage of a moment I had no right to. If you want me to leave, I will leave. We will never speak of it, and I will—"

"Baz."

"—make certain it never happens again, I give you my word, I—"

"*Baz.*"

He stopped. His ears, the long swept fins at the side of his head, had flattened tight against his skull.

Bloof's tail was slowly unwinding, one loop at a time. One hand rose, shaking and warm, and the fingertips touched their own mouth as if checking it was still there. "You kissed me and now you're apologizing."

"It is the correct response to—"

"It's a *really bad* response." A wet, startled laugh escaped them, small and cracked, but real. It was the first real one all night. "Oh my god. You're blushing. The big scary ancient dragon is blushing."

"I am simply... recalibrating."

"You stole that from me."

Bloof looked at him for a long moment. The Crown Eyes were still open, and for once they weren't showing a threat. They were showing what lay under it. The faint lightning marks along his hands and throat had lit up like circuits waking after a long dark. His pulse was visible at the base of his throat, hammering. He knelt with his hands hovering in the air as though he had just dropped something precious and did not dare to pick it up. He had the stillness of someone who has said the thing out loud and is waiting to be told it was a mistake.

*He's scared,* Bloof realized. *He's actually scared of what I'll say.*

Nobody had ever been scared of losing *them* before.

"Don't leave," they said.

They pushed off the wall onto their knees. The distance between them closed to nothing, and their hands settled on the front of his shirt and held on. Their voice shook. "I don't know why I feel like this. I've never been able to explain it. Every time you're around, everything just goes quiet. I hate that I can't make sense of it, but I don't hate you. I could never hate you."

Bazemir's breath left him slowly. "Then let me ask, if I may."

"Yeah." Bloof swallowed. "You may."

This time he moved slowly, giving them every chance to say no. His thumb brushed the smeared black from beneath their eye. Bloof leaned into the touch before he had finished the motion. The kiss was gentle and unhurried, and it tasted faintly of salt. Bloof's arms went around his neck and pressed close, as if trying to climb inside the warmth of it. Their scales ran hot, and Bazemir, who had been cold in his bones for four hundred years, leaned into it with something close to a sigh.

"You are warm," he murmured against their forehead. "I had forgotten what it was to be warm without it being a weapon."

"I'm literally a furnace," Bloof said unsteadily. "It's a design flaw."

"It is a gift." He pressed his lips between the star-mark on their brow and the lids of the Crown Eyes, light as breath. "You are stubborn and sharp and impossibly kind to people who do not deserve it, and you have spent every day since I met you apologizing for existing. I will not spend my remaining centuries listening to you do it."

Bloof's face went scarlet to the tips of their ears, which folded flat. They hid in his shoulder. "I'm really bad at being told nice things. I don't know what to do with them."

"You do not have to do anything with them. You only have to let them stay."

Bloof was quiet for a long while, breathing in the smell of rain and ozone. "The guilt isn't going away," they said eventually, muffled. "She's still gone. I still did it."

"No," Bazemir agreed gently. "It will not simply go away, and I will not insult you by pretending otherwise. But you do not carry yours alone now. Let me hold the weight beside you."

Something in Bloof's chest, a knot they had worn so long they had stopped noticing it, loosened with an almost audible give.

---

The second kiss started soft and did not stay that way.

Somewhere in the middle of it, Bloof made a small, hungry sound against his mouth, and Bazemir felt it all the way down his spine. His hands, which had been so careful, tightened at their waist. He drew them into his lap, and Bloof went willingly, knees on either side of him, claws hooking into the collar of his shirt.

"Baz," Bloof breathed, shakily. "I'm— my scales are going to..."

They were. A red glow was creeping up along the ridges of their shoulders and spine, ember-bright, and the air around them wavered like pavement in summer. Bloof tried to pull away, ears pinned with worry. Bazemir caught their wrist.

"Do not hold it in," he said, low and rough. "Not with me."

"I'll hurt you."

"Then I will find out how much I can bear." Something dangerous and happy curled at the corner of his mouth. "I am rather difficult to hurt, little ember. And I have been cold for a very long time."

He kissed them again, deeper, and let go of the careful restraint he had worn for centuries.

The air answered him. A crackle ran across the blanket, and the fine hairs on their arms stood up. A wave of static rolled down Bloof's spine, every strand of fur and every scale prickling at once. Bloof gasped into his mouth, and their heat flared in reply. Where fire met lightning the room filled with thin bright threads of both, red and blue-white, braiding across their skin like they had been waiting a very long time to meet.

"Oh," Bloof said faintly, staring at the sparks climbing his wrists. Through the Crown Eyes, they could see all of it: the lightning moving under his scales like a second pulse, and the heat in their own chest rising to answer it. "Oh, that's... that's pretty."

"So are you." He said it plainly, as if reporting a measurement, and watched the words land. "I should have said so months ago. I confess I spent a great deal of that time trying not to look at your mouth."

Bloof's ears folded flat. "You did *not.*"

"I did. Frequently. During briefings." His thumb dragged slowly across their lower lip, and Bloof's breath stuttered against it. "It was a poor use of my attention, and I regret none of it."

"You're—" Bloof's voice cracked. "That's not fair, I can't think when you say stuff like that."

"Good," Bazemir said, so softly it was almost a purr. "Then do not think."

The coat was already gone, tossed over the chair. His waistcoat followed, the buttons popping open under Bloof's clumsy, eager claws, one tiny click after another. They tugged the ascot loose and let it slither to the floor, grinning with a mix of shyness and wickedness, and Bazemir let out a breath that was nearly a laugh. When Bloof's palms slid inside his open shirt and flattened against his chest, he shuddered. Lightning flickered across his ribs in bright, branching lines, and his whole body went still under the touch.

It had been so long. Centuries of ice and cold stone, and nobody's hands but the ones that measured him. Now someone touched him, warm and unafraid, and he could feel every fingertip.

Bloof noticed. They stopped. "Hey." Their voice softened, and the fire along their scales dimmed to a low glow. "You okay? You're shaking."

"I am." His voice was thick. "Forgive me. I did not expect it to feel like..." He couldn't finish. His eyes were bright and wet at the edges, and he looked more undone than any scholar had a right to. "It has been a very long time, Bloovrn."

Bloof cupped his face in both hands, the way he had held theirs. Their thumbs brushed the lightning marks at his cheekbones, and the marks warmed under them. "Then we go slow," they said. "You don't have to be anything but here. Okay?"

He nodded, wordless, and for a moment he only breathed against their palm. Then the corner of his mouth lifted, and the shaking in his voice turned into something warmer.

"Though I will say, if you intend to go slowly, you are doing a remarkable job of making it difficult."

Bloof choked. "*Baz!*"

"What? It was an observation." His eyes glinted, wicked and amber. "You are sitting in my lap, little ember. I would be a poor scholar if I did not notice."

Bloof's face went so red it nearly matched their scales. They shoved their forehead into his shoulder with a strangled noise. "Oh my *god.* Who are you? Where did the stuffy ancient dragon go?"

"He was cold for four hundred years," Bazemir murmured against their ear, and the low timbre of it made them shiver. "He has decided he would prefer to be indecent."

Bloof kissed him mostly to make him stop talking. It did not work. It worked, in fact, in the opposite direction, because they pushed him back among the pillows and climbed over him, tail curling around his thigh, and his hands settled low on their hips as though they had always belonged there.

They kissed their way along his jaw, down the long line of his throat, over the fine pale scales at the hollow of his collarbone. Each time their mouth crossed a lightning mark, it flared bright blue under their lips and the skin beneath jumped. By the time they reached his ribs, a low, helpless sound was spilling out of Bazemir's throat, and his hands had knotted in the blanket.

"That," he managed, "is entirely unfair."

"You started it." Bloof grinned against his skin, and their claws traced a slow, deliberate line down his side. His breath caught. The static in the room spiked, and the lamp on the nightstand flickered.

"I am going to make you regret that," Bazemir said, a little breathless.

"Promise?"

The word was out before Bloof could stop it. Bazemir went completely still. Then he smiled, slow and devastating.

"Oh, little ember," he said. "That was a mistake."

He flipped them with a fluid strength that startled a laugh out of Bloof, then caught the laugh with his mouth. The pillows shifted under Bloof's shoulders, and the whole bed creaked. For a long moment he did nothing else. He simply looked down at them, his pale mane spilling over his shoulders and curtaining both their faces, his amber eyes gone dark and molten in the dim light.

"Do you know," he said quietly, "how I have *longed* to see you like this?"

"Like what?" Bloof's voice came out smaller than they intended.

"Unguarded." One long finger traced the dark curve of their jaw, the faint red crescent beneath their eye. "Soft. Looking at me as though I might be worth the risk." His thumb brushed the septum ring, so gently the metal barely stirred. "I intend to be worth it."

Bloof's ears folded flat. "Baz, you can't just *say* stuff like that—"

"I can. I **will.**" His mouth curved. "Especially when it makes you do that."

"Do *what?*"

"Blush down to your collarbone."

Bloof made a strangled noise and flung an arm over their face. Bazemir caught the wrist with a soft laugh and pinned it gently to the pillow beside their head. "No hiding. Not tonight. I should like to see all of you."

His other hand slid under their shirt, and the scales along their ribs burned warm under his palm. He took his time, mapping them like a text he intended to memorize. When his thumb brushed the base of their horn, Bloof arched off the mattress, a sharp breath tearing out of them, and every ember along their spine flared at once.
