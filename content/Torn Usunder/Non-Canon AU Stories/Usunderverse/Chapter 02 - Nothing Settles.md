# Chapter 02 — Nothing Settles

Bloof couldn’t finish the breath.

Their chest lifted. Their ribs strained against a tight, familiar ache, but the movement brought nothing with it. They opened their mouth and tried harder. No coolness touched their throat, no air stirred against their tongue; their body kept reaching for the end of an inhale that would not come.

They pressed a hand beneath their collarbone. Nothing tightened around their neck. Nothing covered their mouth.

There should have been a blanket beneath their other hand.

There was nothing.

Red extended in every direction, dim and depthless. Bloof turned their head, searching for a corner, a light, anything their eyes could settle on. Their feet hung without touching a floor. They could feel their own weight, but nothing held it.

They knew this place.

They twisted and clawed behind them for something solid. Their arm swept through empty space. They kicked downward and met no resistance, yet stayed exactly where they were.

They should have been growing faint. There should have been a limit, some point where the need outgrew what their body could bear. Instead, every failed breath left them awake for the next.

_Slow down. You know how to do this._

Bloof lowered their hand from their throat. Released their jaw. Tried to drop their shoulders.

In through the nose.

Hold.

<span class="voice-echo">“You said you were fine.”</span>

They turned so sharply that pain caught beneath their ribs.

Nobody was there.

The voice had been theirs. Not similar. Theirs—the slight catch before the first word, the familiar shape of the last. But it had spoken easily, without the desperate effort crowding their chest, and a quieter version of the sentence seemed to finish just behind it.

Bloof tried to ask who was there. Their tongue moved. Their lips shaped the question. They felt the muscles working in their throat.

Nothing reached the space around them.

Then something warm closed over their toes.

Dark red gathered between their claws, thick enough to hold a glossy curve over the tops of their feet. No surface lay beneath it, no pool to step out of. It clung to them alone.

They pulled one knee toward their chest.

The coating followed.

They shook their foot, hard, then kicked until their hip ached. Nothing flew free. The red tightened around their ankle and climbed, pressing into the small spaces beneath their scales.

They reached down. Their fingers sank into it without finding the skin underneath.

For an instant they couldn’t tell how deep their hand had gone. They jerked back, and a heavy strand stretched between wrist and shin. They scraped at it with the other hand. It folded over their claws and filled the spaces between their fingers.

Both hands covered now.

Bloof held them apart, afraid to let them touch anything else. The coating crawled past their wrists. The first dark bands reached their knees.

They tried to call for Seris.

Their mouth shaped her name, and the effort dragged painfully at their throat. They tried again, harder, as though volume were something they could force out by clenching enough muscles.

Silence.

They looked behind them anyway. The red hadn’t changed. No shape came through it. Nobody turned toward the sound they hadn’t made.

They bent their legs, searching for leverage, but their feet dragged through nothing. No floor to plant them on. No wall to brace against. Nothing solid enough to strike and push themself clear.

<span class="voice-echo">“You don’t have control. You never did.”</span>

The words sounded close enough to have formed inside their mouth.

Bloof shook their head. They knew the sequence. They could still do the sequence.

Jaw loose. Shoulders down. Stop fighting every sensation at once. Find one thing they could change.

Their fingers uncurled with difficulty inside the coating. Their shoulders lowered a fraction. For one moment, they waited for the familiar easing.

The red crossed their knees.

Their next inhale hitched. They tried to fold forward, but their legs were drawn together beneath the thickening weight, their tail caught along its length. They pulled until the strain shook through their hips.

Nothing gave.

It reached their waist. Pressure gathered across their stomach and pushed beneath the injured ribs, and pain flashed there, sharp enough to wrench their mouth wide.

They clawed at it. Their fingers vanished against their own torso. They could feel them moving, feel the pull through wrists and elbows, but couldn’t see where their hands ended. They dragged them outward. The red stretched, then drew them back.

They tried to scream.

Their throat worked until it hurt.

No sound.

Bloof reached inward instead, toward the **Stillhold**. **Kaleidos**. A grip beneath their palm. An edge. Something they knew how to use.

The familiar reach found nothing. They tried again, slower, because rushing had to be the problem, and forced their attention past the pressure on their ribs to the motion they knew.

Nothing answered.

_One thing. Please. Just let one thing work._

The red climbed their chest.

Bloof tipped their head back, trying to keep their mouth above it. Their throat drew taut. Warmth pooled in the hollow at its base, then rose beneath their jaw.

They turned their head side to side, frantic, hunting for an angle the substance couldn’t follow. Their breaths had become empty, convulsive pulls. They knew there was no air here. They knew the pulling wasn’t helping.

They couldn’t stop.

The coating touched their lower lip.

Bloof sealed their mouth.

It pressed at the seam. They held their lips shut until their jaw shook, hands straining toward their face and pinned against their chest. They tried to bite down before anything could get between their teeth.

Warm thickness slid behind their lower lip.

Their tongue pushed back.

It spread.

Bloof gagged. The reflex opened their mouth, and more forced its way in, coating their tongue, crowding the space beneath it. It tasted of hot iron, with something sweeter underneath, like blood left to warm. They tried to spit. Their cheeks clenched. Their throat contracted. There was no breath to carry anything out, and the next gag pulled it farther back.

They needed their hands at their mouth. Needed to drag it out. Needed someone to see.

They couldn’t lift an arm.

The red pressed against the back of their tongue. Their body tried to swallow around it, and they fought the movement with a violence that made their neck ache.

Then their own voice spoke, clear and quiet, while their mouth was full.

<span class="voice-echo">"Why not just let go?"</span>

Bloof jerked hard enough to feel the strain from shoulders to knees.

The substance rose over their cheeks.

They shut one lower eye as warmth pressed into its corner and kept the other turned upward. The red filled their ears, and the small sounds of their struggle dulled: the scrape of their tongue, the useless working of their throat, the tight click of their jaw.

*DO. SOMETHING.*

Their head could barely turn.

They searched upward with the eye they had left.

No ceiling. No opening. Nothing waiting beyond the red.

It slid over their upper eyes, and the dark above them went absolute. The shut eye vanished beneath it. One lower eye remained, a small oval of sight, and Bloof held it open with an effort so complete that they could feel nothing else.

The red touched its lower lid.

They tried to lift a hand.

It began to close over the eye.





<span class="voice-echo">“<strong>Monster.</strong>”</span>

---

Bloof woke with both hands clamped around their throat.

They dragged air in with a harsh, broken sound. Pain caught beneath their ribs and folded them forward, but something else stung when they moved—a sharp pull beneath their jaw.

Their claws were caught in their skin.

Bloof went still. Then, finger by finger, they forced their hands open. The points came free with small, bright stings. Warmth slid down their neck.

They stared at their fingertips.

Red.

Blood trembled on the tips of their claws. Bloof spread their fingers until the joints pulled, but couldn’t hold them still. They caught one wrist in the other hand and squeezed. The tremor fluttered against their palm.

They wiped their fingers on their sleeve. Fabric dragged over the sticky pads. When they turned the hand over, red still clung in the creases. Each breath caught high in their chest, too small to ease the need for the next.

A swallow pulled at the skin beneath their jaw.

They reached toward it, then stopped with their fingertips hovering close enough to feel the warmth. Even there, they shook. Bloof braced their elbow against their chest and turned the claws away before touching down with the pads.

The first light press stung. They flinched, waited, then felt along the sore skin. Small breaks, wet at the edges. Thin scratches that burned when their fingers passed over them.

Their own blood, fresh and sticky.

*I did that.*

Their next breath dissolved into coughing. They pressed their sleeve against the small wounds and tried to slow down, but every swallow let them feel where the claws had been.

Their shirt clung coldly to their back. Sweat ran from beneath their dreadlocks and mingled with the warmth on their neck. They wanted to wipe it away. They couldn’t bear to put their claws there again.

A curtain shifted beside the bed.

Bloof recoiled against the headboard. One hand rose between their face and the movement; the other caught in the sheet.

Fabric. Dry fabric beneath their fingers.

They gripped it, keeping their claws turned from their palm, and stared into the unfamiliar room. Their mouth stayed open. Air moved through it now, but they couldn’t make themself believe there was enough.

The curtain settled.

Bloof watched it until they could see what had moved it: a narrow gap beneath the window frame, the fabric lifting whenever air slipped through. Beyond it, an engine faded down the street.

Curtains. Window. Basin.

The room Stolas had given them.

They looked toward the door. Shut. The lock was still turned.

“I’m awake.”

Their voice came out rough, but it belonged to the room. They heard it leave their mouth and stop.

Bloof loosened their grip on the sheet. Their legs lay beneath a blanket, not trapped; when they pushed it aside, it fell away. They bent one knee, then the other. Both moved.

They were awake. They knew where they were.

**They were still in Hell.**

Bloof pressed their sleeve beneath their jaw and reached across the bed with their free hand.

“Seris—”

Their fingers met the other pillow. Nobody there but them.

They left their hand there a moment, curled against its edge, before drawing it back.

The phone waited beside the coupler. Bloof picked it up with both hands, elbows tucked against their ribs to steady the screen. Their thumb missed Seris's name once before opening the contact. They didn't check the settings this time. There was nothing left to adjust. There was nothing left to adjust. They watched the call fail, dismissed the message, and tried again.

“Please.”

No ring. Not even the comfort of hearing it go unanswered.

They opened a message instead.

> I’m here.

Bloof stared at the words, then erased them. That told her nothing. They tried again.

> I’m alive. I don’t know how to get back. I didn’t leave you.

They pressed send.

The message sat on the screen with a small failure marker beside it.

Bloof touched it. Retried. Held the phone closer, as though the distance between their face and the glass had something to do with the distance home.

“Just send it.”

Their thumb slipped against the edge of the screen and left a faint smear. They wiped it on the blanket and switched to Bazemir.

He would know what to ask. They wouldn’t have to explain it properly the first time. He would wait while they found the words.

The call failed.

Bloof lowered the phone and covered their face with one hand. Their dreadlocks fell forward, catching against their fingers. They pressed their palm over their eyes until sparks shifted behind the lids.

They wanted someone else to decide what happened next. Not forever. Just long enough to stop hurting. Long enough to take a breath without having to supervise it.

When they lowered their hand, the contact list had returned.

Mara’s photograph was there.

Relief rose so quickly that Bloof’s thumb was already moving before they remembered.

They stopped above the call button.

For several seconds, they couldn’t look away from it. The phone still offered the option. Her name was still spelled correctly. The photograph was the one she had chosen herself, not some solemn image selected afterward.

The tremor worsened. Bloof lowered the phone into their lap before it could slip.

“*Oh, Mom*.”

The words barely carried.

Bloof locked the screen and set the phone down carefully. They kept their hand over it until they were sure they wouldn’t pick it straight back up.

Then they moved their feet to the floor.

Cool boards. Weight beneath their heels. A solid edge of mattress against the backs of their legs.

They could work with that.

They stood, steadied themself on the bedpost, and crossed to the basin. A clean cloth replaced their damp sleeve beneath their jaw. They pressed it there gently and watched the small spots of blood spread, rather than looking at their reflection.

“Dream,” they said. “That was a dream.”

The cloth was white. The water stayed clear except where they rinsed the blood away. No thick red coating. No resistance when they lifted their hand.

They set the cloth down and went to the door.

The handle stopped against the lock.

Good.

Bloof released it, then tried it once more.

“I checked it.”

Their fingers stayed around the metal.

“I already checked.”

They made themself let go.

Back at the bed, they sat with both feet planted and their hands open on their thighs. **Vaelith** first. They knew where to begin.

In through the nose. A small breath; their ribs would tolerate that.

Hold.

Out through the teeth.

Their shoulders eased. They followed the next breath, then another, letting the familiar sequence give their attention somewhere narrow to rest.

Their fingers still trembled against their thighs.

The warmth beneath their sternum did not recede.

They reached the hold and lost track of how long they had been there. Their chest tightened. They let the breath out too fast, then tried to correct it with an inhale that caught painfully on the way in.

“Wait.”

No one was asking them to hurry.

They began again.

Somewhere in the quiet, Seris would be finding the number still in her contacts. She would press it, just as Bloof had. She would hear whatever a failed call sounded like from that side and try again, because she wouldn’t know there was no point.

Bazemir might tell her to wait. To look properly. Not to assume.

_They’ll think I’m gone._

They reached for the phone. The unsent message to Seris was still there.

> I didn’t leave you.

Bloof pressed the failure marker again.

Nothing changed.

The minutes went by without anything to mark them, and the room became harder to sit in. Too quiet between the sounds outside. Too much time to hear their own breathing. They looked at the door and couldn’t remember whether the last check had come before or after the message.

Their feet shifted toward it.

“No. Stay here.”

They set the phone beside the coupler, squared its edges, and pulled their hand away.

“I’m fine.”

The words brought back the voice’s cadence so precisely that Bloof shut their mouth.

Nothing had spoken. They knew that. It was only the memory of how easily their own voice had said it.

They tried a different sentence.

“I know how to do this.”

Their hands settled on the mattress, fingers spread, shoulders down. One breath. Another. The pressure beneath their ribs seemed to rise against each measured exhale. They tried not to watch for it, but their attention kept returning there, waiting for the easing that would tell them the method was working.

It didn’t come.

Bloof bowed their head. Their fingers tightened in the bedding.

“Just give me a minute.”

Some unmeasured stretch later, a sharp smell interrupted them.

They lifted their head, searching toward the door, then the basin. Nothing there.

The odor grew stronger.

Bloof looked down.

The sheet beneath their hand was turning brown.

Bloof snatched their hand away.

The brown patch held the shape of their palm. Near its center, the fabric had blackened, a thin thread of smoke lifting from between the impressions of their fingers.

For a moment, they waited for it to disappear.

Then they turned their hand over.

Red light traced their fingers.

It ran beneath the scales toward their wrist, vanished under the clean dressing, and showed again above it. Bloof pulled their sleeve back. The lines were unmistakable. **Wrath.**

*My arm hasn’t looked like that in—*

They stopped the thought before it finished.

They spread their fingers. Closed them. Spread them again.

The glow remained.

Heat crowded beneath their skin, too close to the surface. Their sleeve dragged against their forearm, and they flinched. They pulled it clear with their other hand, only to find red threading that wrist, too.

A small, dry crackle came from the bed.

Water. The basin.

They crossed the room too fast, knocked their knee against the stand, and caught the basin with both hands. Water slopped over the edge. They carried it back, held it above the scorch, and tipped enough onto the sheet to soak through.

The hiss made them jerk. They nearly dropped the basin.

“Easy,” they said, though nothing about their voice was. “Just—easy.”

They lowered it to the floor. A little water ran across the boards beneath it.

The smoke stopped.

Bloof leaned close enough to check the dark folds. No orange point. No fresh curl of smoke. They wet the edge once more with a corner of the sheet and waited.

It stayed wet.

Their hands were still glowing.

They held them away from the mattress and backed onto the clear patch of floor beside it.

Feet down. Both heels. Unclench the jaw.

They knew the order. **Vaelith** was more than breathing. The count gave them something to follow while they drew the output back, easing it toward the boundary they had learned to keep. It was an internal motion so well practiced that they rarely had to think through each part.

Tonight, they thought through all of it.

Their shoulders lowered. Their fingers stopped curling toward their palms. Red dimmed along the nearest knuckle, and Bloof fixed on that small change with such fierce relief that they lost the next breath.

The light brightened again.

“No. Again.”

They reset their feet and tried smaller breaths, since their ribs refused the deeper kind. For two counts, the pressure eased. Then heat tightened across their sternum, and their arms drew in before they caught the movement. Their tail pressed against the back of one leg, trembling. A faint red line showed above their collar. When they tugged the fabric away, even that light touch stung through the overheated skin.

“This is supposed to work.”

It came out louder than they intended.

They glanced at the door, lowered their head, and tried again. Not harder. Harder was the wrong response. They knew that, too.

Knowing it did not stop their shoulders from locking.

They made themself release them. The glow faded slightly.

Bloof waited.

It returned.

_How much more was I supposed to practice?_

They had done the work. They were doing it now.

Bloof looked at the bed. The blackened imprint was still there beneath the water.

Their lower lip trembled. They pressed their teeth against it, then stopped, frightened by how easily pain had become another thing to reach for.

“I know how,” they said.

No one answered.

They tried to say it again, but the words broke apart before they reached their mouth.

A pulse of heat ran down their arms.

Bloof recoiled from their own hands. Their elbows struck their sides, jarring their ribs, and sent them stumbling backward. They stopped short of the wall with their fingers spread in front of them, watching the red brighten beneath each claw.

They had to stop looking at it.

They couldn’t stop looking at it.

Every faint change demanded attention. Brighter near the thumb. Dimmer at the wrist. Had the dressing started smoking? Was that heat from their palm or the room? They turned one hand, searching, and lost the count entirely.

“_Please._”

The word escaped on the exhale.

A floorboard creaked somewhere beyond the door.

Bloof’s head lifted. They listened, holding so still that the tremor in their hands seemed worse.

Another small sound followed. Distant. Perhaps downstairs.

Stolas was in the house.

The thought brought the kitchen back with painful clarity: the spoon placed within reach, his hand withdrawing, the way he had allowed them time to answer.

He might come if they asked.

Bloof looked at the bell.

It sat beside the phone, small and silver, exactly where he had said it would be.

They took a step toward it.

Their hand lifted.

“Sto—”

They stopped.

The red in their fingers reflected along the bell’s polished side.

He would hear it. He would come upstairs. They would have to open the door and explain, and he would look at their hands, at the ruined sheet, at whatever was showing on their face.

He might take their arm.

Bloof’s throat tightened around the scratches beneath their jaw. For an instant, they felt again the helpless certainty of being held, unable to pull free. They jerked their hand back before touching the bell.

Stolas hadn’t hurt them.

They knew that.

He might know what to do. He was a prince. He had caught their wrist with scarcely a movement. Perhaps this would be simple for him.

Their eyes went to the wet, blackened cloth.

And if it wasn’t?

Bloof could picture him standing where the bed was now. Close enough to offer a hand. Close enough for the next release.

_He let me stay._

Their face crumpled before they could stop it. They turned away from the bell, breathing through their open mouth, and pressed the heel of one hand against their forehead.

The heat made them pull it away.

There was nowhere to put their hands. Nowhere to lean. They stood in the middle of a room someone had prepared for them and couldn’t trust themself to touch it.

“Not here,” they whispered. “No. No! Please, not here.”

The door remained shut.

Bloof looked from it to the curtains.

The hallway would take them deeper into the house. Past other rooms. Toward him.

The window led outside.

They didn’t finish weighing the difference. The idea of distance caught and held when nothing else would: air between them and the walls, between them and Stolas. Somewhere they could lose control without him standing close enough to help.

They crossed the room without looking back. Their jacket stayed on the chair. The phone and coupler lay beside the untouched bell, and neither crossed their mind.

---

The curtain dragged aside under an unsteady hand. Glass threw back the red lines along their fingers, then their face: eyes wide, mouth parted, dreadlocks stuck damply to their cheeks. Beyond the reflection lay rooftops and open space.

Bloof lifted the latch and shoved the window outward. It opened a few inches and stopped with a hard click. They pushed again. The gap widened just enough to admit cold air before something pulled it short.

Their fingers fumbled along the frame for a metal arm beneath the sash, something to lift, or fold, or press. Heat drew their skin tight beneath the black fabric of their tank top, and the red along their forearms brightened against the glass. Their hand wouldn’t fit the mechanism.

“Come on.”

Air touched their face through the gap. Below lay the garden, an empty stretch of path beyond the plants.

They hauled the sash toward them to take the pressure off the catch. Their grip slipped. They caught it again, leaving a darkened patch beneath their palm, and shoved.

Wood cracked beside the hinge. The frame twisted, and the pane broke with a sharp report that filled the room.

Glass struck the sill. More fell into the garden, ticking against stone.

Bloof stood with their arm through the broken frame, hand still shaking. Red traced the exposed skin above their collar now, bright enough to color the underside of their jaw.

Footsteps sounded beyond the door.

“Bloof?”

_Stolas._

The pressure beneath their sternum climbed. They tried to draw it inward, but the effort broke when the door handle moved.

“Are you hurt?”

They tried to answer. What came out was a breath.

The handle stopped against the lock.

Bloof ducked their horns under the frame and shifted sideways through the opening. A claw snagged on the wood; they wrenched it free and crouched outside on the narrow ledge, one hand braced against the stone surround.

The cold surface gave a moment of relief beneath their palm. Then it began to warm.

They lifted their hand. Nothing lay beneath their heels but the ledge. The garden was farther down than it had looked.

Behind them, the lock clicked.

The door opened.

“Bloof, wait!”

They looked back before they could stop themself.

_I can't._

Stolas stood in the doorway. His gaze crossed the bed, then found the red glow around the window. He came forward, one hand lifting.

“Stay there. Let me—”

_I CAN'T._

Bloof pushed off the ledge.

Their wings sprouted from their back and snapped open in the cold air. One clipped the wall; then they were clear and dropping, stomach lurching, and Bloof hammered out the first downstroke with more strength than coordination.

Pain tore through their ribs. The red along their sides flared and held brighter than before.

The garden swung beneath them. A second stroke carried them over the path, a third toward the wall. They searched for the count between wingbeats, but every breath belonged to the flight first, and their chest never emptied enough to begin.

Their name reached them again from the house.

Below, a guard stepped into the path and looked up. Another voice called from the gate.

Bloof beat upward instead of turning.

Cold air stripped the sweat from their face. It should have helped. Beneath their skin, the heat kept gathering across their shoulders while their hands curled against their chest. They forced their fingers open. The house fell behind. They fixed on the nearest rooftop and climbed until they cleared it.

Only then did they look ahead.

The walled grounds gave way to close-set roofs. Farther on, the matching shopfronts broke into taller buildings, broad signs, and windows lit in different colors. Streets opened between them, every one unfamiliar.

They had put distance between themself and Stolas.

**Wrath** was still building.

For a few wingbeats, there was room. Bloof held their hands away from their body and let the air pass between their fingers. Nothing close enough to scorch. No one reaching for them. The cold on their face gave them something simple to feel.

They only needed somewhere to land.

Their next downstroke caught beneath the ribs. One wing finished before the other, and the turn swung them toward a row of chimney pots. Bloof corrected with a hard tilt and passed close enough to hear the air rush against brick.

Too close.

They leveled out, breathing through their mouth. Heat pressed across their chest beneath the damp tank top. The red along their arms stayed bright, and holding it back kept dragging their attention from the roofs.

They tried to separate the tasks. Fly first. Keep clear. Find an opening.

A lit billboard rose ahead. Bloof banked around its edge, harder than necessary. The movement pulled their injured side tight and shortened the next wingbeat. They cleared the sign but lost height beyond it, dropping toward the street.

Headlights slid beneath them.

They pulled up.

Their upper eyes opened.

Heat and magical pressure crowded into the view: bodies behind windows, engines beneath metal, a bright concentration behind a shopfront they passed too quickly to understand. Everything demanded a second look. Bloof tried to narrow their attention to the nearest roof, but the street stayed painfully distinct, every moving shape another thing they might hit.

_Somewhere empty. Just somewhere empty._

They could no longer tell whether they were choosing turns or only surviving them.

A gap opened between two buildings ahead and to the right: a narrow side street, dimmer than the main road, closed shopfronts along one side. No moving vehicles. No obvious crowd.

Bloof angled toward it.

The first part of the descent was easy enough that their shoulders loosened. They let themself lose height, eyes on a bare stretch of pavement beyond an awning.

Then they saw how fast the walls were rising around them.

They flared their wings to brake.

Pain struck through their ribs. Their body folded toward it before they could stop the reflex, one wing drawing in while the other caught the air.

The turn swung them at the shopfront.

Bloof saw the projecting sign and tried to pull clear.

Its outer edge caught their shoulder.

Metal rang. The impact spun them sideways and tore their sight from the pavement. Their wing raked across the sign’s support, and they dragged it tight before it could snag.

The awning came up beneath them.

Canvas struck their back, held for an instant, then ripped under their weight. Bloof threw their hands out through the collapsing fabric. Their palms hit pavement, their elbows buckled, and their hip followed with a jolt that drove the last breath out of them.

They slid a short distance and stopped.

For several seconds, they stayed where they had landed, one knee drawn up, hands flat against the ground. Above them, the damaged sign swung on its bracket. A loose strip of canvas dragged across their shoulder.

Bloof shoved it away.

Their first breath hurt. The second came more easily, though it shook on the way out. Grit stung their palms. Their shoulder throbbed where it had met the sign, and a small warmth tracked from the edge of their brow toward their cheek.

They pushed onto one elbow.

“Anyone—”

The word caught. They swallowed, felt the scratches at their throat, and tried again.

“Did I hit anyone?”

They searched beneath the torn awning, then along the pavement past their feet. No one trapped under the fabric. No shape lying where they had slid. No smoke curling from the shopfront.

Their wings drew in behind them, trembling. Bloof held still long enough to feel both respond, then looked at their hands.

Red still traced their fingers.

The pavement beneath them wasn’t smoking.

---

By the time the sign’s swing had shortened to a slow creak, Bloof had made it as far as sitting.

They had worked one knee beneath them, paused while the ache in their hip eased, and shifted until the wall held their back. Their wings folded with them, sore from exertion but answering without the catch of a new injury.

They should get up.

Bloof looked toward the end of the street.

They stayed where they were.

A few minutes ago, every thought had demanded movement. Out of the room. Away from the house. Clear the roof. Miss the sign. Now they could see the next few steps they needed to take and couldn’t begin them.

Their hands rested loosely between their knees. The red along their fingers had dulled.

They turned one palm upward and watched it.

Light still threaded their wrist, faint enough now that the gray beneath showed clearly. As they waited, another section faded. The tight heat under their skin eased until the air could feel cold again.

They hadn’t done anything differently. Hadn’t completed the count or found the familiar internal hold. Their last attempt had been somewhere above the rooftops, lost between one painful wingbeat and the next.

Beneath their sternum, **Wrath** remained unsettled.

They looked away from their hand.

The awning lay across the pavement in a torn heap. Someone would have to repair it. The sign, too. And the window at Stolas’s house.

_I'll leave a note. “Sorry, was glowing.”_

They tried to remember which direction they had come from.

A roof. A billboard. The turn they’d taken too sharply.

Their attention stalled there.

Stolas had called their name.

Bloof lowered their head. A little shame reached them, enough to make their mouth tighten, but none of the force that would have sent them scrambling a few minutes earlier. They wanted to explain that they hadn’t meant to frighten him. They wanted him to know the bed wasn’t still burning.

Their hand went to the pocket where their phone should have been.

Bare fingers met the fabric of their trousers.

The jacket was on the chair.

They remembered it clearly now: the phone beside the coupler, the silver bell within reach. All of it still upstairs.

Their hand fell back.

_I could have rung it._

They sat with that until the sign gave another metallic creak.

Footsteps approached and stopped a few paces off.

Bloof took a moment to lift their head. Their gaze found black trouser legs first, a chain hanging at one thigh, gray fingers resting on a hip. Higher: a broad sharklike muzzle beneath dark curls, pale strands near the jaw, purple eyes.

Those eyes went to the torn awning, followed the damage up to the sign, then came down to Bloof. They moved over the wings folded behind their shoulders, the horns, the scales along their arms.

The last faint line of red at their wrist disappeared.

Bloof knew they should say something. An apology, perhaps. Their lips parted, but they couldn't gather the words quickly enough.

Her eyes narrowed.

<span class="voice-vendetta">“Couldn't find a street big enough for your fucking entrance, your highness?”</span>
