The knife was still in her hand. She dragged it down their back, a bright, burning line from shoulder to hip, and Bloof snarled into her throat and fucked her harder. Pain and heat folded together until they couldn't tell them apart. The pull of Vaelith flared once, a hot, bright reflex, and they ignored it so completely that it felt like laughter.

_Hers. Hers. Her taste. Her voice. Hers, hers, hers—_

"Look at me," Seris panted.

They lifted their head. Her face was flushed and wet with sweat, her lips swollen and red, her eyes fever-bright and wild. She looked nothing like the cool, patient beastfolk who had sat across from them an hour ago. She looked like she'd been taken apart and was glad of it.

"I want you to feel this," she said, voice rough. "Every time you sit down tomorrow. Every time you put on a shirt. I want you to remember who did this to you."

"I'll remember."

"Say who."

"_You, Seris._"

She kissed them, biting, tasting blood, her mouth hot and ruthless. Bloof groaned into it and slammed into her, and her whole body bowed away from the wall.

"Close," she gasped. "Close, I'm close, don't—"

Her tail uncoiled from their thigh.

It slid up their back, sleek and strong and warm, and drew a slow line over their shoulder. Bloof felt it before they understood it. Then it wrapped around their throat and squeezed.

Not hard. Just enough.

Their breath caught. Their pulse hammered against the coil. The world went bright at the edges.

_Oh._

Their hips snapped forward with a savage, uncontrolled force. Seris cried out, a raw, wild sound, and the knife slipped from her fingers and rang against the floor. Her hands flew up and caught their face, cupping their jaw in both palms, and she held them there with her forehead pressed to theirs.

"_Bloof,_" she gasped. "Bloof, look at me. Look at me."

They looked. Their eyes were glazed and burning and fixed on her.

"That's it." Her thumbs stroked their cheekbones, a tender gesture that didn't match the savage squeeze of her tail or the way her heels dug into their back. "That's my good dragon. Come with me. Come inside me. **Now**."

They shattered.

It hit them in a long, searing wave, a pulse that tore from the base of their spine and shuddered through every inch of them. A broken sound ripped out of their throat, muffled by the coil around it. Seris came a heartbeat later with a scream she didn't try to stifle, her whole body clenching around them in long, helpless waves, her head thrown back against the wall and her nails digging bloody crescents into their jaw. They spilled into her in heavy, scalding pulses, and she took all of it, shaking, gasping their name.

The tail loosened.

Air flooded their lungs. Bloof's legs gave out, and they held her up with the last of their strength as the wall slid cold against her back, and neither of them let go.

The wall was cold against Seris's back, and Bloof's forehead was hot against her collarbone. Neither of them spoke for a long time.

Eventually her fingers moved in their hair, slow and absent.

"Bloof."

"Mm."

"Put me down before your legs do it for you."

A tired huff left them, close to a laugh. They lowered her carefully, one hand under her thigh, the other braced against the stone, until her feet found the rug. She swayed. Bloof caught her elbow without thinking, and she let them.

She didn't let go afterward, either. She stood there with her palm resting against their chest and her head tilted, looking at them the way she might look at a map she was memorizing.

"Are you hurt?" she asked.

It wasn't the teasing question. It was the other one, the quiet one, and she asked it with the same flat attention she'd given the knife earlier.

Bloof took stock. Their back burned in long, bright lines. Their throat ached where her tail had pressed. Their lip was split and tasted of copper. Their knees weren't entirely reliable.

"No," they said. "Not like that."

"Look at me."

They did.

"Not like that," Seris repeated, and watched their face until she was satisfied. "Good. Come."

She took them by the wrist and led them down the hall.

---

The bath was deep, black stone, wide enough for two, and the water ran hot and steaming. Seris poured something from a glass bottle into it, and the room filled with a smell like cedar and smoke. Candlelight caught on the tiled wall. Past the narrow window, the city lights had thinned to a slow, distant glitter.

She eased in first, then held out a hand.

Bloof hesitated at the edge. The cuts down their back stung at the first touch of heat, and they hissed through their teeth.

"Come here," Seris said.

They settled between her knees with their back to her chest. The water closed over their shoulders, and the sting sharpened, then dulled. Seris's arms slid around their ribs, careful of the cuts, and her chin came to rest on their shoulder.

For a while they just sat there.

Steam curled off the surface. Blood bloomed pale pink in the water around their forearm and thinned to nothing. Seris dipped a cloth and drew it down their spine, slow, almost reverent, cleaning each line like she wanted to remember where it was.

"This one's deeper than I meant," she said, frowning at the long slash across their back.

"It's fine."

"It's not fine. It's just yours."

_That's the part that gets me._

Bloof closed their eyes.

They sat in the quiet and felt the water move around them, felt the cloth glide over their skin, felt the steady rise and fall of her breath against their neck. Their pulse slowed. Their body, which had been wound tight for weeks, loosened a little at a time.

And then, slowly, the rest of it caught up.

The wall. The sound they had made. The way they had picked her up like she weighed nothing and held her there and _begged_. Heat crawled up the back of their neck that had nothing to do with the bath. They pulled their knees up, hunched forward, and pressed the heel of one hand against their eyes.

_Oh gods. Oh no. I said please. I said please so many times._

"Bloof?"

"Nothing."

"That wasn't nothing." Her fingers found their chin and tried to tilt it. They tucked it down. "Hey. Look at me."

"Gods, I can't."

"You've been looking at me for two hours."

A strangled sound left them, half a laugh and half a groan. They folded further forward until their forehead nearly touched their knees. Their ears had gone flat against their skull.

"I don't _do_ that," they said into their arm. "I don't. I don't act like that. I don't grab people, and I don't—" Their voice cracked. "I said whatever you wanted. I said I was _yours._ I bit you. I don't know who that was."

"It was you."

"It wasn't."

"It was." Seris's voice came quiet and certain, right against their ear. She wrapped both arms around them and pulled them back until their spine met her chest again, and she didn't let them hide. "Every word. Every sound. I was there."

She let that sit, and then the corner of her mouth must have curved, because her tone lightened.

"Though I'll admit," she murmured, "I've never seen you look so pleased with yourself. _Put me down before your legs do it for you._ And you, carrying me across the room like—"

"_Seris._"

"I'm only saying, it was impressive."

Bloof made a sound like a kettle. She laughed into their shoulder, low and warm, and then her arms tightened and the laughter slipped away.

"Hey." She pressed her cheek against the side of their head. "I'm not laughing at you. I'm not going to. Listen to me."

They listened.

"There's nothing to be embarrassed about. Not with me." Her thumb stroked slow circles on their ribs, careful of the cuts. "You think I'd let anyone else see you like that? You think I'd let anyone else _hear_ you like that?" A pause. Her voice dropped. "That was mine. All of it. Every sound you made was for me, and I'm not sharing it."

Heat flooded Bloof's face again, but this time something else rode in with it. It loosened a knot in their chest they hadn't known was there.

"So you can stop apologizing," she said. "To me, of all beasts. You're allowed to want things. You're allowed to want them this much, as long as you want them here." Her lips touched their temple. "*With me*."

Bloof breathed out, long and shaky. Slowly, they let their hands fall away from their face and tipped their head back against her shoulder.

"That's better," Seris said, softly. "There you are."

The water lapped around them. Bloof's eyes drifted shut.

_That should bother me more. How much I want to hear her say it again._

The whisper of Vaelith had been right there the whole night. A single thought and the cuts would have been gone. They had not reached for it once.

_Funny. That's the only part that scares me._

Behind them, Seris's thumb found the pulse at the side of their throat, where the bruise was darkening, and pressed lightly.

"You're thinking too loudly again," she murmured.

"Sorry."

"Don't be." Her lips brushed the edge of their ear. "What is it?"

Bloof looked at their forearm, at the thin red line drifting under the water.

"I don't want them healed," they said.

A pause.

"I know." Seris's fingers traced the line along their wrist, lightly, and the water rippled around her hand. "You'd have most of them gone by tomorrow anyway. Your body does that for you." Her voice dropped. "And if that's too slow, you can reach for Vaelith whenever you like. They'd be gone in a breath."

"I know."

"I also have more tonic. It'd leave you smooth by morning. No marks at all."

"No."

The answer came without hesitation, which surprised them both.

"That's a sudden decision."

"I want to keep them. For now." Bloof's ears dipped. "Is that stupid?"

"Yes." She turned their wrist over and pressed her thumb along the cut, slowly. "I'm not saying no."

Her voice had changed. The softness thinned, and underneath it came something harder, measuring, almost proprietary. Her hand slid up to their ribs and pressed against the edge of another cut, just hard enough to make their breath catch.

"But you don't heal them without telling me," she said. "Not with Vaelith, not by hiding it. They fade on their own, or I decide when they're done." Her lips brushed their ear. "And if I decide, you'll drink the tonic. Understood?"

Bloof opened their mouth to argue.

They didn't.

_You should say no. You said you'd come to her for this, and you did, and now she's deciding what happens to your body like—_

They felt her arms tighten around them, felt the quick, fierce press of her chin against their shoulder.

_Like she's afraid to lose them._

"Understood," they said.

She kissed their shoulder, just above a thin red line, and said nothing else.

The candles burned lower. The water cooled by degrees, and neither of them moved. Bloof leaned back into her and listened to her breathe, and counted their cuts without meaning to, and wondered how many more it would take before the number stopped feeling like a promise.
