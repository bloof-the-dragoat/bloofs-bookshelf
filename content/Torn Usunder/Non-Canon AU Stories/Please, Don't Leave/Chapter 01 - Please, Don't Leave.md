> [!caution] Content note 
> Body horror, physical restraint, and fear of captivity.

> [!info] Reader notes 
> **Wrath.** is one of the seven Calamities, world-altering forces of nature, each carried by a living host. It turns pain and strong emotion into raw force, and it makes the host’s feelings harder to contain. Bloof carries Wrath.
> 
> **The Crown Eyes.** The Crown Eyes are canon, but they have not yet appeared in the mainline story. They are an ancient draconic sensory trait, not prophecy or a Calamity. They let a bearer sense heat, magical energy, and the tension in a body just before it moves, so danger shows up before it takes shape. Bloof’s are the second pair of eyes above their ordinary ones.

# Chapter 01 — Please, Don't Leave

<span class="voice-forest">“Come back.”</span>

It is said gently, the way someone calls after a stranger who has dropped a glove.

Bloof does not turn around.

They hit the next stretch of bare earth hard enough to throw mud up the backs of their trousers, cold and wet against their calves. Fire gathers through their legs in a tight, practiced compression, heat pooling beneath the soles of their boots. They let it go. **Cinderstep** hurls them forward between two trunks along a single committed line, with no room left to change their mind. Bark streaks past close enough to feel. Their shoulder clips it on the way through and the impact spins them half around, but their tail snaps out for balance and the next stride lands beneath them instead of across them. A palm slaps a trunk. Their boots plow furrows through the leaf litter. The heat in their legs gutters out.

Behind them, something goes through the space they have just left.

Not around the trees.

Between one footfall and the next comes the crack of splitting wood, then a long, fibrous tearing, like a seam being ripped by something that does not care about the cloth. A branch lashes the back of Bloof’s neck. They duck the next and drive on through wet leaves that slap cold across their muzzle.

The voice had sounded farther away a moment ago.

Their upper eyes are open and straining. Heat should show them everything: their own breath leaving in ragged clouds, the furnace of their legs, the brief print of their palm on bark, already fading. The fire they leave behind glows against cold soil. But the forest around them is a single temperature from root to canopy, cold and wet, and nothing in it is warmer than rain.

To the upper pair, Bloof is alone in the dark.

Yet something follows so closely that displaced air lifts the loose hem of their olive overshirt.

Bloof drops their weight and cuts sharply downhill. A dark length passes through the branches where their head had been, too fast to resolve into anything but an interruption. Leaves shower over their shoulders. Behind them, the ground is struck once, heavily. They feel it through their boot soles.

Then the movement resumes.

There are no footfalls. A rush through brush. A dragging sound. Three quick impacts, too close together. Half a breath of silence before another trunk shudders.

Bloof keeps their mouth shut and breathes through their nose until the cold begins to burn.

A few minutes ago, they had stood still.

That is what their mind keeps snagging on: damp earth through the soles of their boots, unfamiliar trees, and a quiet so complete they had mistaken it for deafness. A weight they had carried for so long they had stopped feeling it had lifted from behind their ribs all at once, the way a held breath leaves. They had reached inward, carefully, the way a tongue checks the place where a tooth used to be.

Nothing answered.

_Don’t enjoy it. It’s probably a trap._

They had enjoyed it anyway. One foolish, wonderful breath with no leash, no itch behind the skull that no amount of scratching could reach, no hook beneath the ribs tightening whenever they tried to breathe too deep.

It had been a new feeling. Peace, in a place that had never heard of them.

Then something beyond the trees had said hello.

Now, they reach for **Wrath** again as they vault a fallen limb. The same empty distance answers: no resistance, no heat gathering, nothing to close a hand around. It is like grabbing for a railing in the dark and finding only air. Their ordinary fire still moves readily beneath their skin. Past it, there is nothing to pull.

Their jaw tightens. They stop reaching.

Ahead, the ground levels around a stand of thin, tightly packed trunks, too narrow for a straight sprint. Bloof angles for the left edge, plants a foot against an exposed root, and twists their upper body without surrendering any forward momentum.

Fire leaves their hand in a low, spreading sweep.

Orange light strips the woods bare. Wet bark steams where the flame passes and refuses to catch. Ferns crushed flat. Moss hanging, dripping.

And between two trunks, a tall shape already sliding out of the light.

The flame catches a black strand trailing behind it. For an instant the strand draws taut, glossy and thick as a wrist, its skin puckering in the heat. Beneath that skin, tissue contracts the way a muscle flinches.

It parts with a sound like a soaked rope tearing.

Bloof is moving before the severed length hits the ground. The flare gives them a route through the saplings and they take it, a shorter push of heat driving through their legs. Behind them the cut end thrashes, the fire dying on it with a hiss, and a wet scraping follows: the sound of the severed length being gathered back up.

Then the pursuit accelerates.

<span class="voice-forest">“Why are you running?”</span>

From their right.

Bloof turns left so hard that their boot skids. Their claws catch a trunk and bark peels away beneath them. On the far side of the tree, a pale shape slips past.

The words had come evenly, almost kindly. Only the last syllable stretched, thinning into a high, trembling whine that stays in the air after the voice is gone, like a wet fingertip drawn around the rim of a glass.

_Don’t answer. Don’t be polite._

Their ears flatten.

They cannot keep doing this blind.

Bloof cuts toward a broad trunk, jumps, and catches a low branch. It bows under their weight. They haul themself up, boots scrabbling for bark, and scramble into the next fork. Overhead, a gap in the leaves lets through a patch of sky no wider than their hand.

They climb for it.

Their shoulders grind against crossing limbs. They work one through, then the other, testing each gap before committing to it. Above the first layer of leaves waits a second ceiling of branches, woven so tightly that it feels less like a canopy than a lid. Pale strips hang among them, long and limp.

Below, the brush stops moving.

Bloof freezes with one hand clamped around a branch and their boots braced against the trunk. For a moment there is only their own breathing, loud inside their skull, and the slow drip of water.

Then the branch beneath their hand begins to bend upward.

Not under their weight. Against it.

A narrow black cord slides over the bark beside their fingers, cool and unhurried.

<span class="voice-forest">“<strong>Stop.</strong>”</span>

Bloof lets go.

They drop through the fork and land badly on their left foot. The jolt hammers up through the shin. They roll with it, shoulder grinding through wet leaf litter, and come upright already gathering fire.

The cord comes down after them.

They hit it with a concentrated burst, and this time they watch it fail. The black rind blisters and splits. Underneath is not wood, not entirely, but pale root-fibers wound around a slick central hollow. The cord peels back from the flame like fingers curling from a stove.

The end breaks off. What remains splits into two thinner lengths.

Bloof abandons the tree.

_Fine. Ground it is. Ground has never grabbed anyone._

They run on shorter pulses now, enough to gain speed where the earth allows. A full **Cinderstep** would commit them to one line, and in a forest that will not hold still, that is a gamble they cannot afford. Heat thickens beneath their clothes. Their thighs begin to tremble between bursts, and each breath scrapes on the way down.

The forest smells of torn green stems and, underneath, a sweetness like flowers left too long in old water.

A forked trunk rises ahead. Bloof aims for the gap beside it.

Their own fire glows inside a fresh scar in the bark.

They nearly miss the next step.

A shallow black arc cuts across the trunk at shoulder height. Above it, a broken branch hangs by a ribbon of wood. At its base, in the mud, lies the strand they burned through, curled and blackened and very still.

_I burned this tree. I burned this exact tree._

Bloof looks left, then right. There is no time to choose carefully. They take the steeper slope, driving toward a pale stretch between the roots where there might be room to build speed.

Behind them, the broken branch drops.

<span class="voice-forest">“Help me.”</span>

It sounds close enough to touch their shoulder.

Bloof pushes harder.

A low branch snags their overshirt and tears free with a ripping sound. They shove through a wall of fern, skid around a moss-covered stone, and feel the earth give beneath their next step.

Something closes around their right ankle.

The grip is so sudden that the rest of their body keeps running without it. Their leg is snatched straight out behind them. Their hands hit first, claws raking through leaves into soil, but the impact still drives the air from their lungs. Their chin strikes the ground hard enough to click their teeth together.

They are dragged backward.

Mud and rotted leaves pack into their collar and up their sleeves. The trouser cuff tears. The trapped ankle twists, and pain bites up the outside of the joint like a hot wire.

Bloof rolls onto their side before the next drag can turn the strain into something worse. A tendril runs from their ankle into the ferns, slipped through the gap between boot top and torn cuff and wound tight around the joint. It is cool against bare skin and faintly tacky, and it pulses against the bone with a rhythm that is not theirs.

They hook their claws beneath it.

The surface gives. Their claws sink through cool, yielding tissue, then stop against fibers that slide away from the pressure. Clear fluid beads around the cuts. The severed edges draw together over their fingers the way a wound would close if it were in a hurry.

They wrench their hand free.

Fire gathers in their other palm, held narrow to spare their leg. They press it flat against the coil. A smell of scorched sap rises. The tendril convulses, loosening just enough for two fingers to wedge beneath it.

Above the ferns, a shape shifts.

Bloof looks up.

Something stands on the far side of the moss-covered stone.

For one suspended instant, the details arrive separately: a narrow face, pale fur at the throat, dark eyes catching the firelight. Fine antennae spread from her brow. A long, greenish shape hangs behind each shoulder, folded close like a cloak dragged through rain. Her clothes are dark and carefully fitted. One sleeve has been repaired with a line of pale stitching.

To their upper eyes she is not there at all. The clearing is cold from stone to fern, and she is exactly as cold.

Then something behind her lifts.

The folded shapes part, and the darkness between them fills with moving cords.

Bloof tears at the restraint and tries to drag their knee beneath them.

She comes over the stone in a single, boneless rush.

The impact drives them flat. Her weight lands across their upper body, heavier than anything that narrow should be, and the air leaves them in a short, ugly sound. Their head strikes the leaves. They turn their muzzle just in time to keep the back of a horn from catching against the ground.

Their right arm gets halfway between them.

She catches it.

Her hand is cold. Too many small movements pass beneath the skin of her fingers as they close around Bloof’s wrist, like something turning over in its sleep. Another coil slips beneath their left forearm and draws it outward. They kick with their free leg and connect with something solid at her hip.

Her body yields around the impact.

Not recoils. Yields.

The shape of her waist dents beneath their boot like set gelatin, and rises again after the boot slides away.

A tendril catches their knee. Another lashes their legs together below it, tightening until the injured ankle sends a bright pulse through their whole body. A coil snakes beneath their back and cinches around their torso. Their tail strikes the ground once before a loop traps it near the base.

Bloof tries to turn their hips.

The restraints rob the movement before it becomes leverage.

She leans over them.

Up close, there is still enough of a face to understand. Ivory fur traces her jaw. Her eyes are narrow and reflective, fixed on all four of theirs. One antenna sweeps forward and brushes their brow with a touch so light it is worse than the grip. The sweet, waterlogged smell of the forest is stronger here, and underneath it runs a green, raw edge, like a snapped stem.

Her mouth is closed.

Something inside her cheek moves toward it.

Bloof holds still. They have little choice.

A thin line opens from the corner of her mouth to the base of one antenna. At first it looks like a cut. Clear fluid wells along it. Then the line continues downward through her cheek, curves beneath her chin, and meets another seam opening on the opposite side.

Her face loosens.

The lower part separates first, peeling outward with a small, adhesive sound. Fine strands stretch between the parting surfaces. They hold for a moment, trembling, then break one after another against Bloof’s muzzle, cold as threads of spit.

The upper sections lift.

What looked like the front of her face folds back into four wet, articulated plates.

Each inner edge carries teeth.

Small ones crowd the outer seams, fine and bright as splinters of glass. Farther in, they grow long enough to overlap, set at different angles, some pointing inward, some buried sideways in the pale lining as though another row is being pushed up beneath them.

There is no tongue where Bloof expects one.

A folded mass draws back inside the opening, exposing a dark passage that narrows and widens independently of her breath. Along its walls, soft ridges pull against each other. A click sounds, deep inside.

Her eyes remain on them.

They have moved apart with the opening face. The skin around them stretches without tearing.

Bloof’s next breath stops halfway in.

Her shoulders rise. Beneath the repaired fabric, shapes travel outward from her spine. The seams strain, then part along openings already worked into the clothing. Pale supports unfold through them, jointed lengths slick with a thin membrane, and brace against the earth beside Bloof’s head. One sinks into the mud with a sound like a knife entering fruit.

The tendrils tighten.

Her torso makes a slow internal adjustment. Bloof sees it beneath the cloth: a hard ridge sliding down behind her breastbone, the shallow impression of ribs spreading and settling into a new arrangement.

Bloof’s upper eyes find no preparation in any of it. No pattern, no gathering heat, no tension to read before the movement comes. The only warmth in the clearing is the pulse leaving their own restrained hands.

The opening above them widens.

She screams.

The sound strikes their face warm and damp, the only warm thing about her. It begins high, then splits into pitches that scrape against each other. A lower vibration travels through her weight into Bloof’s chest and rattles their ribs. Their ears clamp flat, but the sound is already inside their teeth.

Somewhere within it, a word shows through for one ragged instant.

<span class="voice-forest">“Don’t—”</span>

Then the scream closes over it again.

They pull against the restraints before they can stop themself.

Pain flares through their ankle. The coil around their wrist bites deeper. They stop.

The scream breaks unevenly, one part of it continuing after the rest has fallen silent.

<span class="voice-forest">“Don’t run.”</span>

The words come from the dark passage below her eyes.

A second, softer version follows a fraction behind.

<span class="voice-forest">“Please don’t run.”</span>

Bloof stares up at her. Their mouth opens just enough to breathe.

_Ah. I’m going to die._

Her hand releases their wrist.

For half a second they think there is an opening.

A tendril takes its place, pressing the arm into the ground, and her fingers move to their throat.

Bloof draws their chin down as far as it will go. Her fingertips slip beneath the edge of their jaw anyway. Cold pressure settles beside the working pulse.

They wait for it to close.

It does not.

Her fingers shift a little, finding the beat again.

The four parts of her face lower toward their neck.

Bloof turns their muzzle away. Teeth pass close enough to catch the loose collar of their shirt. One point drags across the fabric and plucks a thread free. Warm fluid touches the fur below their ear and begins to slide.

Air drags in somewhere deep inside her.

The sound is thick and obstructed, a growl with no throat to carry it. The open surfaces quiver around it.

She pauses at their throat.

Bloof feels each beat beneath her fingers. Too fast. Nothing they do to their breathing slows it.

Her free hand settles over their sternum.

The palm spans the upper part of their chest, fingers splayed against the damp charcoal cloth. She presses once, not hard enough to hurt, then holds there.

The growl falters.

Her antennae angle toward them. One trembles against their cheek.

The pressure on their throat lightens.

Bloof does not move. The hand on their chest rises with a shallow breath and falls again. Above it, the opened sections of her face make minute adjustments, teeth shifting past teeth without closing.

<span class="voice-forest">“Still here.”</span>

This time there is only one voice.

She lowers herself farther, and all of her attention goes with her.

It gathers in her hand. The palm over their sternum is less a weight now than a listening: fingers spread, pads pressed to the damp cloth, shifting by fractions as though following something small that keeps trying to slip away. When Bloof’s heart stumbles, her fingers tighten. When it catches itself, they ease. The cold of her palm has begun to take their heat.

Her antennae have bent down toward it. They hover over their chest without touching, tips trembling, and seem to notice nothing else.

Her eyes are no longer on their face. They have drifted to her own hand.

The thick, obstructed sound of her breath comes only in the gaps between their heartbeats. She draws it in between beats and holds it through each one, as though afraid of covering the sound.

The supports beside their head bend slowly. A strand of fluid stretches from the inner edge of her face to their collar and breaks against the cloth. Bloof watches the nearest teeth slide out of sight beyond the edge of their vision.

Whatever she is doing, she is doing it with everything she has.

The restraints remain closed.

Something soft brushes the base of their ear. Then the opened mouth is beside it, close enough that the first breath lifts the fine fur there. Her hand has not moved from their heart. It rises with each beat and settles again, and does not leave.

<span class="voice-forest whisper">“Please, don’t leave.”</span>
