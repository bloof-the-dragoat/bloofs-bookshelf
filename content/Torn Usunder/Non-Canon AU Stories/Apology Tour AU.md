The party was in a house that had clearly once been somebody's nice home.

Bloof stood in the doorway for a second and let the noise hit them. Music thumped through the floorboards. Someone had hung paper bats from the ceiling, someone else had set fire to them a little, and a small crowd of imps was cheering about it. The whole place smelled like cheap sugar, spilled liquor, and smoke.

They'd flown in through Stolas's crystal, as they always did, with a few seconds of cold pressure and then a lurch of air. Halloween was apparently the one night when nobody asked how a dragon-goat had both gotten into Hell and a suburban living room. Bloof had decided not to ask either.

"You *came*!"

Verosika Mayday crossed the room like it was her stage, which, given the party, it more or less was. Her hair was a pink flare and her smile arrived with a smirk already attached. She took one look at Bloof, stopped, and put a hand to her chest.

"Oh, you did *not*."

"I absolutely did," Bloof said. They spread their arms and let the cape fall around them, black on the outside and deep violet inside, with a high gothic collar. Under it, the corset bodice was laced in violet, the lavender skirt was doing its best to swish, and the choker had a little silver bat charm. Bloof's fangs flashed. "What do you think?"

"I think you've got a little vampire thing going and I hate how well it works." Verosika circled them. "The earring. The *ribbon on the horns*. You even did your eyes."

"I'm a professional."

"You're a menace." She poked the choker. "What are you supposed to be?"

"The world's *cutest* bloodsucker."

Verosika laughed so hard she had to lean on their shoulder. Several nearby demons turned to look, and Bloof saw the usual flicker go through the room: the nod, the second glance, the *oh, that's Bloof*. A succubus at the snack table raised her cup. A hulking demon in a witch hat gave a slow, approving thumbs-up.

*Cool high-class demon. Sure. Please nobody ask me to do anything formal.*

"Everyone's thrilled you're here," Verosika said, nudging them toward the drinks. "Which is good for me. Don't tell anyone I said that."

"You're using me for clout."

"A little." She grinned, unashamed. "Mostly I like you. Clout's a bonus."

"That's a yes."

"That's an *I'm not telling you*."

*It's a party. You're allowed to look at a friend.*

*You've been looking at this friend since last spring.*

Stolas had taken off the top hat. That was the first thing Bloof noticed.

"I am *not* drunk," Stolas protested, from somewhere far too high above the crowd.

"He's a little drunk!" Verosika called, and a ripple of laughter went through the room. She pulled him up beside her.

It wasn't polished. It was a drunk, theatrical, wounded lament set to a beat, and every line was about someone who never looked back. It was also, Bloof realized, the first time they'd heard him say any of it *out loud*, to a room, instead of in long, late-night messages that ended with "I'm fine, darling, it's nothing."

*Too late. Three months too late, probably.*

The room was too loud, too hot, and too crowded. Bloof had never loved crowds, and tonight, with a drink in one hand and thirty bodies pressed around them, the Crown Eyes were no help. The heat of a hundred demons made a smear across the edges of their vision. Mana flickered everywhere, bright and meaningless, like static.

That was why it took them so long to notice the *tablecloth*.

It wasn't a good disguise. It was a white sheet with two eyeholes and a very small imp under it, moving through the crowd with a fixed, stubborn purpose. A *very* familiar posture. Short, tense, one shoulder hunched. The way a body leans when it's about to say something stupid.

Bloof's upper eyes cracked open.

The room sharpened. The heat smear cleared into outlines. They saw the small body under the cloth, the quick breathing, the clenched hands. They saw exactly who it was, and exactly who it was following: a tall shape across the room, drifting toward the back hall with a fresh drink.

*You're not fucking serious.*

They watched. They waited. They did not interfere.

---

He had hoped, in a small and foolish part of himself, that the song would be enough. That singing it to a room full of demons would put the thing outside of him where it could be looked at and put away. It had not. His chest still felt like a house with all the windows open and nobody home.

"Stolas."

He closed his eyes. The voice behind him was small, rough, and so familiar that he could have identified it through a wall.

"I'm not doing this tonight, Blitzø."

"It's not a *thing*, I just..." The tablecloth rustled. "I'm not gonna *do* anything. I want to say something. For once. Without you crying."

"You're *kind of* crying."

*That is a very bad apology,* Stolas thought, quite calmly. *That is, in fact, an excuse with a bow on it.*

"You," Stolas said. "*Why are you here? I don't want you here, just, go home please!* Let me not feel so sad."

The warmth in the air eased. Bloof let their upper eyes slide shut, and breathed out through their nose.

*Nobody caught fire. Gold star.*

They turned to Stolas, and all the anger went out of their face like water down a drain. What was left was shy and a little bit crooked.

"Sorry. That got intense."

"It was," Stolas said, with something like wonder, "rather magnificent."

"Yeah?" Bloof's tail twitched. "Okay. Good. Because I'm about to be extremely uncool."

"Oh?"

"Do you want to dance?"

It had been a very long time since anyone had asked him to dance without wanting something from him.

He should have said no. He was a prince of the Ars Goetia, and he was drunk, and he had just been told, quite correctly, that he deserved more than the life he'd been living. "What?"

"Everybody's too busy being stupid to notice you being stupid."

"That," Stolas gasped, laughing, "is a *terrible* philosophy."

"I've got worse. Why was the dragon bad at dancing?"

"Oh, no."

"Because he had *two left feet and a tail!*"

Stolas threw his head back and honked, an actual owl-like honk, loud and undignified. A few demons nearby clapped. Stolas clapped a hand over his beak, mortified and delighted, and Bloof's chest did something absurdly fluttery.

"I'm so sorry," he said. "I don't know what came over me."

"I *will not*."

*I want someone to want me. To see me. To hold me.*

He fled.

*That. Was. A. Kiss.*

Bloof stood in the middle of the dance floor with their hands still half-raised and their entire brain doing the equivalent of a small, polite scream. The crowd closed in around the gap Stolas had left. Somewhere in the distance, Verosika yelled "Go *after* him!" in a voice that carried across three rooms.

Bloof did not need to be told. Well, maybe.

They were fast. Faster than a drunk dragon in platform boots had any right to be. "Stolas."

"Please don't." His voice was muffled. "I've already humiliated myself quite enough."

Stolas looked up at them. The red of his eyes was wet and bright. He opened his beak. He closed it.

Then, very quietly, he said, "No."

Stolas had flown before. He was a Goetic prince; he'd been raised with wings. But he had never been *carried*.

"I'm a great deal taller than you." His voice came out smaller than he meant. "This *can't* be comfortable."

He was. It surprised him, how easily it came. The wind pulled at his plumage and the lights below slid past in streaks, and for the first time all night he could breathe.

Bloof didn't answer right away. Their wings beat in a steady, low rhythm. Stolas's head rested near their collarbone, feathers brushing their jaw.

"All of it," they said finally. "Starting from the middle of your apology."

"Ah."

"You said you wanted someone to care if you stayed or went."

He went still in their arms.

"I do," Bloof said. "I care."

"Bloof..."

You're funny. You're kind to your daughter even when she's furious with you. You grow carnivorous plants and name them. You write me messages at two in the morning and then apologize for taking my time, and I have never once wanted you to *stop*."

Stolas said nothing for a long time.

"I think," he said at last, very quietly, "I'm beginning to believe it will never happen. That sort of thing. For me."

"Yeah." Bloof didn't argue, because he wasn't wrong to feel it. "I get why you'd think that. You keep looking for it in the wrong crowd, that's all."

"The wrong..."

"You keep auditioning for someone who never opened the door." Bloof tilted their head, close enough that their horns nearly clipped his beak. "Maybe it's time to stop auditioning."

A pause.

"That," Stolas said thickly, "was very good."

"I've been rehearsing since spring."

He laughed, and it turned into a hiccup, and then a sniffle, and then something that was both. Bloof held him tighter and let him.

The estate came up out of the dark: spires and balconies, a black lawn, a white-lit garden dome. Stolas felt his stomach tighten.

He was already composing a graceful goodbye when Bloof's wings flared, and the whole world spun.

He yelped. In one smooth motion, Bloof turned him in the air so that they were face to face, chest to chest, hovering over the roof with the wind curling around them. Bloof's hands settled on either side of his waist, their cape floating out behind them like a banner.

"Hi," Bloof said.

"*Hello*," said Stolas, breathless.

Bloof's heart was going like a drum. They'd planned this part, sort of. Not the words. Just the shape of it, the idea that when the moment came, they wouldn't stall.

"I'm going to say something," Bloof said. "You can tell me to stop."

"I will not tell you to stop."

"You might."

"I *won't*." His red eyes were wide and fixed on them. "Please."

Bloof took a breath.

"We're friends," they said. "I know that. I know you're hurting, and you're drunk, and I'm a little drunk too. I know I don't live in Hell, and I'm gone as soon as the sun comes up on Earth. And I'm not asking for anything you don't want to give."

"Bloof..."

"But for tonight." Their voice softened. "For *one* night, I want you to feel wanted. Because you are. By me."

The wind tugged at their cape. Stolas stared.

Then he reached up with trembling fingers and caught the end of the little black ribbon wound around Bloof's horn. He turned it over in his claws like something precious.

"Do you mean it?" he whispered.

"Every word."

"Even in the morning?"

"Especially in the morning." Bloof leaned in, forehead to forehead. "Is it— okay if I kiss you?"

"Yes," said Stolas. "Oh, *yes.*"

It was nothing like the first one.

The first had been fast, startled, a question asked at the wrong moment. This one was slow. Stolas's hands came up to cradle their face, careful of the horns, and their wings beat a steady, shaky rhythm between them. Bloof felt him shiver. They felt the little puff of breath against their mouth, the soft click of his beak against their fangs, and the way his whole body leaned into them like he'd been waiting years for permission.

They landed on the roof of the manor, barely, in a flurry of cape and feathers. Stolas laughed against their mouth. Bloof laughed too, helpless, and kissed him again.

They kissed on the roof for what felt like a long time, while the moon moved a little way across the sky. At some point Stolas pulled back just enough to say, in a breathless, delighted voice, "I have a rather nice sitting room, you know."

"Do you."

"With a very large sofa."

"Is that an invitation?"

"It is the most *direct* invitation I have ever made." He pressed his forehead to theirs. "Come inside. Stay until you must go."

Bloof smiled, wide and soft and nothing like the guarded face they usually kept for strangers. "Lead the way, Your Highness."

He took their hand. They went down together through the garden dome, past plants that turned their blooms toward them as they passed. The door swung shut behind them, and the house, for the first time in a very long while, did not feel empty.

"*Stay until you must go.*"

*I'm going to make that one count,* Bloof thought.

---
The sofa was, in fact, very large.

It was upholstered in deep burgundy velvet and wide enough for a family of owls. Stolas had never once thought of it as romantic. It had simply been *furniture*, a place to read, a place to sulk, a place to doze with a book on his chest.

Tonight it looked like the most important object in Hell.

"Wow," Bloof said, from the doorway. They'd stopped just inside and were turning in a slow circle, taking it all in: the high ceiling, the gold-framed star charts, the shelves of leather-bound books, the faint green glow of the garden dome through the tall windows. "This is *so* you."

"Is that good?"

"It's perfect." Their tail swished once, then curled around their ankle. "I thought maybe I'd feel weird. You know. Lord Stolas's manor. Fancy."

"And do you?"

"A little." Bloof smiled, crooked. "But mostly I just want to sit next to you and not do anything wrong."

Stolas's heart gave a small, absurd lurch. He crossed the room and took their hand, and they let him lead them to the sofa. He sat. Bloof sat beside him, close enough that their knees touched. Neither of them said anything for a moment.

"Stolas," Bloof said gently. "Before anything else. How much have you had to drink?"

"Not so much anymore." He considered. "The flight did a great deal toward sobering me. I'd say I'm... pleasantly warm. Not muddled."

"Okay." Bloof nodded slowly. "And you're sure you want this? Not just because tonight was awful, and you want to feel good. Because I'll stay either way. We can just watch the stars and talk."

It was such a considerate thing to say that Stolas had to look away for a second.

"I want this," he said. "I want *you.* I wanted you before tonight, if I'm honest. I was too much of a fool to say so."

"Because of Blitzø?"

"Because of everything." He laughed, shakily. "I kept thinking I had to be loyal to someone who was never loyal to me. And you were so *kind*. I thought, if I let myself want you, I would ruin it."

"You *can't* ruin it." Bloof's hand found his, silver claws loosely curled around his feathers. "Not by wanting me. That's the opposite of ruining it."

Stolas swallowed. "Then yes. I'm sure."

"You can stop me whenever you want," Bloof said. "Any time. One word, and I stop."

"And you?"

"Same." Bloof grinned, a little shy. "I've got a lot of words, so you'll have to pick one."

"Darling," Stolas said, "I would like to kiss you now."

"Yeah." Bloof's whole face went soft. "Yeah, me too."

They didn't rush.

Bloof had expected nerves, maybe a clumsy fumble, the awkward energy of two friends suddenly crossing a line. What happened instead felt like falling into warm water. Stolas's hand slid up their jaw and into the soft fur beside their ear, and Bloof leaned into the touch without meaning to, a small noise rising in their throat.

"Oh," Stolas breathed. "That's lovely."

"The fur?"

"Mm. It's so soft. I thought it would be coarse." He stroked it again, thumb tracing the line of their cheek. "May I?"

"You don't have to ask every time."

"I'd like to." He smiled, a little rueful. "It seems like a habit worth building."

*He's asking me. He's the one who keeps asking.*

Stolas was trying hard to keep his composure. *Okay. Heart. Calm down.*

They kissed again, slower. Bloof shifted, one knee sliding onto the cushion, and then, carefully, they swung the other leg over until they were straddling Stolas's lap. They held themselves up on their knees, hands braced on the sofa back on either side of his head.

"Is this okay?" Bloof asked.

"This is very okay." Stolas's hands settled on their hips. "You're very warm."

"Dragon thing. Sorry."

"Don't apologize. I *like* it." He looked up at them, red eyes huge and bright. "I've been cold for a long time."

Bloof's chest tightened. They bent down and kissed his beak, then the white feathers of his cheek, then the soft spot at the corner of his eye. He sighed and tipped his head back to give them more.

"Tell me if I do something you don't like," Bloof murmured against his temple.

"I *will*." A breathless laugh. "Although I doubt there's a great deal you could do wrong."

"Famous last words."

They kissed their way down his neck, slowly. His feathers were an extraordinary thing up close: fine, layered, shading from dark slate on his crown to a pale, almost silver gray-blue at his throat. They were softer than anything Bloof had ever touched, and warm underneath, with the faint, clean scent of old books and rain. Bloof pressed their muzzle into the fluff at the base of his neck and breathed in.

"You smell nice," they said, muffled.

"I smell like a *library*."

"Yeah. That's what I said. Nice."

Stolas laughed so hard his whole body shook. Bloof felt it against their chest, and grinned into his feathers.

Stolas had never been touched like this before.

Not that he lacked experience. But the touches he knew had always carried an agenda, a rush to get to something else. Bloof wasn't rushing. Bloof stopped to notice things. Their claws traced the line of his collarbone and paused at a pale, puckered scar near the top of his shoulder, where the feathers grew in thin and slightly uneven.

"What's this from?"

Stolas's breath hitched. "Ah." He looked away, toward the fire. "A souvenir, I'm afraid. From a cowboy with a holy knife."

Bloof's claws went still. The room warmed by about a degree.

"Striker," they said quietly.

"You know about that."

"You told me. A little." Bloof's tone stayed gentle, but a thin line of heat had crept into their shoulders, and the cape on the arm of the sofa shifted as if in a draft. "You said you were fine. You said it was *nothing*."

"It *was* nothing, darling. It was a Tuesday."

"It was a blessed knife in your shoulder."

"Yes, well." A faint, rueful smile. "I've had worse Tuesdays."

Bloof breathed out slowly. They flexed their claws, forced the warmth down, and let their hand soften against his skin. "I'm not mad at you," they said. "I'm mad at the cowboy. And a little mad at the imp who knew about the hit and didn't say. But mostly I'm just really, really glad you're here."

"So am I," Stolas said, almost inaudibly.

"I could...*do* something, you know, about that cowboy."

"No. At least. Not tonight. Not now."

"Okay. Understood....can I?"

He nodded.

Bloof bent and pressed a kiss to the scar, light as a breath. The feathers around it were thin and cool, and under them Stolas's pulse jumped. He drew in a shaky breath and let it out slowly, and some tension in his shoulder let go.

"There," Bloof murmured, against the mark. "That one's mine now. No more Tuesdays like that."

"That," Stolas said thickly, "is a very large promise."

"I'm a very stubborn dragoat." They lifted their head, and their mouth tilted up, a little shy. "Okay. I'm going to say this next part and you can tell me if it's weird. I want to look at you. *All of you*. Like, *really* look. Is that okay?"

His breath caught. "That is not weird."

"Good." Bloof's eyes, the lower pair black as ink, the upper pair violet and shy at the edges, met his. "Can I take this off?"

His fingers were already at the clasp of the burgundy cape. "Please."

It slid from his shoulders in a rustle of velvet. Bloof's claws found the gold buttons of the tunic next, one by one, with a care that made his throat ache. "You're so beautiful," they said quietly, to the cloth, the buttons, the pale feathers beneath. "I don't think you know that."

"I'm a very awkward bird."

"You're an *incredible* bird. You've got the longest, loveliest limbs I've ever seen. And these." Bloof's hand spread across his chest, where the feathers lightened into a soft, silvery down. "Stolas. I've been thinking about these since spring."

"Since spring?" He gasped as claws combed gently through the fluff. "That's *months*."

"I told you I rehearsed."

Stolas laughed, startled, and reached up, because it was suddenly the only thing he could think to do. His fingers found the black ribbon wound around one of Bloof's horns. He traced it down, tentatively, to the horn itself, the dark, ridged, backward-swept curve of it.

Bloof went very still.

"Is this all right?" Stolas whispered.

"Yeah." The word came out unsteady. "Yeah, that's. They're sensitive."

"How sensitive?"

"Very. But, maybe don't—."

It was too late. Stolas stroked slowly, one long claw following the segments of the horn from base to tip. Bloof's breath hitched. Their eyes slid half-shut. Their tail swished once, hard, against the sofa cushion.

"Oh," Stolas said, delighted. "*Oh.* That's quite a reaction."

"Don't *tease*."

"I'm not teasing. I'm taking *notes*." He drew his claw down again, and Bloof's whole body shivered. "I think I should like to learn everything about you."

"That's... a lot of notes."

"I have a very good memory."

Bloof lost the thread of their sentence somewhere around the third pass of Stolas's claws along their horn. Warmth pooled in their chest. Heat prickled along their spine, and, distantly, they felt the faint glow of their own scales brightening along the shoulders.

*Careful,* they told themself. *Don't get carried away. Controlled. You're controlled.*

"Hey," they managed. "Stolas."

"Mm?"

"Tell me if I get too hot. Sometimes I..." They gestured vaguely at themself. "Run warm."

"Darling, I'm an owl in a very large, drafty house." He pulled them down for another kiss, soft and unhurried. "Run as warm as you like."

Bloof huffed out a laugh and kissed him again, and somewhere in the middle of it their upper eyes slid open without permission.

They hadn't meant to. It happened the way a sigh happens, a loosening somewhere behind the ribs. The lids parted, and the world turned a shade clearer and a few degrees warmer. Stolas's heat signature bloomed against their skin like a lamp: the fast flutter of his heartbeat, the low glow in his chest, the faint tremor in the wing tips.

Stolas had pulled back an inch, and he was staring.

"Oh," he said softly.

"Sorry. They just." Bloof tried to close them, failed, and gave up. "They do that. When I'm, when I'm paying a lot of attention."

"Don't close them." His hand came up and hovered near their cheek. "May I?"

"Idiot. You know you don't have to *ask*."

"I'm asking anyway."

"Okay." It came out smaller than they meant. "Yeah. Okay."

His thumb brushed, very lightly, along the arc of bone beneath the upper pair. Bloof's lashes fluttered. The violet irises, each with its pale white slit, caught the lamplight and flared.

"They're *violet*," Stolas murmured. "I had no idea. I thought they were closed all the time."

"They usually are."

"Why?"

"Because they, I don't know." Bloof's ears drooped a little. "They show too much. If they're open, it means I'm serious. Or scared. Or really, really mad." A shaky breath. "I don't usually let anyone see them just because I'm *happy*."

"Are you happy?"

"Yeah." The word came out cracked. "So much that it's a little embarrassing."

"It isn't embarrassing." Stolas's voice was very gentle. "I have two pairs of eyes myself, you know. Mine are rather plain by comparison. Red, red, red. Yours..." He shook his head, faintly awed. "They're like stained glass. They're like a window at dusk."

Bloof made a small, strangled sound. Their tail whipped against the cushion. "You can't just *say* things like that."

"Why not?"

"Because I'm going to *melt*. Like actually. I'm going to be a puddle on your nice sofa."

"Then I shall have a very expensive puddle." He smiled, mischievous, and traced the edge of one violet eye with the tip of a claw. "May I look at them a little longer?"

"You can look as long as you want." Bloof's ears were burning. "Just, I'm warning you, I'm not good at being looked at *this* much."

"You were rather good at it on the dance floor."

"That was *different*. That was performing." They hid their face in his shoulder for a second, then peeked back up through their lashes. "This is. This is *real*."

"Yes," Stolas said quietly. "It is."

They stayed like that for a long, trembling moment, nose to nose, both pairs of eyes open. Bloof's tail curled around his ankle. Their breath came fast and uneven, and neither of them looked away.

"Hi," Stolas whispered.

"Hi," Bloof whispered back, ears flat and grinning like an idiot.

They kissed until neither of them could say how long. Bloof's hands slid up under the open edges of the tunic and pushed it from his shoulders, and Stolas helped, shrugging until it fell away. His chest rose and fell with quick, shallow breaths. Bloof sat back a little, just enough to look at him.

He was long and lean, all pale gray-blue plumage and graceful angles. The feathers along his ribs rippled when he breathed. His wings, folded loosely at his back, trembled just slightly at the tips.

"Okay," Bloof said, voice rough. "That's. Okay."

"Is something wrong?"

"No. No, I'm just..." A helpless laugh. "Sorry. I'm having a very gay moment, give me a second."

Stolas's expression melted into pure, helpless affection. "That's the nicest thing anyone has ever said to me."

"I'm serious. You're stunning."

"I'm *rather* flushed."

"It's a good look." Bloof took his hand and kissed the inside of his wrist, where the feathers were thin and the pulse flickered fast. "May I take mine off too?"

"Please." Stolas's voice cracked. "I'd very much like to see you."

Stolas's eyes traveled down, slowly, from the little silver bat at their throat to the violet lacing at their ribs, and a flush crept up under the pale feathers of his face.

"Though I must say," he added, rather faintly, "it seems a shame to remove it."

Bloof blinked. "What is it? The dress?"

"The *everything*." He gestured vaguely, feathers fluffing at the nape of his neck. "The cape, the little choker, the ribbon on your horns. I've been trying not to stare all night, and failing very badly. You look..." He cleared his throat. "Frankly, you look *adorable*. I wanted to say so at the party, and I didn't know how."

"*Adorable*," Bloof repeated, in a very small voice.

"Devastatingly so." He pressed the back of his claw against his own cheek, as if checking the heat there. "I nearly dropped my drink when you walked in. I am a prince of the Ars Goetia, and I spent an entire song trying not to look at a vampire costume."

"You were *singing*."

"I was singing a song about heartbreak, while thinking about your *earring*." He covered his beak with one hand, mortified and delighted. "It's a small crescent. It's so *clever*. It matches your face."

Bloof's ears went flat as their tail whipped against the sofa. The warmth that had been simmering in their chest flooded straight up into their cheeks, and their upper eyes, open since a minute ago, went very wide.

"Stop," they said weakly. "I'm gonna combust. I'm serious. I will actually catch fire in your sitting room."

"Would that be so terrible?"

"It's a very nice sofa!"

"It's only velvet." Stolas smiled, softly wicked, and tucked a stray white lock behind their ear. "I only want you to know that I noticed. That I *like* it. That I like *you*, in all of it. The sweet little vampire. The dragon who set a doorframe smoking for my sake."

"I didn't..." Bloof stopped. "Okay, I did do that."

"You did. It was very gallant."

Bloof gave up. They dropped their forehead onto his shoulder and made a long, strangled noise into his plumage. His wing came up and curled around them, and they felt him laughing, silently, all the way down his spine.

"You're the worst," they said, muffled. "You're so much worse than Verosika."

"I've had practice."

"I *hate* it."

"Your tail is wagging."

"My *tail* does what it wants."

Bloof reached back for the laces of the corset bodice, fingers fumbling slightly with the ribbon. Stolas's hands found theirs and stilled them.

"Let me."

Bloof breathed out. "Okay."

He undid the lacing slowly. Violet ribbon slid free, one loop at a time, until the bodice loosened and slipped. The little silver bat on the choker clicked softly against Bloof's collarbone. Stolas lifted the cape from their shoulders, careful of the wing slits, and set it aside on the arm of the sofa.

Then he just *looked*, for a long, quiet moment, at the lean, charcoal-scaled lines of their body, the violet markings fading along their ribs, the pale spray of soft fur at their jaw and the base of their neck. Bloof felt every bit of it. They didn't hide.

"Well?" they said, a little hoarsely. "Verdict?"

"I'd like," said Stolas, "to kiss every single scale."

"That's... a *lot* of scales."

"I have all night."

Bloof's whole body flushed warm. "Yeah. Okay. Do that."

---
He did not manage every scale.

He managed a good many of them, working slowly down from the soft hollow of Bloof's throat, past the choker, across the cool slate-gray plates of their shoulder. Each time he paused, Bloof's breath caught, and each time, he looked up to check. Bloof's face was flushed, their upper eyes half-open and glowing a soft violet.

"Still all right?" he asked, against their ribs.

"Perfect," Bloof said. "You?"

"Never better."

"Good." They tangled their claws gently in his crest feathers. "Ignore my blushing. And the smiling. And—the heat. Sorry. I'm a little ridiculous."

"You isn't ridiculous."

"I *absolutely* am."

"Then let us be ridiculous together."

Stolas dragged his beak slowly along their ribs, and then, in the same unhurried voice he'd use to read a star chart, whispered, "You know, I've been imagining the sound you'd make when I told you how lovely you are. I'd quite underestimated how lovely, and *how much I'd want to hear it*."

The dragon's brain **shut down**.

It didn't slow down. It didn't stutter. It simply *stopped*, mid-thought, like a game frozen on a single frame. Every part of them lit up at once: their ears, their tail, their upper eyes, the faint violet glow along their shoulders. A very small, very undignified noise escaped their throat.

"You," they managed, "*wait. Can't. Stop.*"

"Stop what?"

"*Talking*."

"I thought you enjoyed my talking."

"Yeah, that's— the *problem!*" Their tail snapped around before they'd decided to move it, a warm, scaled coil that looped over his shoulder and, with the desperate competence of a dragon who has run out of options, wrapped around the lower half of Stolas' face. "There. *Shh*. I just— need a minute."

A muffled, delighted sound came from somewhere under the tail.

"Hey! Stop! I can *feel* you smiling."

Another muffled sound, which was unmistakably a laugh.

"Oh gods, fuck you. Sorry, having a crisis. Don't be *smug*."

The tail loosened, just a little, and Stolas's beak slid free enough to speak. His red eyes gleamed over the dark, scaled curve. He looked like an owl who had caught a particularly large mouse.

"Darling," he said, voice silky, "I should tell you that you've just made a *serious* tactical error."

"What error?"

"You've shown me *exactly* what works."

"I... *oh no*."

"Oh *yes*." He pressed a kiss to the scales of the tail, then another, then followed it slowly down toward the base, where the muscle was warm and tight. Bloof's whole body jerked. "I have a very good memory. And I have all night."

"Stolas..."

"Is that a stop word, or is it a *please*?"

Bloof opened their mouth. Closed it. Their tail thumped helplessly against the cushion.

"...The second one," they whispered.

"Thought so." His voice softened, warm and a little fond. "Tell me if it's too much. You know I'll stop."

"I know." They pulled in a shaky breath. "Don't stop."

He didn't. He worked the tail gently free of his face, claws light on the scales, and held it in his hands like something rare. He kissed the tip. He turned it over and kissed the underside. Bloof watched through half-lidded eyes, with the stunned look of someone watching their own ship sail away without them.

"You said 'since spring,'" Stolas said, thoughtfully, to the tail. "Twice, if I recall."

"I... might have."

"I would very much like to hear what you were imagining. In some detail." His red eyes flicked up, bright and wicked. "*Take your time*."

"I'm *not*..." Bloof's voice cracked. "You *cannot* make me say that out loud!"

"I wouldn't dream of making you. I'm only asking." He tilted his head. "I'll go first, if that helps."

"*Stolas.*"

"I imagined you in my reading chair," he said, in the warm, dreamy voice of a bird describing a very pleasant afternoon, "with your hair loose, explaining firewalls and magi-tech to me, and me interrupting every other sentence to kiss you until you lost your place."

Bloof made a sound like a kettle.

"I imagined learning which of your horns' notches makes your breath catch. I was right, as it turns out, about three of them." A pause, a delicate little smile. "And I imagined you looking at me *exactly* like this. All four of your eyes, and every single one of them mine."

*No. Oh no no no. Fuck, too good. He knows too much.*

Bloof's ears folded flat. Their tail went limp in his hands. Their upper eyes, which had been wide, squeezed shut, then flew open again, then shut. "Okay," they said, in a very small, very faint voice. "Okay, that's. I'm. Wow."

"You said three of them." Stolas let their tail slip from his hands and straightened, until he was sitting upright and Bloof was perched in his lap, forehead nearly touching his beak. "I should like to see how right I was."

"Stolas, I..."

"May I?"

His hand was already lifted, hovering near the base of the left horn, the one with the black ribbon. Bloof looked at it, and then at him. Their throat bobbed.

"...Yeah," they whispered.

"Tell me if it's too much."

"I will."

"Promise?"

"*Promise.*"

He started at the ribbon. His claw slid beneath it, not pulling, just lifting, and then followed the ridged curve of the horn up from where it grew out of Bloof's brow. The segments rose in slow, dark steps, each one a little warmer than the last. Bloof's breath stayed even through the first. It stayed even through the second.

At the third, it hitched.

"Ah," Stolas said, softly. "There's one."

"*Don't*..."

"I'm only noting it." He drew the claw back down, then up again, and lingered at the same notch, tracing the slim ridge with the pad of his thumb. A tremor ran through Bloof's whole body. "That one's lovely. It's the very first one that responds, isn't it? That's why it was so quick to catch you off guard."

"Fuck. Why are you so *good* at this?."

"I'm a *scholar*. I take my research seriously." He moved on, and the next notch drew a small, stifled sound from Bloof's throat. "Another."

"S-Stolas."

"Mm. And this one." He traced a slow circle where one of the segments dipped, and Bloof's head tipped back against his hand without them meaning it to. Their claws dug into his shoulders. "Oh, that is *quite* a reaction."

The horn was warm now, almost hot. Faint violet light was starting to gather in the grooves, the way embers gather in coals. Stolas felt it against his palm and was fascinated and a little moved. He had touched many things in his long life that were beautiful, but very few of them had been alive and trembling under his hands because he'd *asked*.

"Is that all right?" he whispered. "Still good?"

"Yeah." It came out breathless. "Yeah, I. Keep going."

He found the third spot near the tip, a narrow ridge where the horn curved sharply back, and ran the flat of his claw along it, slow and deliberate.

Bloof *moaned*.

It was low, a little rough, and entirely unplanned. It rolled out of them like the sound of a dragon sighing in the dark, and in the quiet of the sitting room it seemed to ring.

They froze.

Both of them did.

Then Bloof clapped both hands over their mouth. Their ears went flat. Their face, scales and fur and all, flushed several shades darker, and the little violet crescents under their lower eyes seemed to glow with embarrassment. Above their hands, the upper eyes were huge and wet and horrified.

*Oh— gods.*

"That," they said, muffled, "did *not* happen."

"It most certainly did."

"I didn't mean..." A strangled breath. "I don't make noises like that. That was a *glitch*."

"It was lovely." Stolas's voice was soft and unmistakably delighted. "May I hear it again?"

"No."

"Please?"

"Absolutely not." They tried to bury their face in his chest and nearly succeeded. "I'm moving to another dimension. I'm going to go live in my rift. You're the worst. Again. **Why** are you so *good* at this?"

"Because I've been paying attention." He gently drew one of their hands down from their mouth and kissed the knuckles. "You don't have to hide, darling."

"But it's *embarrassing*."

"It's wonderful." A pause. Something shifted in the line of his mouth, a small, very pleased curve that Bloof, face-down against his feathers, did not see. "You know, I did say I'd be taking notes."

"...Stolas."

"And I do find it so *difficult* to stop in the middle of a study."

"Stolas, *no*, I swear to..."

His claw found the ridge near the tip of the left horn again, and Bloof's sentence died in their throat.

It was gentle. That was the worst of it. Not a rough, hurried touch but a slow, patient one, the kind a bird gives to a feather he's decided to preen *properly*. The pad of his thumb traced the narrow notch, back and forth, back and forth, and at each pass a tremor ran down Bloof's spine and out the end of their tail. The sound that came out of them this time was lower, and longer, and absolutely **not** a glitch.

"There it is," Stolas murmured, warm and satisfied. "That's the one."

"You *planned* this," Bloof managed, through their fingers.

"I *adapted.* It's quite different."

"I'm going to, I'll *get* you for..."

"Oh, I do hope so." He stroked again, slow, and their next breath broke in half. "Do you want me to stop?"

Bloof's mouth opened. Nothing came out. Their eyes, all four, had gone glassy and half-lidded, and the violet in them was running together like paint. They tried again. Their head shook, a small, helpless movement, and they heard themself whisper:

"...No. Fuck no. Dammit."

"Good." He pressed a kiss to the base of the horn, just under the ribbon. "Tell me if that changes. Tap my shoulder. Anything. I'll stop at once."

"I *know*," Bloof gasped. "I know you, oh, *oh*. Don't. Just...shush. Don't you dare stop."

"I wouldn't dream of it."

He didn't.

He took his time. He had, he reminded himself, all night. He found the second notch, the one lower down, and learned what a slow circle there did to the arch of Bloof's back. He found the third, near the curve, and learned that if he pressed *just* so, Bloof's tail would curl around his waist without any consent from their brain. He alternated, light and patient, steady as a metronome, and listened to the room fill up with soft, broken sounds he'd never heard before, and would never forget.

Bloof tried, at first, to hold on to something. A joke. A pun. A shred of their composure. **Anything**. "I want it noted, this is *extremely* unfair," they got out, and a moment later, "I'm going to make you *pay* for this, I'm going to, *ah*, tell Octavia you're, mmm, *a menace*."

"Octavia already knows."

"Then I'll tell her you're— a *nerd*."

"She knows that too."

"Then I'll tell her I'm *so*..." Another sound, helpless and rich, and Bloof's forehead dropped against his collarbone. "Shit, gods—I forgot what I was saying."

"Do you want me to remind you?"

"I want you to *never stop*," Bloof blurted, then froze. "Wait. Wait, that wasn't. I didn't—."

Stolas laughed, quietly, right against their ear.

"You *absolutely* did," he said, and kissed the tip of the horn. "And I absolutely will."

By the time he finally slowed, Bloof had stopped being embarrassed.

It had happened gradually. First the hands had come down from their mouth, and then the shame had gone out of their shoulders, and somewhere in between, the little noises had stopped being something to hide and become something to *give*. Now they were slumped half across him, ears flat, eyes shut, breathing in long, shaky gasps, with one claw hooked loosely in the fur of his chest.

He eased his hand away. The horn was hot as a coal and faintly aglow, violet light pooling in every groove. He rested his palm lightly against it, just to feel it cool.

"Are you all right?" he whispered.

"Mm." Bloof opened one upper eye, slowly, as though it had cost them something. "Huh? Oh. I think I died."

"You seem remarkably animated for the deceased."

"Oh, *you*." They squinted up at him. "I'm so going to *make you pay* for that."

"Do you promise?"

"You're *insufferable*."

"You're blushing."

"I'm *always* blushing. It's a defect." They lifted their head, ears wobbling back upright, and gave him a long, drowsy, besotted look. "Stolas?"

"Yes?"

"*Come here*."

He went. Bloof caught his face in both hands and kissed him, and this time it was slow and deep and sure. Stolas let himself sink into it. Their hands moved over each other with the unhurried curiosity of two beings who had suddenly been given permission. Bloof's claws traced the edges of his wing joints, light as a whisper, and he shuddered and arched against them. His own fingers found the base of their tail, still warm from his kisses, and followed it down, slowly, the tight, scaled muscle flexing under his palm.

"That okay?" he murmured.

"Yeah," Bloof gasped. "Yeah, that's. Wow. Okay."

They kissed until the sofa creaked and the fire in the grate burned low, murmuring softly between breaths:

"You're so soft."

"You're so warm."

"Tell me if it's too much."

"It's not. It's just enough."

"I want to stay like this."

"We can."

At some point, Bloof slid off Stolas's lap and tucked in beside him, one arm draped across his chest, their legs tangled together. Stolas pulled the burgundy cape over both of them. The two of them lay there, breathing, foreheads together, hearts slowing.

"Hey," Bloof said after a while, voice soft and a little drowsy. "We don't have to do anything else tonight. If you don't want to."

"I think," Stolas said slowly, "I would like to just hold you. If that suits."

"It suits." Bloof pressed a kiss to his beak. "More than."

"And in the morning?"

"In the morning, I make you breakfast." A sleepy grin. "I'm very good at toast."

"And Verosika?"

"Verosika is *never* finding out about tonight."

"I wouldn't dream of telling her." A soft, wicked pause. "Although she did seem *awfully* pleased with herself at the party."

Bloof groaned, and buried their face in his chest. Stolas laughed, and the sound felt like something unlocking. He tucked his chin over Bloof's head, one wing curling loosely around them both. The garden glowed green outside the window. The great house, for the first time in a very long while, was quiet in a way that felt like peace and not emptiness.

"You know," Bloof said, muffled, into his plumage, "I *did* say I'd make you pay for that."

"Mm." Stolas's voice was drowsy and smug. "I believe you said it *twice*."

"I'm a dragon-goat of my word."

"Are you?"

"**Yeah**." Bloof lifted their head. Their ears had come back upright. Their lower eyes were bright, and the upper pair, which had been half-lidded and soft, were suddenly very focused and very violet. A slow, crooked grin was spreading across their face. "So. Question. Can I?"

Stolas blinked. "Can you what?"

"Take notes." They tilted their head. "Since you're such a big fan of research."

It took a moment. Then Stolas's feathers fluffed, all at once, all along his neck. "Oh," he said, in a very different voice. "*Oh.*"

"Is that a yes?"

"It's," his beak clicked, "it's a *very* firm yes. Provided..."

"Provided you can say stop whenever you want, and I stop. Same deal." Bloof pressed a kiss to the corner of his beak. "You tap, you say 'darling,' you honk. I'll know."

"I do *not* honk."

"Yeah? You honked three times at the party."

"That was a *different* kind of honk."

"Then you can honk," said Bloof, "differently." And they rolled, smoothly, until they were above him on the sofa, one knee on either side of his hips, his burgundy cape slipping off their shoulders. "Hold still."

---

They started with his crest.

It was a long, soft ridge of dark feathers that swept back from his brow, and when Bloof sank their claws in, light as rain, Stolas's whole head tipped back into the cushion. His eyes slid shut. His beak fell open on a small, startled "*ah*."

"Ah," Bloof murmured. "Found one."

"That isn't *fair*, you've been looking at me all night..."

"Yeah. I took notes too." They scratched, slow and careful, along the base of the crest, where the feathers grew in thick and warm, and Stolas shivered all the way to his toes. "Is that okay?"

"Y-yes."

"Good." They moved lower, kissing a trail from the corner of his beak to the pale hollow of his throat. The feathers there were fine and silvery and nearly weightless, and when Bloof pressed their muzzle into them, Stolas made a little fluttering noise that sounded a great deal like a dove. "*Oh.* And there's that. You're ticklish."

"I am *not*."

"You absolutely are."

"I'm *sensitive*. It's a very different thing."

"It's very cute," Bloof said, and nipped, very gently, at the base of his neck. Stolas yelped. His wings flared half open before he caught himself and tucked them in, trembling.

*Revenge is a dish best served warm,* Bloof thought, with grim satisfaction. *Fortunate that I run hot.*

---

They found out several things in the next while.

They found out that his wing joints, where the long gray-blue feathers folded into the shoulder, were as sensitive as Bloof's horns, and that a slow trace there made him arch right off the cushion. They found out that if they dragged a single claw down the silvery down of his chest, one precise line from sternum to ribs, he went *silent*, as though the air had been pulled out of him. They found out, accidentally, with their tail, that the long plume of his own tail feathers curled up in his lap whenever Bloof's brushed against them.

That discovery proved useful.

"Bloof," Stolas said, thinly. "What is your tail doing?"

"Nothing."

"It is *clearly* doing something."

"I'm just. Getting *comfortable*."

The tip of the dark, scaled tail had slipped under the hem of his tunic, which still hung loosely around his hips, and was tracing a slow, lazy spiral along his thigh. It didn't hurry. It didn't press. It simply drew a line, then another, light as a breath, until Stolas's claws had curled into the velvet and his breathing had gone choppy.

"You *wretch*," he said, in a voice that sounded like it was trying very hard to be stern. "You are doing this on purpose."

"What gave me away?"

"The *smirk.*"

"I don't smirk. I *smile warmly.*" Bloof kissed his collarbone, right over the scar, and let the tail glide a little higher. "Is this okay?"

"Yes."

"You sure?"

"I'm *very*, I'm perfectly..." Stolas's breath hitched as the tail swept slowly up and across his hip. "Oh, you are *awful*."

"I learned from the best."

A shaky, delighted laugh. Then, a little desperately, "Bloof. *Darling.*"

Bloof paused at once. Their tail went still. Their hands went still. Their upper eyes searched his face.

"Too much?"

"No," Stolas whispered. "No. It's only that I find myself rather, ah, *overwhelmed*." His wings quivered. "In the most lovely way."

"Okay." Bloof pressed their forehead to his. "Tell me what you want."

He thought about it for a long moment, with an owl's grave attention. "I'd like you to keep going," he said finally, softly. "And I'd like you to take your time."

"That," Bloof said, "I can do."

They did take their time. They explored the long, graceful line of his ribs, the lighter down that fluffed at his sides, the small, soft place behind his hip bone where the feathers thinned. Their claws went light as breath. Their mouth followed, slow, as though each patch of plumage were a sentence to be read twice. Somewhere in the middle of it, Bloof's tail coiled loosely around his wrists, not pinning, just resting, a warm, scaled loop that said *I'm here*. Stolas didn't pull. He only sighed and let his head fall back, and the loop tightened a fraction in response.

"Okay?" Bloof breathed.

"Mm. Yes. Please."

"Say it again."

"*Please*."

They kissed him there, low on his stomach, where the feathers were softest, and felt him shudder from beak to talons. Heat was rising between them, thick and golden, and Bloof's hand had begun to slide lower, slow, unhurried, toward the edge of his tunic and the thing beneath it. They stopped.

They didn't *have* to. But something in the quiet, in the way his chest was rising and falling and his red eyes were wide and shining, made them want to be careful.

"Hey," they said softly. "Stolas."

"Yes?"

"I could keep going." They searched his face. "I want to. But I also, this is a big night. You've had a lot of drinks, and a lot of feelings, and I want to get it right. So tell me honestly. Do you want to keep going *now*? Or do you want to save that for later? Both are good. I'm not going anywhere till morning."

He stared at them. His beak trembled.

"You," he said unsteadily, "are the most *considerate* creature I have ever met."

"That's not an answer."

"No." He laughed, wet-eyed, and lifted a hand to their cheek. "I think I should like to wait. Not because I don't want you. Because I want to remember *all* of it. Every single moment, with a clear head."

"Okay," Bloof whispered. Their whole face softened. "Okay. Then we wait."

"You're not disappointed?"

"I'm *thrilled.*" They kissed him, slowly, and felt his smile against their mouth. "I just get to do this again later. With a better plan."

"A better plan?"

"I was going to say *a lot of sofa cushions*, but I figure we can work that out."

Stolas honked.

It was a short, bright, entirely involuntary honk, and he clapped a hand over his beak a second too late. Bloof grinned, wide and wicked. "*Different* kind of honk," they said. "Noted."

"I *despise* you."

"You're crying."

"I'm *happy*," Stolas said, and kissed them, helplessly, again.

They settled, finally, wrapped in the cape, tangled in each other's limbs, a long gray-blue wing draped over a slim charcoal shoulder. The tail loop loosened from his wrists and curled around his ankle instead. The fire in the grate had burned down to red coals. The house was quiet.

Stolas's breathing had gone slow. His eyes were half closed. Bloof lay with their cheek on his chest for a while and listened to the heartbeat under the down, and the longer they listened, the less they wanted to be anywhere else.

*Don't fall asleep on the sofa,* they told themself. *He's got a whole sky out there and he hasn't looked at it once tonight.*

"Hey," they said, very quietly.

"Mm?"

"Come up with me."

"Up?"

"The roof. Well." They considered. "Somewhere flat. You've got a balcony or a tower or something, right? Somewhere you can see the sky." They propped themself up on one elbow, and their upper eyes slid open, soft and a little shy. "I realized you showed me the whole house and I never got to see the stars."

---
There was a terrace.

It sat at the top of the east tower, up a narrow stair with a worn carpet and a lamp at every landing. It was a wide, flat circle of pale stone with a low wall around it and a view that went on forever. Stolas had come up here for years to read, and sulk, and watch things that never quite came close enough. He'd left a pile of cushions in one corner and a folded blanket on a stone bench. Bloof saw them and laughed softly.

"You *planned* this."

"I did *not*."

"Cushions. Blankets. A stone bench. This is a *date spot*."

"It is a *reading* spot."

"It's quite romantic for a reading spot."

"It is," Stolas admitted, hiding his face in the fur of his cape, "I suppose, a little romantic."

Bloof collected the cushions into a nest at the center of the terrace, and spread the blanket across it, and then, on a whim, took the burgundy cape from Stolas's shoulders and laid it over the top like a quilt. They tested it with one hand. Then, with a small, private grin, they went and stood in the middle of it and unfurled their wings.

They were wide and dark and faintly violet at the edges, and in the lamplight they cast two long curved shadows across the stone. Bloof gave them a slow beat, just to stretch, and a warm breeze rolled out across the terrace and ruffled Stolas's feathers.

"Oh," he said. "That's *much* better than a fire."

"Dragon thing."

"I'm beginning to appreciate the *dragon thing*."

"Good." Bloof held out a hand. "Come lie down."

He did. They lowered themselves onto the nest of cushions together, a long tangle of limbs and wings, and Bloof drew the cape up over them both. The stone beneath was warm from the day's heat. The air smelled of night and old books and the green, damp scent of the garden far below. Stolas settled on his back with one long arm beneath Bloof's neck, and Bloof curled into his side, one leg over his, and tucked their tail up around his ankle.

Then they looked up.

---
The sky was enormous.

It was nothing like the sky of Bloof's own world. It was bigger and redder, with slow, strange colors at its edges. A long pale river of stars ran from one horizon to the other, thick as spilled salt. Some pulsed gently, like heartbeats. Others burned steady and white, and the dark between them was full and soft, like velvet.

Bloof's breath went out of them in a long, quiet "*oh.*"

"I thought you might like it."

"It's so *loud.*"

"It is, rather." Stolas's voice was warm and low, close to their ear. "It's been talking for a very long time. I'm only beginning to learn the language."

"Teach me."

He lifted his free hand and pointed, very slowly, with one long claw. "That one is the Lantern. Those four, in a line, are the Sisters. And that bright one with the red edge, my mother used to say, was the eye of a very old and very sad dragon."

"A dragon?"

"I may be embellishing."

"You're *totally* embellishing."

"A little." He smiled. "But I like to think it's true. It seems like the kind of thing a sky ought to remember."

They lay for a while, shoulder to shoulder, and watched. Somewhere, very far away, a bell rang once in the city, and the sound drifted up and out and was gone.

"Hey," Bloof said, softly.

"Yes?"

"What do you call mine?"

"Yours?"

They tapped the small purple star on their forehead. "This one. Does it have a constellation?"

Stolas turned his head and looked at them for a long moment. The starlight caught the edge of his white facial disk and made the feathers around his eyes glow faintly silver.

"No," he said at last. "Not yet."

"Not yet?"

"I think I should like to name one."

Bloof's breath caught. "Stolas."

"I haven't decided what to call it." He lifted his hand again and, with the tip of a claw, drew a slow, curving line across the sky, past the Lantern and under the Sisters, and stopped at a faint cluster of violet-white points that Bloof hadn't noticed before. "There. Those seven. They sit rather low, and they're easily missed. But if you know where to look, they hold a very particular light."

"Seven stars."

"Seven." His fingers found theirs and wove between them. 

"I shall have to think of something suitable. Something with *horns*."

Bloof laughed, a wet, unsteady laugh, and pressed their face into his shoulder. "You're ridiculous."

"I'm a scholar. We name things."

"You're *so* ridiculous."

"And you are staying," Stolas said, very softly, "until you must go."

"Yeah." They closed their eyes, then opened them again, because they didn't want to miss it. "I am. But right now, I'm here. With you. And I'd like it if it stayed that way for a long time."

---
Neither of them said much after that.

The stars wheeled slowly overhead. Stolas's breathing deepened and evened out. His hand loosened in Bloof's, and his head tilted gently against theirs, and one long wing slid down to drape across both of them like a second blanket. The garden glowed green far below. The wind, high and thin, turned the edges of the cape.

Bloof lay awake a little longer, listening to his heartbeat.

*Wanted,* Stolas had said, once, in a hallway at a party, in a voice cracked down the middle. *I want to feel wanted.*

They looked at the sleeping owl beside them, with his beak tucked into their locs and his long fingers curled around theirs, and something in their chest went very quiet and very warm.

*Stay until you must go,* they thought. *I'm going to make that count.*

They tightened their arm around him, and curled their wing a little higher, to keep the wind off his shoulders. Stolas hummed once, deep in his sleep, and settled closer. Above them, the seven low stars burned steady and violet, and the long, slow night went on.
