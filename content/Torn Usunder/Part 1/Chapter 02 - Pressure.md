# Chapter 2: Pressure

>“Knowing what comes next does not put your feet in the right place.”
>— Instructor Vesk

---

Orlan does not wait for the murmur to finish.

“Ring one,” he says, in the same flat, unhurried voice he used for the names. “Everyone else, off the mats.”

The room peels back toward the walls in a rustle of soles and half-finished sentences. Somebody near the back says a number under their breath. Somebody else says, too brightly, “Well. At least it’s early.”

By the foam-shield rack, a pair of long cream ears stands straight up, then goes very still. Pip catches Bloof looking. He smiles and lifts a hand, and Bloof gives him one nod.

The bag goes against the wall. Bloof unbuckles the collar, slips off the spiked wristbands, and unclips the chains from their belt. The jacket comes last, and pulling the right sleeve free sends the old hot wire from neck to blade. They fold it over the bag. Paper crackles in the pocket under their palm.

Beneath it, a fitted black shirt, cuffs to the wrist. Nothing shows but the taped hands.

Orlan waits beside the equipment rack with the bands: slim, gray, stamped on the underside in black. M.1-ABS. Everyone calls them dampers. Two fasten around Bloof’s forearms, over the sleeves, and a narrow strap cinches across their ribs. Orlan checks the calibration on his slate before nodding them through.

Each band has a thumbnail window with a clouded crystal behind it and two pale indicators beside it: charge and heat. Bloof checks the vent slots before closing the last catch.

Beyond the white boundary, a clear barrier rises with a faint shimmer, leaving a strip of mat between the ring and the students gathering outside.

“Incapacitate your opponent or put them out of the marked ring,” Orlan says. “The barrier catches stray casting. It doesn’t keep you in.” He looks from Bloof to Isolde. “The bands take the edge off. That is not permission to be careless. A white light means the zone is locked. Stop when I call.”

On the other side of the ring, Isolde Fenmarsh finishes tightening a flat water pack across her lower back, a tube coiled at her hip like a sleeping snake. She is a head taller than Bloof, long in the neck and leg, with slate-gray feathers and a white throat streaked dark. A black plume sweeps back from her crown.

She checks the space behind her heels, then crosses to the center.

“Graymark.”

“Fenmarsh.”

“I watched you with Ashwick.” Her voice is level, pitched not to carry. “Clean work.”

“Thank you.”

“Ashwick isn’t patient. I am.”

“I’d heard.”

One yellow eye shifts toward the taped hands, then back to Bloof’s face.

“Good. Then we won’t waste each other’s time.”

She returns to her mark. Orlan’s eyes drop to the slate, and Bloof knows what he is reading: every outward discharge will be written down.

Mist makes the approach difficult. Close contact makes her work. The problem is getting there.

Bloof closes the taped right hand. Opens it. The last gap in the barrier shimmers shut.

“Begin,” says Orlan.

---

Isolde does not move.

She lifts two fingers of her right hand. Water rises from the tube at her hip, unwinds into a thin ribbon, and breaks into mist. It rolls across the mats, pale and low, a cold tide that finds Bloof’s ankles and climbs to the knee. The far side of the ring dissolves. Beyond it, the barrier catches the overhead lights in a thin white line, and the watching students shrink to smears of color.

Bloof keeps their upper lids shut. Their lower eyes follow Isolde until the veil takes her, and then there is only cold air against their muzzle, the floor under their feet, and the sound of water moving somewhere ahead.

“Take your time,” Isolde says. “I’m not going anywhere.”

*Wonderful. She narrates.*

Through a thinner patch of mist, a tall silhouette stands left of center, one pale arm lifted. Bloof does not charge it. They step right instead, following a shallow arc, with a false lean toward the center lane: enough movement to ask a question without giving the whole answer.

Water cracks down the lane and slaps the empty mat.

A narrow seam opens farther right. Isolde’s hand appears, water tightening above the knuckles—

Bloof turns away from it.

Not far enough.

The lance catches the outside of their left forearm with a blunt, heavy slap. Pale light flares across the sleeve, the band chirps, and a numb sting runs toward the elbow. Amber. Not white.

Bloof gives ground, three short steps out of the lane. Behind them, spray strikes the barrier and runs down it in a sheet.

“Sensible angle,” Isolde says. “I covered it.”

Someone outside the ring draws a breath through their teeth. Bloof flexes the left hand. There used to be more time between the glimpse and the strike.

They move again.

A film of spray shines across the mat, and through it a narrow ripple runs toward Bloof’s supporting foot. The foot lifts. A water lash snaps through the space beneath it. Bloof sets the foot down short, turning on the heel instead of taking the larger recovery Isolde has seen before.

The second strike comes at chest height.

The hip follows the pivot. Bloof folds the forearm close over the ribs and turns edge-on as the lance tears past, spray needling the sleeve. Behind them, the barrier flashes and swallows the rest.

For a moment, the mist opens around Isolde’s face.

“That recovery is new.”

Bloof steps toward the glimpse. Her right hand closes, and water sweeps between them in a low, broad lash that makes them check the approach instead of planting into it. By the time it falls, she has moved.

They have not broken through. But they have not been hit again, either.

Bloof lets the distance settle.

A shoulder feint draws nothing. A partial step into the lance’s lane brings a pale seam through the mist, but no strike, and they withdraw without unloading the rear leg. Isolde is waiting for more than a foot to move.

Bloof gives her more. The hip turns, the lead hand rises, and water shoots across their path. They stop short and let it pass.

A rhythm begins to emerge. The low threat makes them lift or check a step, and the lance follows the recovery. When Isolde moves, the mist thins where she has been and thickens across the route to wherever she is going.

Not every gap is an invitation. Not every gap is false.

“You’re counting,” Isolde says. “Good. Count faster.”

A lash skims the mat. Bloof steps over it and brings the left hand up, drawing the next narrow opening in the veil. They catch the edge of a gray sleeve. Then it is gone.

“You’re waiting for an opening, Graymark.” Mist folds across the lane. “I’m not offering one.”

Heat gathers in Bloof’s left palm.

They advance half a step, enough to draw the low ripple again, and release a small pulse into the air. The mist curls away from it with a hiss, and for an instant there is a hollow no wider than a shield: a planted gray foot, a slant of white throat, Isolde’s left arm raised across her body and her right fist low, water gathering against the knuckles.

The hollow begins to close.

Bloof steps off the line, and the lance punches through the space they have left.

Their rear foot finds a dry patch. Knees bend, weight sinks, and heat packs into the heels until thin orange seams brighten beneath the hems of their trousers.

**Cinderstep.**

The floor lets go.

The mist tears open in a long pale scar. Bloof crosses behind the lance’s tail, spray pelting their shoulder, and Isolde’s shape rushes out of the white with her gaze already snapping to their face. She is stepping back. The burst ends short of her guard; two braking steps bite into the rubber, and the third carries a short left strike into range.

Her forearm meets it at an angle. Bloof’s hand slides along the contact and closes around her wrist.

“You’ve closed,” Isolde says. “Now what?”

“Now I stay.”

She pivots. The water gathered at her right hand strikes the inside of Bloof’s elbow, and their grip opens. Her foot drives into their hip, a long, economical push that sends them a step back. Space. She wants the whole arm’s length of it.

Bloof gives her less.

The back of their left hand sweeps her extended leg aside as she draws it in, and they step after her, keeping the movement short. No second burst, no hard launch to recover from. A strike toward the body makes her turn, and Bloof follows the turn into contact.

Her left forearm frames against their chest. They knock it across her center. Isolde draws the arm back and pivots to free it, and Bloof steps with her, right hand checking the shoulder; pain pulls tight from neck to blade. They keep the check brief.

The heel of their left palm drives in above her elbow, heat tightening through the striking arm.

Contact.

The band on Isolde’s left forearm gives a sharp click. White runs along its lights. Her fingers uncurl, and the arm drops against her side as the veil loosens around them.

“Left arm,” Orlan says. “Locked. Fenmarsh?”

“Continue.”

She is already moving. Her right forearm comes between them, and water follows in a broad sheet that doesn’t throw Bloof back but breaks contact while she steps away. Outside the barrier, several voices rise at once.

Bloof follows.

Isolde’s right hand threatens high, and their guard answers while their feet keep moving. A short strike toward her ribs makes her turn, and they stay with the turn, denying her the clean step away. Mist drifts between them; Bloof checks instead of striking blind. She gains half a pace. They take it back.

Her release is still quick. A compact strike brushes Bloof’s guard and throws cold water across their throat. They shorten the next step, let the pressure pass, and enter behind the withdrawing hand.

Isolde has to move again, and the white boundary comes into view behind her heels.

The mist is thinner now. Runoff gathers near her rear foot in a narrow stream, and as she pulls it back toward herself, the veil loses another layer. Bloof presses through that breath. A feint toward her left side; she cannot frame with the locked arm, so her right comes across instead. Bloof changes the angle, and one more step brings her close to the line.

Her remaining hand drops near her ribs, water gathering against the knuckles. She holds it there.

Bloof settles their weight. Her right foot has room to slide along the boundary, but not back. One flank is open, but only while that hand stays ready to strike.

Heat swells in Bloof’s right palm. A short flare across the escape lane, a step into the space it leaves, enough to force her over the line. On the band, the indicator climbs, well under its ceiling but higher than anything they have released so far. Beyond the barrier, Orlan holds the slate.

“You have me on the line, Graymark,” Isolde says. Her breath is no longer quite even. “So finish it.”

Bloof moves.

The flame begins to unfold from their palm.

Then Bloof closes the hand.

They turn the strike into a shove and step to sweep her supporting leg. The change costs half a beat.

The bead leaves Isolde’s knuckles.

It hits low under Bloof’s ribs, blunt and heavy as a kicked door. A pale flash runs across the strap, and every light along it turns white. Their trunk stops answering. Breath comes shallow and startled, their weight tips forward, and one knee strikes the mat. They catch themself with an open hand.

At the wall, cream ears flatten.

“Match,” Orlan says. “Fenmarsh.”

Isolde lowers her right hand. The mist begins to settle, and the barrier fades, leaving the room beyond it sharp and bright.

Then the hall breaks into talk.

---

Orlan crosses the ring before the last of the mist has fallen.

“Stay there. Fenmarsh, you too.”

He crouches beside Bloof and checks the slate. The bands click twice, and white drains from the strap’s lights. Feeling returns in a wash of pins and needles, sternum to hip. Bloof takes a breath that reaches the bottom of their lungs. Across the ring, Isolde opens her left hand, closes it, and rolls the wrist.

“Locks are clear,” Orlan says. “Stand when you’re ready.”

Bloof plants a foot and rises. The legs hold. The right hand trembles against the band, and they fold it into the opposite elbow.

“—had her on the line—”

“The arm was luck.”

“Luck doesn’t do that to an elbow.”

Tamsin already has his tablet out. “Fifty,” he announces. “Maybe forty-eight.”

“It’s not official,” Kell says, but she is grinning.

Isolde crosses the ring, working her left fingers as she comes, damp feathers clinging along her throat.

“You hesitated at the end,” she says. “Don’t.”

Bloof looks up.

“You changed the attack after you’d committed.” She flexes the hand again. “That’s what I answered.”

“Thank you.” Their ear dips. “For the match.”

“You’re welcome.” A small shift at the corner of her beak. “Nicely placed, by the way. I’ll feel it for a week.”

She returns to her starting mark to reclaim the last water from the mat.

Orlan waits while Bloof undoes the forearm catches and loosens the rib strap. The equipment comes away warm; they pass him each piece, and he checks it before setting it in the return tray.

“Graymark.”

“Instructor.”

“Your entries did more work than your casting.”

Bloof pulls the sleeve over their right hand. “She left me room.”

Orlan looks at them.

“Keep the entries. Rest the shoulder.”

He turns toward the next pair.

Half a beat. Bloof catches themself rehearsing the conversion and makes their hand open.

At the cubbies, Pip is waiting.

“Bloof.” His voice is bright, quick, nearly a whisper. “You were incredible. She was all fog and you just—” His hands sketch a lunge. “And the arm. You got right through.”

“It was close.”

“It was so close.”

His eyes find the hand Bloof has tucked back into the crook of their arm, and the hands making the lunge fall still.

“Just say,” he adds.

Bloof opens their mouth to tell him they are fine. Their fingers tremble against the sleeve.

“…Yeah.”

Pip nods. “Can I see?”

Bloof holds out the right hand.

From a separate compartment in his satchel, he takes a flat tin, fresh gauze, and a pair of clean gloves, and sets them on the bench. The tape comes away slowly; where it catches, Pip waits for Bloof to ease it free.

The salve smells green and cold.

“Ready?”

Bloof nods.

He lays it across the split knuckles with two careful fingers. The cold bites. Bloof breathes through it, watching his ears lean toward the work.

*He’s going to be unbearable about saying yes.*

Pip winds the gauze without pulling the cuff back. “There.” He waits while Bloof flexes the hand. “Better?”

“Better.”

“Good.” He closes the tin and puts it away. “Sit for a bit?”

Bloof sits. Pip settles beside them until Orlan calls his name.

---

By the end of class, the gauze has stopped feeling new.

Bloof has spent the remaining matches watching from the wall, left hand resting over the right. The tremor has eased. The shoulder has not. They dress carefully, feeding the sore arm into the jacket first, then settling the spiked bands over the fresh gauze, buckling the collar, and clipping the chains back into place.

Paper crackles in the pocket. They leave it there.

The empty lunch box is still in the backpack, Mara’s cloth folded around it. Bloof checks for the phone and keys, then closes the bag. Nothing school-issued.

On the stairwell landing, they wait for a group of students to pass before opening the message.

Nyra.

> Briefing moved up. Intake at 4:45, Bay C. Files are in the usual place.
>
> Eat something before you come in. Take the time you need.

Bloof reads it twice. Something at the corner of their mouth moves. They have eaten. They check the clock anyway.

The association assessment is due on the first.

They put the phone away and take the stairs down.

---

The tram is slower going out than it was coming in. Light lies long and amber across the windows, and the car smells of wet wool and fried dough. Bloof stands by the doors with the bag on their left shoulder, hands in their sleeves. Every joint in the right arm has opinions.

At the transfer stop, they take a seat while they wait. The bench is cold through their trousers. Across the tracks, a vendor argues with a shutter that will not quite close. Bloof works one finger beneath the edge of the gauze. It itches. They leave it alone.

The next tram arrives with its windows full of reflected light. Bloof boards, finds a place beside the doors, and watches the streets pass.

At Weaver Street, they turn down a covered arcade, a long strip of lanterns, steam, and stalls frying dough. Warm air slips beneath their collar. Somewhere behind a counter, a tray hits the floor and earns a burst of laughter.

A figure waiting at a stall window steps aside as they pass. Bloof angles the bag clear of the counter and follows the crowd toward the crossing.

Their phone catches against the empty lunch box when they reach into the bag. They free it carefully, check the time, and put it away.

Eleven minutes to Aetherion. Enough.

At the tram line, the signal is red. Bloof waits behind a delivery cart while the last carriage rattles through, hitching the bag higher on the left shoulder.

The light changes.

Beyond the crossing, the street opens onto a broad plaza. Evening catches in the upper windows, bright enough to make Bloof lower their gaze. They tuck both hands deeper into their sleeves.

Three short notes sound nearby.

Close. Clear. Bright and evenly spaced.

Bloof’s shoulders jerk inward. Their breath catches, and their hands close inside their sleeves. One step lands short.

For a heartbeat, they wait for something.

Nothing comes.

A cart rattles over the paving behind them. Bloof draws a breath and moves aside to let it pass. Their fingers loosen slowly.

They keep walking, hands in their sleeves.

The walking is deliberate. The flinch was not.
