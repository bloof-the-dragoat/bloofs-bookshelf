# Chapter 3: Crossfire

> “Quiet isn’t the same as slow.”
> — Director Nyra Veyl

---

Aetherion Technologies is easy to miss because it is everywhere.

It is the thin branching mark in the corner of half the mana-fiber banners along the tram line, a stroke that could be a circuit or could be a wing. It is stamped on the regulators inside the leyline lamps on the avenues of Concordia, and on the crystal housings in clinics that cannot afford to lose power at night. It designs the rune circuits other companies certify, the storage crystals those circuits run on, and the fiber that carries current and signal between them.

It also pays for things. Scholarships, mostly. Internships, equipment stipends, tuition aid for engineering students who never had a family name to open doors for them.

Its motto is printed on every lanyard in the building: *Power needs a circuit.*

Most of the city knows it as the company that made magic dependable. That is true, as far as it goes.

Bloof’s card clears the Aetherion turnstile on the first chime.

No amber loop. No held breath. The light goes green, the bar slides back, and the guard behind the glass is already looking past them at the next badge. It is the only reader in the city that has never made them wait.

Bay C sits at the end of a long subfloor corridor, past the equipment cages and the smell of warm flux. The access panel wants a palm. Bloof lays theirs against it, and it answers with one clean tone.

Their hand stays there a half second longer than the panel needs.

Then the lock drops, and they go in.

Intake lockers line the first wall. Bloof stows the backpack on the bottom shelf, with the empty lunch box inside it and Mara’s cloth folded around the box. The student ID goes in after it. Paper crackles in the jacket pocket, and they leave it where it is. They shut the locker with their left hand. The right arm stays close to their side.

The briefing room off the bay has one tall window and too much table. Late light lies across it in a copper bar that stops a hand’s width short of the far corner. A deep chair sits in a recess there, where the sun doesn’t reach.

Director Nyra Veyl stands at the head of the table with a slate in one hand. The charcoal suit fits so cleanly it looks grown rather than sewn. Their feathers are pale gray, banded with cream. Their face is a soft disc of concentric rings, and the dark, steady eyes blink less often than they should.

“You’re early,” Nyra says.

“Nine minutes.”

“Early.” The head tilts. The eyes go to Bloof’s face, then down to the gauze at the wrist, and stay there a moment. “Did you eat?”

“Yes, Director.”

“Good.” A pause that costs nothing and is noticed anyway. “Sit.”

Bloof takes the chair two down from the middle, with a clear line to the door. They ease into it, bracing with their left hand, then set their own slate on the table. The right arm stays where it is, close to the ribs.

“Client arrives at 4:45,” Nyra says. “Mr. Halden Voss, independent commercial broker. He is carrying one case. The case is cuffed to him, and he will not discuss it.” They slide a line of text across the slate. “Delivery is to a private receiving site on the east side. Primary route is the river service road. Secondary is the freight corridor, and the gates on it are keyed to this contract.”

Bloof reads. The file is what the file always is: dates, a route, a fee. There are three paragraphs of liability language and no explanation of why a broker would want two escorts.

As Nyra speaks, their thumb passes over the inlaid panel at the table’s edge, once, the way a hand does while turning a page. Bloof’s eyes are on the route.

“You will be working with a partner on this one,” Nyra says. “Ms. Daelune reviewed the file this morning, so she is current.”

Bloof glances at the second chair, which is empty, and at the door, which is closed.

“Is she coming to this brief?”

Something in the room is wrong.

It isn’t a sound. The air itself has a wrong edge to it, the way a step feels wrong when the stair isn’t there. Bloof’s upper lids part, a hair and no more.

The room comes up in warmth. The table holds the last of the sun. Nyra is a steady banked ember by the window, and the vent over the door breathes out a thin, even heat. In the far corner the gradient simply stops. It isn’t cold, because cold has an edge that gives itself away. This is a cut-out, a stencil of nothing roughly the size and shape of someone sitting down.

“She’s already here,” Nyra says.

The corner moves the way water moves when you stop looking directly at it.

White comes first: coat tails draped over the chair’s arm, a wide hat brim, one leg crossed over the other. Beneath the coat, pearls rest against an open-collared black shirt. Violet hands settle on the armrests, dark claws edged by a glint of blue scales.

Pale, ridged horns rise above the brim. Beneath it, dark hair frames a purple face: right eye magenta, left cyan beneath a thin scar. Her grin shows sharp teeth.

She doesn’t stand. She lets the whole shape of her arrive at its own pace, and the chair looks like the one being allowed.

“Eventually,” she says.

Her voice is low and smooth, and it enjoys itself.

Bloof lets their upper lids close. “Ms. Daelune.”

“You found the chair.” She tilts her head, and the brim tilts with it. “A promising start.”

*I found a hole in the room. That is not the same as a chair.*

“Three seconds,” Seris says. “I’d allowed two. The file said 'attentive'. I assumed that meant faster.”

Bloof says nothing to that. The silence isn’t pointed, just there.

“Bloovrn Graymark. No—Bloof, was it?” Seris says, tasting the name.

“Yes.”

“It sounds like something you say after being hit.”

“It’s what I’m called.”

The tip of her tail ticks once against the chair leg, a small, dry sound. Her eyes go to the gauze. “And Aetherion sends its interns out pre-damaged now. I’d read the internship was competitive.”

“It is.”

“Hm.”

“The hand is handled,” Bloof says.

“How reassuring.”

Nyra clears their throat, so softly it could have been the air. “The client’s escorts will be Ms. Daelune and my operative.” They turn the slate. “For this assignment, Mr. Voss will know you as Ash.”

“Ash,” Seris says. “How dramatic.”

“It was assigned.”

The door chimes. Two soft tones, and it opens before anyone answers.

The beastfolk who steps through is small and very old: a tortoise in Aetherion medical gray, her shell a heavy dome beneath the coat, her face worn smooth as river stone. She carries a case no bigger than a lunch box and moves as though the corridor has been informed in advance.

“Director.”

“Doctor Brannock,” Nyra says. “Thank you for the speed.”

“You called it urgent.”

“I called it timely.”

Bloof looks from one to the other. “Director?”

“The right shoulder.” Nyra doesn’t raise their voice. “You have been guarding it since you came through the door. The bag on the left, the chair taken left-handed, the slate set down with the left. You looked at the file rather than reach across the table for it.”

“It’s a pull.”

“Then Doctor Brannock will find it quick.”

“I can work with—”

“You can work with it in front of a client who is already deciding whether to trust you. Or you can sit still for five minutes.” Nyra’s gaze rests on them. It is not unkind. “I’d like you whole before anyone’s life depends on your reach.”

There is a catalogue of things Bloof could say. None of them is useful, and most of them are about money.

*Add it to the tab.*

“Thank you,” they say.

“There is no invoice with your name on it.” Nyra turns a page they don’t need to turn. “Sit.”

“I usually arrange my own repairs,” Seris remarks from the corner. Nobody answers.

Brannock sets her case on the table and opens it without hurry. “Jacket, please. Leave the sleeves where they are. I only need to see the hands.” She waits a beat. “And I’ll tell you what I’m doing before I do it. That’s not negotiable, on my end.”

Bloof unbuckles the collar and sets it on the table, spikes up. The jacket comes off by stages, the right sleeve last, and the old hot wire runs from neck to blade as the arm comes free. In the fitted black shirt, cuffs to the wrist, nothing shows but the gauze-wrapped hands. Bloof unfastens the spiked bands one at a time and lays them beside the collar.

“My hand will be between your shoulder blades,” Brannock says. “Flat against your shirt. All right?”

The tail has gone flat against the chair rail. Bloof notices and puts it back.

“All right.”

The palm is dry and cool and weighs almost nothing. For a moment there is only that, and the small room around it. Then light comes up under her fingers, not bright, the color of tea held to a window. It sinks in along the line of the shoulder, and something that has been pulled tight since the roof before dawn lets go.

A breath leaves Bloof on its own.

“There’s the knot,” Brannock murmurs. “You’ve been pulling against it all day. Don’t do that. It isn’t a character-building shoulder.”

She works her way down. The shoulder first, then the left forearm where the lance found it. She calls each step before she takes it. Bloof holds still and lets her.

“Ribs,” Brannock says. “Low, under the strap line. Something blunt, earlier today.”

“Yes.”

The ache there loosens like a held breath let go.

“Hand, now. The right.”

The gauze comes away in one careful roll. The knuckles are an ugly landscape of split skin and half-healed seams, and Pip’s salve still lies green and faintly cold across them.

Brannock sniffs once. “Willow bark. Nettle. Somebody dressed this properly.” She looks up. “Friend?”

“A friend.”

Seris tilts her head. She says nothing at all, which is somehow worse.

The light moves across the knuckles. The split edges draw together, and fresh skin comes up pink and tight beneath it. The left hand is the same, fainter. The scabs on both go dull and flake.

“Closed skin is not toughened skin,” Brannock says, wiping her fingers on a cloth. “Nothing harder than a pillow until tomorrow.”

“Understood.”

“No, you don’t. That’s the voice of someone who’s going to hit something.” She snaps the case shut. “I mend the damage. The habit is yours.” She considers Bloof for a moment. “You knit quickly. Don’t take that as permission. And sleep, if the Director allows it.”

“Noted,” Nyra says.

The door closes behind her.

Bloof rolls the right shoulder, slowly, all the way around. There is no catch. They flex both hands once, and the knuckles pull a little but hold. They settle the jacket over their shoulders, refasten the wristbands, and buckle the collar.

*Now I have nothing to blame the day on.*

“You look relieved,” Seris says.

“I look the same.”

“No.” She lifts a finger from the armrest, a small, languid motion. “You look like someone who no longer has an excuse.”

---

Halden Voss arrives at 4:45 exactly, which Nyra acknowledges by not looking at the clock.

He is a pine marten, rangy and restless, with a cream throat patch and gold-rimmed spectacles that he pushes up his nose twice before he is through the door. His overcoat is expensive and slept in. The case is a matte gray hard shell with guarded corners, and a thin chain runs from its handle to a steel cuff at his left wrist. A status window is set into the lid. It shows a pale green level bar and a slow pulse.

Through the open doorway, his eyes follow a medical-gray coat receding down the corridor before returning to the room.

“Medical,” he says.

“Pre-deployment,” Nyra says.

“On my contract time, or yours?”

“Yours has not started.”

He grunts. Behind him the door shuts, and he turns and tries the handle. Then he tries it again. Then he pushes it, as if a door could lie about being locked, and only then walks to the table. He takes one chair, rejects it because the window throws his reflection into the rune-board, and moves to another.

“That is my protection?”

“Ash is one of your two escorts,” Nyra says.

Voss’s gaze catches on the spiked collar. “Is that supposed to inspire confidence?”

“The spikes are cosmetic,” Bloof says.

“Mostly,” Seris adds, from the corner.

Voss’s gaze goes to the corner, where the white coat has become unmistakable. His shoulders rise a fraction and settle. “Ms. Daelune.” He says it carefully, like a word with an edge on it. “I know the name. I’m told it is expensive. I am not told what it buys.”

“Do you know who I am, Mr. Voss?”

“I know your name.”

“Then you’re ahead of most.” She smiles, and it is a good smile with a great many teeth in it. “The rest arrive too late to be useful.”

“Ms. Daelune,” Nyra says.

“*Director*.”

Voss swallows and pushes his spectacles up again. “I would like it noted that I asked.”

“Noted,” Nyra says.

He sits. He stands. He sits again. The case rides in his lap with his right arm clamped over the lid, and the chain from his left wrist catches on a coat button with a small metallic tick. He jerks his arm, the case jerks with it, and he makes a noise of pure irritation at his own wrist.

“May I?” Bloof says.

Voss stares at them.

“The chain’s caught on your button. I’ll lift it clear. I won’t touch the case.”

“You will not touch the case.”

“It stays with you until the handover. I don’t carry it, hold it, or move it unless you tell me to.”

A long moment. Voss holds out the arm, and the case with it, his fingers white on the handle. Bloof slips the chain from the button with two fingers and lets it hang straight, then steps back. The chain never brushes the lid.

“Which hand is the cuff?”

“Left.”

“How much slack?”

“Arm’s length and a bit.”

“Any release?”

“Thumbprint. Mine.” His mouth tightens. “Or a cut through the lock plate, which I would rather you not.”

“Then I’ll ask first. If we need the cut, you say yes before I do it.” Bloof straightens. “Left wrist. One turn of slack in the car, no more. If I say down, you go down, and you don’t ask why until after.”

“I am not a child.”

“No. You’re a client. That’s more important.”

From the corner comes a small, dry sound that might be a breath. When Bloof glances over, Seris’s face has not changed at all.

Voss does not answer. But the hand on the handle eases by a finger’s width, and his eyes stop darting for the length of a breath.

“Questions,” Nyra says.

“Who holds the secondary route?”

“Aetherion. The freight corridor’s gates are keyed to this contract and no other.”

“And the receiving party? How are they verified?”

“A paired seal token. Half is set in the lid of your case.” Nyra nods at it. “The receiving site holds the other. No matching half, no handover.”

Voss’s brows lift a very little. It’s the first thing in this room he hasn’t argued with.

“And if they come?” he says, glancing at the white coat. “Interceptors.”

“Then they will be unlucky,” Seris says.

“Interceptors are to be left conscious,” Nyra says. “Recovery and report. This is not a retaliation contract.”

“How tedious.”

“Conscious interceptors can be questioned,” Bloof says.

Seris looks at them for a moment. “You’re very reassuring.”

“Was that a question?”

“An observation.”

“Then I’ll leave it with you.”

The tip of her tail ticks once. Voss, who has been looking back and forth between them like a witness at a duel, finally lets out his breath.

“I want to know what I am paying for,” he says. “Two operatives and a route. And no one will ask what is in my case.”

“No one has,” Nyra says.

“I will,” Bloof says. “In one respect.”

Voss goes rigid.

“Not what. How.” Bloof nods at the lid. “Orientation, shock limit, temperature. I don’t know whether it rides level or takes a hard stop. I can’t protect what I don’t understand. I’ll protect you first either way.”

“That is a very smooth sentence.”

“I can say it less smoothly.”

“Ash is the reason the fee is what it is,” Nyra says, without looking up. “I put my name to this contract on that basis.”

Voss’s gaze goes from Nyra to Bloof to the corner and back. He chews the inside of his cheek. He adjusts his spectacles.

“It rides level,” he says at last, grudgingly. “The cradle’s rated for limited shock. The lid is the only display.”

Bloof looks at the lid. Damped cradle. Sealed housing. Orientation tags along the hinge, and a fabricator’s seal set into the metal beside them. The level bar is the only thing on the window that isn’t a status light.

“Calibrated tooling,” they say. “Not a component. A component doesn’t need a level bar. And that’s a fabricator’s seal, so it’s something that makes things.”

Voss does not move.

“A master,” he says, finally. His voice has dropped. “Rune imprinting. One regulator pattern. It’s worth more than this floor.”

“Surge regulation,” Bloof says. “For wards.”

His head snaps up. “Not a word of that leaves this room.”

“It won’t.”

“Which does what?” Seris asks.

“The part that keeps a ward from cooking its own power supply,” Bloof says. “Ask anyone who builds them, and it’s the only part that matters.”

Seris looks at them for a long moment, and for once the look has no edge in it. It’s the look of someone resetting a number.

“Then it rides level,” Bloof says to Voss, “and I’ll keep it that way.”

Voss lets go of the breath he’s been holding for several minutes. “Thank you,” he says, as if the words are expensive.

Nyra stands. “The vehicle is in the bay. The route is loaded. Masks.” Their gaze moves between the two operatives. “Both of you, before you leave the bay.”

No lecture. Bloof reaches into the jacket and draws out theirs: matte gray, molded to the muzzle, covering everything from nose to brow. A dark visor spans both pairs of eyes, leaving the upper lids free to open. A hairline of pale rune-light traces its edge. They settle it over the face and seat the catch behind the horns.

Their voice, when they check it, comes out half a register lower and flatter, all its edges sanded off.

Seris has hers from somewhere inside the coat, pearl-white and smooth. It covers the lower half of her face and leaves her mismatched eyes unobstructed beneath the brim. She fits it with one hand, without looking.

“Mine matches the coat,” she says, in a voice gone smoother still. Her gaze drops to Bloof’s. She watches them make the final adjustment, a small tug at the strap behind the ear. “There. Much more mysterious.”

“Ready?”

“Try to contain your enthusiasm.”

Nyra stops Bloof at the door.

“Bloof.”

“Director.”

“Come back in the condition you left in.”

“Yes, Director.”

The corridor opens into the bay. The transport waits on its pad: a squat armored courier, black and low, with a smooth nose and no windscreen, because the front section has no driver to need one. The route system hums behind its plating. A faint ward-light runs in a seam along the cabin, and a status panel by the rear door shows a row of small green marks.

Voss stops at the step. He looks at the cabin, then at the chain on his wrist, then at the case.

“I’m behind you,” Bloof says.

He nods once and climbs in.

Seris folds into the seat opposite in one movement, coat tails settling around her like a closing wing. Bloof checks the lid’s level bar a final time and the status panel after it. All green.

They step up. The door seals behind them with a soft, expensive sound.

---

Voss immediately tests it.

“Emergency release is under the red cover,” Bloof says.

“I can read.”

“Good. Leave it covered.”

He takes his hand away and settles the case across his thighs. The cuff chain makes a loose curve between his wrist and the handle.

Bloof takes the seat on his left, cuff side, and draws their own harness down across their shoulders. Across the cabin, Seris has already fastened hers. The broad brim of her hat almost touches the headrest. Beneath it, magenta and cyan regard the interior with equal disapproval.

Voss examines the straps beside his seat.

“Does that go over the case?”

“Against you first. Left hand through.”

“It will catch the chain.”

“Keep the handle in your right.”

He feeds his cuffed hand beneath the shoulder strap, working slowly enough to make a point. Bloof waits until the case is back in his lap before checking the harness catch.

A firm pull. Then the shoulder strap, clear of the cuff.

“Too tight?”

“It is certainly present.”

“It should be.”

A padded retaining band sits beside Voss’s knee. Bloof points to it.

“Across the case. You fasten it.”

His eyes narrow.

“It keeps the weight off your wrist if we stop hard.”

That earns a pause rather than an argument. Voss draws the band across the lid and locks it into the seat bracket himself.

“Release?”

“Blue tab. Yours to pull.”

He tests it, then fastens it again.

Above the partition, three indicators turn green. Cabin protection. Restraints. Rollover frame.

Through a narrow strip of reinforced glass above them, the bay doors begin to part. The front drive compartment sits lower than the cabin, leaving a clear view over its smooth armored nose.

The courier rolls into evening.

For a while there is only the hum of the drive and the faint tick of Voss’s cuff against the handle. Loading bays give way to shopfronts, then a tram stop crowded with beastfolk heading home. A vendor lifts a steaming lid. A child presses both palms against a bakery window.

Voss leans forward.

“We should have turned.”

“Freight congestion.” Bloof watches the amber section appear on the route display. “We rejoin at the service junction.”

“That was not in the itinerary.”

“Traffic wasn’t consulted.”

Seris’s gaze shifts to them.

Bloof keeps watching the display.

Voss studies it for himself, finds the same amber line, and sits back with the air of someone who has prevented a serious error.

Across from them, Seris smooths one white cuff.

“Does the silence cost extra?”

“No.”

“A wasted opportunity.”

Bloof looks toward her.

She waits.

They return their attention to the window.

“I would appreciate knowing whether either of you considers this assignment serious,” Voss says.

“I’m wearing a seat belt.” Seris glances down at the harness, then back at him, as though she has supplied all the evidence he could reasonably need.

“That is not—”

“We have your destination,” Bloof says. “Your receiving token, two routes, and transport monitoring. If anything changes, we’ll tell you.”

Voss’s mouth works once before closing.

The courier takes a long curve. The case shifts against its retaining band, and the level indicator drifts off-center.

“Level,” Bloof says.

They don’t reach for it. Voss huffs, shifts his forearm, and the bar settles back to center.

The last shopfront slips behind them. Concrete supports rise beside the road, carrying the elevated freight line overhead. To the left, a low divider borders a recessed maintenance walkway. To the right, a shuttered depot stands above a loading platform.

A maintenance carrier waits farther along, angled toward the curb. Its rear cargo doors stand open. Amber hazard lamps blink against the pillars. No crew stands near them.

Bloof notes it and does not like it.

The courier slows for the service bend.

Voss looks at his case.

“If that cradle has to be recalibrated—”

"It's insured," Seris says.

"I'm not."

"Then leave those fastened," Bloof says.

Pressure builds behind their eyes, a thumb pressed hard against bone.

Their gaze lifts.

Beside the next support, a gray utility housing is drinking power far too fast.

Bloof's upper lids open.

The road still holds the day's warmth. The pillar is cooling. But inside the housing a seam of mana swells bright through a vent, winding itself tighter and tighter, and the whole pattern is turning toward the underside of the road.

Toward the courier's nose.

"_Down!_"

Voss's head snaps around.

Bloof is already moving. Their right forearm folds his cuffed arm against the case, wrist and handle pinned together. Their other hand cups the back of his skull. They never touch the case. They don't need to.

Across the cabin, Seris sets a boot against the seat pedestal.

The housing's cover jumps.

White floods the viewing strip, and the front of the courier is simply gone.

The blow comes up through the chassis like a hammer through a drum. The partition buckles. The cabin ward catches the force and flares, its pale lattice blooming through the upholstery. Beside Seris a seam tears open. She wrenches her shoulder clear as darkness thickens along the gap, narrow and dense and instant. Two fragments strike it. One punches halfway through before the dark folds shut around it.

Then the world tips.

Voss makes a strangled sound. Both harnesses bite, locking Bloof across him as the cabin rolls. Window becomes floor. Floor becomes wall. The roof meets the road with a crunch, and the lattice flares beneath Bloof's boots, swallowing the worst of it. Metal shrieks. The cabin slides, turns a last fraction, and stops.

The ward gutters out.

Something heavy skitters away across the asphalt.

Then there is only glass, ticking as it settles.

Voss hangs in his harness, the retaining band still pinning the case to his chest. His spectacles sit crooked, one lens crazed white.

Bloof eases their hand from behind his head. "Anything hurt?"

"The vehicle—"

"Anything on you."

He looks down at himself, slowly, as if the answer might be hiding.

"No."

"Good. Keep hold of the handle."

Across the cabin, Seris is braced against the overturned seat frame, her hat knocked askew. She straightens it with two fingers. A metal fragment drops from the fraying shadow beside her shoulder and rings against the roof.

Her gaze travels from Bloof's hand to Voss's cuffed arm, braced and not wrenched, and then up to the open upper eyes.

The cyan eye narrows beneath its scar.

She says nothing.

Outside, a door slams. Boots hit the road.

"Company," she says.

Bloof sets a foot against the roof and unlatches their harness, dropping into a crouch beside Voss. "Blue tab first. Keep the case against you."

He pulls it. The retaining band falls away, and he locks both arms around the case.

"Now your harness. I have your shoulders."

"I can manage."

"Then we agree."

Bloof takes his weight as the catch releases and lowers him to his knees, his fingers still white on the handle.

At the rear door, Seris hauls the emergency lever. The lower lock clicks free.

The upper corner doesn't move. It is buried in a twisted frame.

"Very expensive," she observes.

"Lock's clear. The frame's holding it."

"I see that."

Her dagger slides into the gap. She isn't cutting, she's levering, and the metal groans as she twists it, opening just enough room for her fingers. She withdraws the blade and grips the edge.

Bloof braces the opposite side. "Now."

Her heel slams into the buckled corner.

The frame gives with a scream. The door swings out and drops against the road, hanging from its lower hinge. Cool air spills in, smelling of scorched metal.

"Stay where you are," a voice calls.

Seris tilts her head toward the opening. "How fortunate. We were waiting for permission."

"Case comes out first," a second voice says.

"No," Bloof says.

They duck beneath the low frame.

---

The first mercenary stands three paces from the door, shock baton raised, a small shield strapped to his left forearm. He is watching the wreck.

Not the doorway.

Bloof comes out low and fast.

Their left hand clamps his baton wrist. Their right forearm turns the shield across his chest, and their foot takes the space behind his heel. He sits down hard. The baton leaves his hand and goes skidding beneath the wreck. Bloof catches the shield's rim before he can drag it back.

_No mist. No lance waiting for me to make the first mistake._

A second attacker swings around the courier's corner, club high. Bloof hauls the seated man's shield up into the blow. Wood cracks on metal. Bloof lets go of the rim, catches the stalled club by its head, and steps inside the attacker's reach. An elbow into his padded middle. A heel behind his ankle.

He falls across his partner's legs. The club follows the baton under the wreck.

"Hands where I can see them."

The first mercenary stares at his empty hand.

Both comply.

Beyond them, the maintenance carrier sits eight or nine paces off, its open rear doors facing the wreck like a mouth. A bull braces a casting rod against the cargo frame. Other shapes shift between the freight supports and along the depot platform on the right.

On the left, the concrete divider runs toward the carrier, its access gap close enough to reach under cover of the courier.

Bloof points. "Behind that."

Seris steers Voss through the doorway with a hand at his elbow. He clutches the case to his chest.

"Careful," he snaps.

"What an intriguing suggestion."

The bull's rod flares.

Bloof throws an orange bolt at the carrier's rear step. It bursts in a spray of sparks right under his firing position, and the bull jerks inward, rod and all.

Seris shoves Voss through the gap. Bloof watches until the broker's head drops below the concrete.

"Client down," she says.

Heat gathers in Bloof's legs. Thin orange seams brighten beneath their trouser hems. A planted foot, clear road, the carrier's rear corner for an endpoint.

**Cinderstep.**

Three hard footfalls, and the road tears past. Bloof arrives beside the open cargo door in a rush of heat, catches its edge, and slams it inward just as the bull thrusts his rod out again.

The barrel jams against the frame.

Bloof forces it down, strips the bull's grip, and sends the rod sliding beneath the carrier. He lunges after it. Bloof plants him against the bumper instead.

"Hands up."

Wind hits the step like a thrown wall.
